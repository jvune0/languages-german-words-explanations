# empfindlich — sensitive; touchy, delicate; keenly felt

A neutral, very common adjective with **two faces**. Of people and
materials it means "sensitive, easily hurt, fragile"; of a loss, a
fine or the cold it means "**severe, keenly felt**" — the kind you
really notice. Both grow from one root: *empfinden*, "to feel".

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Part of speech | adjective / adverb |
| Comparison | *empfindlicher* — *am empfindlichsten* |
| Government | *empfindlich **gegen** etw.* — sensitive to sth. |
| Also | *empfindlich **auf** etw. (acc.) reagieren* |
| Less common | *empfindlich **für** etw.* (criticism, disturbance) |
| As adverb | intensifier: *empfindlich stören, treffen, kalt* |
| Noun | *die Empfindlichkeit* — sensitivity; touchiness |
| Antonym | *unempfindlich* — insensitive, resistant, hardy |

## Main meanings

### 1. Sensitive, quick to react (body, skin, instruments)

> *Ich habe eine sehr empfindliche Haut.*
> I have very sensitive skin.

> *Mein Magen ist empfindlich gegen scharfes Essen.*
> My stomach is sensitive to spicy food.

> *Die Zähne reagieren empfindlich auf Kälte.*
> The teeth react painfully to cold.

### 2. Touchy, thin-skinned (of character)

The closest English word is *touchy* or *thin-skinned*; the tone is
usually mildly critical, unlike admiring English *sensitive*.

> *Sei doch nicht so empfindlich!*
> Don't be so touchy!

> *Er reagiert empfindlich auf jede Kritik.*
> He takes any criticism badly.

> *Sie hat mich an einer empfindlichen Stelle getroffen.*
> She hit me in a sore spot.

### 3. Delicate, needing careful handling (of things)

> *Vorsicht, das Gerät ist sehr empfindlich.*
> Careful, the device is very delicate.

> *Empfindliche Stoffe bei 30 Grad waschen.*
> Wash delicate fabrics at 30 degrees.

> *ein empfindlicher Messfühler* — a sensitive sensor

### 4. Severe, considerable, keenly felt (of damage, penalties)

Here the reading flips to **passive**: not "the thing that feels" but
"the thing that **is felt**". Standard in the press and in legal
language, where English says *heavy*, *stiff*, *severe*.

> *Das Gericht verhängte eine empfindliche Geldstrafe.*
> The court imposed a hefty fine.

> *Die Firma erlitt empfindliche Verluste.*
> The company suffered severe losses.

> *Der Ausfall stört den Betrieb empfindlich.*
> The outage disrupts operations badly.

> *Es war empfindlich kalt.*
> It was bitingly cold.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**emp-** (= *ent-*) + **find-** + **-lich**

- The root verb is **empfinden** "to feel, sense", Old High German
  *intfindan* "to come upon, discover by touch, perceive".
- Its core is **finden** = English **find**. Feeling is imagined as
  *finding*: a sensation is something you come upon inside yourself.
- The prefix **ent-** regularly becomes **emp-** before *f*: the
  nasal assimilates to the labial (*n* → *m*) and an epenthetic
  *p* slips in between. Hence German's famous *emp-* trio:
  *empfangen* (← ent+fangen, cf. *fang*), *empfinden*, and
  *empfehlen* — the last one **not** from modern *fehlen* "to be
  missing" (a loan from Old French, cf. English *fail*) but from
  ancient Germanic *\*felhan* "to entrust, conceal", still alive
  in *befehlen*.
- The **ent-** here is not "un-, de-" but the old *and-* "towards,
  facing" — the same element as in *Antwort*, English *answer*
  ("a word given back"), and Greek *anti-*. So *empfinden* is
  "to find towards oneself" → to perceive.
- **-lich** (= English **-ly**, older "-like") supports both an
  active reading, "apt to feel", and a passive one, "such as is
  felt" — which is exactly why the word has meanings 1–3 and 4.

English keeps the same double life in *sensitive skin* versus *a
sensitive loss*, and above all in **feeling**: a *feeling cold* was
once good English for *empfindlich kalt*. German simply built the
word from native *find* rather than from Latin *sentire*.

## Watch out: empfindlich vs. empfindsam

| Word | Sense | Colouring |
|------|-------|-----------|
| **empfindlich** | easily hurt, touchy, fragile | ⚠️ often negative |
| **empfindsam** | tender-hearted, sentimental | 📚 literary |
| **feinfühlig** | sensitive **to others**, tactful | ✅ positive |
| **sensibel** | finely tuned, sensitive | ➖ neutral |

*Empfindsamkeit* is also the name of the 18th-century literary period
(English *Sentimentalism*). The pair is a classic trap: the
*empfindliche* person **suffers**, the *empfindsame* one **feels for
others**. English *sensitive* covers both, which is why calling
someone *empfindlich* as a compliment misfires.

## How not to confuse it

| Word | Core idea |
|------|-----------|
| **empfindlich** | reacts easily; is damaged easily |
| **sensibel** | finely tuned (person or instrument); gentler |
| **zart** | tender, delicate by nature (*zarte Haut*) |
| **heikel** | tricky, requiring care (*ein heikles Thema*) |
| **anfällig** | prone, susceptible (to illness, failure) |
| **verletzlich** | vulnerable, capable of being wounded |
| **spürbar / merklich** | noticeable (neutral, no pain implied) |

## Typical collocations

- *empfindliche Haut / Zähne / Augen / Magen* — sensitive skin…
- *eine empfindliche Stelle* — a sore spot, a weak point
- *empfindlich auf etw. reagieren* — to react badly to sth.
- *empfindlich gegen Kälte / Lärm / Staub sein*
- *eine empfindliche Strafe / Niederlage / Lücke* — a stiff
  penalty / a painful defeat / a serious gap
- *empfindliche Verluste / Einbußen* — heavy losses
- *empfindlich stören / treffen / schwächen* — to disrupt / hit /
  weaken badly
- *empfindlich kalt* — bitingly cold
- *empfindlich teuer* (colloquial) — painfully expensive

## How to remember

Look inside the word: **emp-find-lich** — "find-ly", built on
**finden** = **find**. A touchy person *finds* an insult where nobody
put one; an *empfindliche Strafe* is one you will certainly *find* —
in your emptied wallet.

Pair them to keep them apart: *empfindlich* is the one who **feels**
it, *empfindsam* the one who **feels for** you.
