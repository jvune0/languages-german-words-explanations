# -kommen (bekommen · ankommen · vorkommen · auskommen …) — “to come”

> *kommen* is the mirror of [bringen](bringen-Verben.md): there someone
> **carries** something, here something **comes of its own accord**. That
> governs both the grammar (almost the whole family takes *sein*) and a
> whole layer of phrases in which *bringen* and *kommen* form pairs: *in
> Gang bringen* “to get going” — *in Gang kommen* “to get going” by itself.
> The family’s nouns are special too: not *-kommen* but **-kunft**.

## Grammar of the base verb

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | **strong**: *kommen – kam – gekommen* |
| Auxiliary | **sein** — a verb of motion |
| Present | no umlaut: *ich komme, du kommst, er kommt* |
| Participle | *ge-komm-en*; inseparables drop *ge-*: *bekommen* |

⚠️ **The one important exception** is *bekommen* “to receive”: it takes an
accusative object, hence **haben**:

> *Ich **habe** einen Brief bekommen.* — I received a letter.
> *Ich **bin** zu spät gekommen.* — I arrived too late.

But in the sense “to agree with someone” it takes *sein* again:

> *Das Essen ist mir nicht gut bekommen.* — The food didn’t agree with me.

The base verb’s own senses:

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to come, to arrive | *Wann kommst du?* |
| to be from | *Sie kommt aus Polen.* |
| to come about | *Wie kommt das?* — How come? |
| to get round to | *Ich komme nicht dazu.* — I don’t get round to it. |
| to hit on an idea | *Wie kommst du darauf?* — What makes you think that? |
| to lose (one’s life) | *ums Leben kommen* — to be killed |
| to get to the bottom of | *Ich bin hinter sein Geheimnis gekommen.* |

## The key: *kommen* = *come* = *venire*

*kommen*, English **come** and Latin *venire* all go back to the same
Indo-European root \*gʷem-. That is why the German *-kunft* compounds map
neatly onto the Latin *-vent-* ones:

| German | Latin-English | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *die Ankunft* | *Advent* (*ad-venire*) | arrival |
| *die Zusammenkunft* | *convention* (*con-venire*) | gathering |
| *die Übereinkunft* | *convention*, agreement | accord |
| *die Zukunft* | *adventure* (*adventura*) | “what is to come” |

> **kommen = to arrive of itself. The prefix says where from, where to, and
> what it turned into.**

## The *-kunft* nouns

A distinct and rather beautiful subsystem: the family’s nouns use the old
stem *kumft*, and from it come some of the language’s key words.

| Word | Meaning | Literally |
|---|---|---|
| *die Ankunft* | arrival | “coming to” |
| *die Zukunft* | **future** | “what will come” |
| *die Herkunft* | origin, provenance | “where one came from” |
| *die Auskunft* | information, enquiry desk | “what has come out” |
| *die Unterkunft* | accommodation | “where one came in under” |
| *die Zusammenkunft* | meeting, gathering | “coming together” |
| *die Übereinkunft* | agreement | “coming to the same point” |
| *die Einkünfte* (pl.) | income | “what has come in” |

The triad worth memorising whole: ***Herkunft*** (where from) —
***Ankunft*** (arrival) — ***Zukunft*** (what lies ahead).

## The main verbs

### 1. *bekommen* — to receive, to get

⚠️ **The great false friend of German.** *bekommen* is not English
*become*. “To become” is *werden*.

> *Ich habe ein Paket bekommen.* — I got a parcel.
> *Sie bekommt ein Kind.* — She is expecting a baby.
> *Ich bekomme Angst.* — I’m getting frightened.
> *Was bekommen Sie?* — What can I get you? / What do I owe you?

*bekömmlich* — easy to digest, wholesome.

### 2. *ankommen* — to arrive; to depend; to go down well

| Construction | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *ankommen* (sein) | to arrive | *Der Zug kommt um acht an.* |
| *es kommt auf etw. an* | to depend on; to matter | *Es kommt auf dich an.* |
| *bei jdm. gut ankommen* | to go down well | *Die Idee kam gut an.* |
| *gegen jdn. nicht ankommen* | to be no match for | *Gegen ihn komme ich nicht an.* |

> *Es kommt darauf an.* — It depends. (one of the most frequent phrases in
> the language)

### 3. *vorkommen* — to occur; to seem

> *So etwas kommt selten vor.* — That sort of thing rarely happens.
> *Das kommt mir bekannt vor.* — That seems familiar to me.
> *Ich komme mir dumm vor.* — I feel foolish.

*das Vorkommen* — occurrence, deposit (of a mineral); *das Vorkommnis* —
an incident.

### 4. *auskommen* — to get by; to get along

> *Ich komme mit dem Geld gut aus.* — The money is enough for me.
> *Wir kommen gut miteinander aus.* — We get along well.
> *ohne etw. auskommen* — to manage without something.

*das Auskommen* — a livelihood.

### 5. *abkommen* — to stray; to abandon (a practice)

> *Wir sind vom Weg abgekommen.* — We strayed from the path.
> *Davon ist man inzwischen abgekommen.* — That practice has since been
> dropped.

⚠️ *das Abkommen* means **an agreement, a treaty** (*das Pariser
Abkommen*). The noun has travelled far from the verb.

### 6. *nachkommen* — to follow on; to comply with

> *Geht schon vor, ich komme nach.* — Go on ahead, I’ll follow.
> *Er kam seinen Pflichten nicht nach.* — He failed to meet his obligations.
> (📰⚖️ the official formula: *einer Bitte / einer Aufforderung nachkommen*)

*die Nachkommen* — descendants.

### 7. *zukommen* — to approach; to be due to someone

> *Was kommt da auf uns zu?* — What is coming our way?
> *Diese Ehre kommt ihm zu.* — That honour is rightfully his.
> *Wir lassen Ihnen die Unterlagen zukommen.* — We shall forward you the
> documents. (📰 a courteous business formula)

### 8. *entgegenkommen* — to come towards; to accommodate

> *Ein Auto kam uns entgegen.* — A car came towards us.
> *Der Vermieter ist uns entgegengekommen.* — The landlord met us halfway.

*entgegenkommend* — obliging; oncoming; *das Entgegenkommen* — a
concession, an act of goodwill.

### 9. Other useful members

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *umkommen* | to be killed, to perish | *Drei Menschen kamen um.* |
| *entkommen* (+ dat.) | to escape, to get away | *Er entkam der Polizei.* |
| *verkommen* | to go to ruin, to go to seed | *Das Haus verkommt.* |
| *durchkommen* | to get through; to pull through; to pass | *Er kam knapp durch.* |
| *davonkommen* | to get off, to survive | *mit dem Schrecken davonkommen* |
| *aufkommen* | to arise; *für etw.* — to foot the bill | *Zweifel kamen auf.* |
| *unterkommen* | to find a place to stay | *Er ist bei uns untergekommen.* |
| *zurechtkommen* | to cope, to manage | *Kommst du damit zurecht?* |
| *herumkommen* | to get around; to avoid | *Darum kommst du nicht herum.* |
| *hinzukommen* | to be added | *Hinzu kommt, dass …* |
| *zusammenkommen* | to meet, to gather | *Wir kommen monatlich zusammen.* |
| *klarkommen* | 💬 to cope, to get on | *Ich komme damit klar.* |

## *bringen* / *kommen* pairs: someone does it — it happens by itself

The card’s most practical table. Left column: the action. Right column: the
same thing happening of its own accord.

| Someone does it (*bringen*) | It happens (*kommen*) |
|---|---|
| *etw. in Gang bringen* | *etw. kommt in Gang* — it gets going |
| *etw. zum Ausdruck bringen* | *etw. kommt zum Ausdruck* — it finds expression |
| *etw. zur Sprache bringen* | *etw. kommt zur Sprache* — it comes up |
| *etw. in Ordnung bringen* | *etw. kommt in Ordnung* — it sorts itself out |
| *etw. zum Vorschein bringen* | *etw. kommt zum Vorschein* — it comes to light |
| *jdn. ums Leben bringen* | *jd. kommt ums Leben* — someone is killed |
| *ein Kind zur Welt bringen* | *ein Kind kommt zur Welt* — a child is born |

⚠️ The same logic links *jdn. **um** etw. **bringen*** (to deprive someone)
and ***um** etw. **kommen*** (to lose it oneself): 📎
[jmdn. um etw. bringen](jmdn_um_etw_bringen.md).

## Phrases with *kommen*

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Es kommt darauf an.* | It depends. |
| *Das kommt nicht in Frage!* | That’s out of the question! |
| *zu Wort kommen* | to get a word in, to have one’s say |
| *zu dem Schluss kommen* | to come to the conclusion |
| *zur Ruhe kommen* | to settle down, to find peace |
| *zu kurz kommen* | to lose out, to get a raw deal |
| *hinter etw. kommen* | to get to the bottom of something |
| *auf etw. kommen* | to hit on something |
| *Wie kommst du darauf?* | What gives you that idea? |
| *Komm schon!* | 💬 Come on! |
| *Das kommt davon!* | 💬 That’s what you get! |

## How not to confuse them

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| *bekommen* / *werden* | to receive / to become (⚠️ not English *become*) |
| *bekommen* / *erhalten* | to get (neutral) / to receive (📰 formal) |
| *ankommen* / *erreichen* | to arrive somewhere / to reach something |
| *vorkommen* / *passieren* | to occur (regularly) / to happen (once) |
| *auskommen mit* / *zurechtkommen mit* | to get along; to get by / to cope |
| *umkommen* / *sterben* | to be killed (in a disaster) / to die |
| *das Abkommen* / *die Abkunft* | agreement / descent, origin (📜) |
| *kommen um* / *bringen um* | to lose / to deprive |

## Typical contexts

- **Speech:** *Wann kommst du?*, *Es kommt darauf an.*, *Komm gut nach
  Hause!*
- **Travel:** *die Ankunft*, *pünktlich ankommen*, *den Anschluss
  verpassen*.
- **Forms and documents:** *die Herkunft*, *die Unterkunft*, *Auskunft
  erteilen*.
- **Office:** *einer Bitte nachkommen*, *Wir lassen Ihnen … zukommen*, *für
  die Kosten aufkommen*.
- **News:** *ums Leben kommen*, *ein Abkommen unterzeichnen*.

## How to remember

*kommen* is English **come** and Latin *venire*. The whole nest is about
**what arrives by itself**, as against *bringen*, where someone carries it.
The prefix specifies:

- **be-** to oneself → **receive** (only that — never “become”);
- **an-** to the destination → **arrive**; impersonally, **to depend**;
- **vor-** before one’s eyes → **occur** and **seem**;
- **aus-** out of a fix → **get by**, **get along**;
- **ab-** off to the side → **stray**; and the noun means **agreement**;
- **nach-** after → **follow on** and **comply with**;
- **zu-** towards you → **lie ahead**, **be one’s due**;
- **um-** over → **perish**;
- **ent-** away → **escape**.

One sentence with the core of the family: *Er **kam** pünktlich **an**,
**bekam** die Unterlagen, **kam** mit allen gut **aus** — und **kam**
schließlich zu dem Schluss, dass alles stimmt.* — “He arrived on time, got
the documents, got along with everyone — and finally came to the conclusion
that it was all correct.”
