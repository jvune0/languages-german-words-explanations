# verschieben — to move, to shift; to postpone, to reschedule

A strong verb with an inseparable prefix; neutral and very frequent.
One gesture with two faces: **to shift in space** (a cupboard, a
comma, a file) and **to shift in time** (a meeting, a trip, a
decision). In everyday use the second is the main one — "to move it
to another day".

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Type | strong; prefix **inseparable**, unstressed |
| Principal parts | *verschiebt · verschob · hat verschoben* |
| Participle II | *verschoben* — **no *ge-*** |
| *zu*-infinitive | *zu verschieben* (not *verzuschieben*) |
| Government | *etw.* (acc.) *verschieben* |
| New date | *etw. **auf** + acc.* — *auf Montag verschieben* |
| By how much | *etw. **um** + acc.* — *um zwei Wochen verschieben* |
| Reflexive | *sich verschieben* — to shift, to slip |
| Auxiliary | *haben* (also in the reflexive) |

⚠️ **Two prepositions, two different jobs:**
*auf* = **to which point** (*auf Freitag* — to Friday);
*um* = **by how much** (*um drei Tage* — by three days).

## Main meanings

### 1. To postpone, to reschedule (the core sense)

> *Wir müssen den Termin auf nächste Woche verschieben.*
> We'll have to move the appointment to next week.

> *Die Sitzung wurde um zwei Stunden verschoben.*
> The meeting was pushed back by two hours.

> *Verschiebe nicht auf morgen, was du heute kannst besorgen.*
> Don't put off till tomorrow what you can do today.
> (the German proverb, matching the English one word for word)

### 2. To move, to shift in space

> *Kannst du den Schrank ein Stück nach links verschieben?*
> Can you shift the cupboard a bit to the left?

> *Die Grenze wurde nach Westen verschoben.*
> The border was moved westwards.

> *Verschieben Sie die Datei in den anderen Ordner.*
> Move the file to the other folder. (💻 the standard UI term)

### 3. *sich verschieben* — to shift, to slip

Of deadlines, proportions, tectonic plates, emphases.

> *Der Starttermin hat sich noch einmal verschoben.*
> The start date has slipped again.

> *Die Machtverhältnisse haben sich verschoben.*
> The balance of power has shifted.

> *Ein Wirbel hat sich verschoben.*
> A vertebra has slipped. (🩺)

### 4. To traffic, to fence stolen goods (💬 colloquial, criminal)

A branch of its own: moving goods past the law.

> *Er verschob gestohlene Autos ins Ausland.*
> He trafficked stolen cars abroad.

Hence *der Schieber* "racketeer, spiv" and *die Schiebung* "a fix, a
put-up job".

## Word breakdown and etymology

**ver-** + **schieben**

- **schieben** — "to push, to shove"; the English cognate is
  **to shove** (Germanic *\*skeuban*). *Shuffle* and *scoop* belong to
  the same family.
- **ver-** here carries the sense **"away, elsewhere"** — as in
  *verlegen* (to relocate), *versetzen* (to transfer), *verrücken*
  (to displace). The same prefix supplies the "off-course" tinge in
  *sich verlaufen* "to get lost".

Literally: **"to shove aside"** — and from there the metaphor: to
shove along the axis of time as well as across the floor.

**English parallel.** *Shove* kept only the physical sense, but
English says *put off* and *postpone* (Latin *post* "after" +
*ponere* "to place") — "to lay it down later". German **pushes**,
English **places**. Different gesture, identical logic: the meeting is
an object slid around on the table of the calendar.

⚠️ The participle **verschoben** means both "postponed" and
"crooked, out of alignment": *ein verschobenes Bild* is a picture
hanging askew.

## Watch out: verschieben vs. aufschieben

Both translate as "put off", but differently:

| Verb | Core idea | Colouring |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **verschieben** | to move to a **specific** new date | neutral |
| **aufschieben** | to defer indefinitely, "for later" | ⚠️ procrastination |

> *Wir verschieben das Treffen auf Dienstag.* — a new day is fixed.
> *Er schiebt die Steuererklärung ständig auf.* — he keeps dodging it.

Hence the jokey coinage **die Aufschieberitis** — "procrastinitis".

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **verschieben** | to shift / to reschedule | *den Termin verschieben* |
| **verlegen** | to relocate (an event, also a place) | *ins Foyer verlegen* |
| **versetzen** | to reposition; to transfer (staff) | *nach Berlin versetzen* |
| **verrücken** | to shift physically out of place | *den Tisch verrücken* |
| **umziehen** | to move house | *nach Köln umziehen* |
| **bewegen** | to move (the general verb) | *etw. bewegen* |
| **absagen** | to cancel (outright!) | *den Termin absagen* |
| **vertagen** | ⚖️ to adjourn (formal) | *die Sitzung vertagen* |

**The main traps:**

- ⚠️ *verschieben* ≠ *absagen*. Postponing is not cancelling:
  *verschoben ist nicht aufgehoben* — "postponed is not cancelled",
  a stock phrase.
- ⚠️ For dates, *verlegen* and *verschieben* overlap, but *verlegen*
  leans on **place** (*das Konzert wurde ins Stadion verlegt*) and
  *verschieben* on **time**.
- ⚠️ Don't confuse the verb *verlegen* with the adjective *verlegen*
  "embarrassed" — unrelated homonyms.

## Typical collocations

- *einen Termin / ein Treffen / eine Reise verschieben*
- *etw. auf morgen / auf nächste Woche / auf unbestimmte Zeit
  verschieben* — to postpone until tomorrow / indefinitely
- *etw. um eine Woche nach hinten verschieben* — to push sth. back
  by a week
- *sich nach hinten verschieben* — to slip to a later date
- *die Prioritäten verschieben sich* — priorities are shifting
- *eine Datei / ein Fenster verschieben* — 💻 to move a file, a window
- *verschoben ist nicht aufgehoben* — "postponed isn't cancelled"
- *etw. auf die lange Bank schieben* — to put sth. on the back
  burner (an idiom on the same root)

## How to remember

The root **schieben** is English **shove**. The whole word is
"**to shove aside**": the cupboard sideways, the meeting forwards
across the calendar. German treats time as a table you can slide
things along.

A useful idiom from the same root: *etwas **auf die lange Bank
schieben*** — literally "to shove it onto the long bench", i.e. to
shelve it. In a medieval courtroom the long bench was a chest for
documents; whatever got shoved in there stayed for years.

And keep the pair straight: *verschieben* means a **new date is set**;
*aufschieben* means you are simply **dodging**. The first is
administration, the second is a diagnosis.
