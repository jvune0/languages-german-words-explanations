# im Griff vs. im Begriff — “under control” vs. “about to”

> Two set phrases separated by one prefix, *be-*, and by everything
> else. *etw. im Griff haben* = “have something under control, be on
> top of it”; *im Begriff sein, etw. zu tun* = “be on the point of
> doing something”. The first is everyday and neutral; the second is
> bookish, at home in the press and in narrative prose.

## The difference at a glance

| | *etw. im Griff haben* | *im Begriff sein, … zu …* |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | be on top of, master, control | be about to, be on the verge |
| Carrier verb | *haben* (also *bekommen/kriegen*) | *sein* (lofty: *stehen*) |
| Complement | a noun in the accusative | a *zu*-infinitive clause |
| It is about | **skill and power** over a thing | a **moment in time** |
| English core | “have a grip on” | “be on the point of” |
| Register | 💬 colloquial-to-neutral | 📰 written, formal-ish |
| Typical subject | a person, a firm, a fear | a person, a country, a process |

The mental fork: **Griff is the hand, Begriff is the head.** Your hand
holds the thing; your head grasps the idea.

## Grammar

### *etw.* (acc.) *im Griff haben*

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Structure | *jmd. hat etw.* (acc.) *im Griff* |
| Fixed parts | *im Griff* never varies (*im* = *in dem*) |
| Main verb | *haben*; “keep it that way” = *im Griff behalten* |
| Ingressive | *etw. in den Griff bekommen / kriegen* (acc.! motion) |
| Stative passive | *etw. ist im Griff* — “it has been brought under control” |
| Source noun | *der Griff* ← *greifen* (greift, griff, gegriffen) |

⚠️ Note the case split: the **state** is *im Griff* (dative, “where”),
the **transition into it** is *in den Griff* (accusative, “whither”) —
the same logic as *ins Bett gehen* vs. *im Bett liegen*.

> *Ich habe die Lage im Griff.* — I have the situation under control.
> *Ich bekomme die Lage langsam in den Griff.*
> I’m slowly getting the situation under control.

### *im Begriff sein, etw. zu tun*

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Structure | *jmd. ist im Begriff,* + *zu*-infinitive clause |
| Fixed parts | comma + *zu*; the phrase cannot stand without one |
| Variant | *im Begriffe stehen* — 📜 elevated, dated |
| Old inflection | *im Begriffe* with final *-e*, literary |
| Tenses | mostly present and preterite: *war im Begriff, zu …* |
| Everyday swap | *dabei sein, etw. zu tun* / plain *gerade* + present |

⚠️ The phrase takes **no direct object**: *✗ Ich bin im Begriff die
Arbeit* is impossible. You need a verb: *Ich bin im Begriff, die
Arbeit zu beenden.*

## 1. *etw. im Griff haben* — have a grip on, be on top of

The core image is the closed fist: what you hold cannot get away.
Hence mastery over a situation, a machine, an illness, your own nerves.

> *Der Trainer hat die Mannschaft fest im Griff.*
> The coach has the team firmly in hand.

> *Nach zwei Stunden hatte die Feuerwehr den Brand im Griff.*
> After two hours the fire brigade had the blaze under control.

> *Sie hat ihre Nerven nicht mehr im Griff.*
> She can’t control her nerves any more.

> *Die neue Software habe ich noch nicht ganz im Griff.*
> I haven’t quite got the hang of the new software yet.

### The “in its grip” shade (power over people)

Reinforced with *fest*, the phrase turns from competence to
domination — English *have somebody in its grip*:

> *Der Konzern hat den gesamten Markt fest im Griff.*
> The corporation has the entire market in its grip.

> *Die Angst hatte ihn im Griff.* — Fear had him in its grip.

### The dynamic variant: *in den Griff bekommen/kriegen*

This is the “not yet, but getting there” phase — a staple of news and
of medical talk:

> *Die Inflation ist schwer in den Griff zu bekommen.*
> Inflation is hard to get under control.

> *Mit den Tabletten kriegt man den Blutdruck gut in den Griff.*
> With the tablets you can get blood pressure nicely under control.

## 2. *im Begriff sein, etw. zu tun* — be on the point of doing

The core is a threshold: the action has been set in motion in your
mind and body, but has not happened yet.

> *Er war im Begriff, das Haus zu verlassen, als das Telefon klingelte.*
> He was just about to leave the house when the phone rang.

> *Die Firma ist im Begriff, hundert Stellen zu streichen.*
> The company is on the verge of cutting a hundred jobs.

> *Wir sind im Begriff, einen historischen Fehler zu wiederholen.*
> We are about to repeat a historic mistake.

> *Das Eis ist im Begriff zu schmelzen.* — The ice is on the point of
> melting.

### The lofty variant: *im Begriffe stehen*

> *Das Reich stand im Begriffe zu zerfallen.*
> The empire was on the verge of falling apart.

You meet this in historical prose and in translations of the classics;
in speech it sounds deliberately solemn.

## Word breakdown: *Griff* and *Begriff* — one hand, two levels

Both nouns grow out of *greifen* “to seize, grab” — the very same word
as English **grip** / **gripe** / **grope**, and a close relative of
**grasp** and **grapple**.

| Level | Word | What does the grasping |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | *der Griff* | the hand grips a thing: grip, handle |
| Mental | *be-greifen* | the mind grips an idea: comprehend |
| Product | *der Begriff* | what the mind has grasped: a concept |

The prefix *be-* makes the verb transitive and turns the grasp onto an
object: *nach etw. greifen* “reach for” → *etw. begreifen* “get your
arms around → understand”.

**The same metaphor everywhere.** Latin *com-prehendere* “seize
together” gave English **comprehend** and **apprehend**; *con-cipere*
“take in” gave **concept** and **conceive**; and English itself says
*I grasp it*, *get a handle on it*, *it’s beyond my grasp*. German
merely keeps the machinery visible: *Griff → begreifen → Begriff* is
*grip → grasp → concept* with the joints showing.

### So why does *im Begriff sein* mean “about to”?

Because the idiom froze around an **older** sense of *begreifen*: not
“understand” but “**encompass, take in hand, set about**” (compare
*etw. in Angriff nehmen* “to tackle something”). *Der Begriff* here is
not “concept” but “the setting-about, the undertaking” — so *im
Begriff sein* is literally “to be in the act of starting”. Modern
“concept” and idiomatic “about to” are two branches of one root that
parted ways some five centuries ago.

## Related and cognate words

| Word | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| *greifen* | to seize, grab | strong: *greift, griff, gegriffen* |
| *der Griff, -e* | grip; handle, knob | *Türgriff* — door handle |
| *der Handgriff* | knack, deft movement | *mit wenigen Handgriffen* |
| *der Angriff* | attack | *in Angriff nehmen* — to tackle |
| *der Eingriff* | intervention; operation | medical and legal |
| *sich vergreifen* | to pick wrongly, misjudge | *sich im Ton vergreifen* |
| *begreifen* | to grasp, comprehend | the process, not the state |
| *begreiflich* | understandable | *begreiflicherweise* — understandably |
| *begriffsstutzig* | slow on the uptake | 💬 mildly insulting |
| *der Inbegriff* | the epitome, embodiment | *der Inbegriff des Luxus* |
| *der Oberbegriff* | superordinate term | logic and taxonomy |
| *begrifflich* | conceptual | philosophy, linguistics |

### Bonus: a third phrase with *Begriff*

> *Der Name ist mir ein Begriff.* — The name means something to me /
> rings a bell.
> *Sie hat keinen Begriff davon, wie schwer das ist.*
> She has no idea how hard it is.

Here *Begriff* is the modern “concept”: “to be a concept to someone” =
to be known to them.

## How not to confuse them with the neighbours

### Around *im Griff haben*

| Phrase | Nuance | Register |
|---|---|---|
| *etw. im Griff haben* | coping, mastering in practice | 💬 neutral |
| *etw. unter Kontrolle haben* | near-synonym, slightly more technical | 📰 |
| *etw. beherrschen* | master fully (a language, a craft) | neutral |
| *etw. bewältigen* | cope with, get through something hard | 📰 |
| *etw. schaffen* | manage it, pull it off — the result | 💬 |
| *etw. in der Hand haben* | hold the power/the cards, not the skill | neutral |

> *Er beherrscht Russisch.* — He has mastered Russian (knows it).
> *Er hat das Programm im Griff.* — He can handle the program.

### Around *im Begriff sein*

| Phrase | Nuance | Register |
|---|---|---|
| *im Begriff sein, zu …* | about to, on the verge | 📰 bookish |
| *dabei sein, etw. zu tun* | exactly the same, but spoken German | 💬 |
| *gerade* + present | the ordinary everyday way to say it | 💬 |
| *kurz davor sein, zu …* | on the brink, often “very nearly did” | 💬 |
| *drauf und dran sein, zu …* | about to, emotionally charged | 💬 informal |
| *vorhaben, etw. zu tun* | intend to — a plan, not a moment | neutral |

> *Ich bin gerade dabei zu kochen.* — I’m cooking right now.
> *Ich war drauf und dran zu kündigen.* — I very nearly quit.
> *Ich habe vor zu kündigen.* — I’m planning to quit (some day).

## Traps

| ❌ Wrong | ✅ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *Ich bin im Begriff die Arbeit.* | *… die Arbeit zu beenden.* | needs *zu* |
| *Ich habe im Begriff, zu gehen.* | *Ich bin im Begriff, zu gehen.* | only *sein* |
| *Ich bin die Lage im Griff.* | *Ich habe die Lage im Griff.* | only *haben* |
| *… in dem Griff bekommen* | *… in den Griff bekommen* | motion → acc. |
| *Er hat Deutsch im Begriff.* | *Er hat Deutsch im Griff.* | control = *Griff* |

Separately: *Die Lage ist im Griff* does exist (an impersonal result,
“the situation is under control”), but *✗ Die Lage ist im Begriff*
with no infinitive is simply meaningless.

## Register

| Setting | *im Griff haben* | *im Begriff sein* |
|---|---|---|
| 💬 Conversation | ✅ entirely ordinary | ⚠️ bookish; people say *dabei sein* |
| 📰 Press | ✅ very frequent | ✅ frequent, esp. in analysis |
| 🏛 Officialese | ✅ | ✅ |
| 📚 Literature | ✅ | ✅ plus *im Begriffe stehen* |
| 📜 Archaic | — | *im Begriffe* with the *-e* |
| 🔬 Academic | ✅ (of processes) | ✅ (of transitional states) |

## Typical contexts

**im Griff haben:** a fire, an epidemic, inflation, a crisis, pain,
diabetes, one’s nerves, a class, a team, a market, a machine, a new
tool, a language at the practical level.

**im Begriff sein:** news and chronicle (“the country is about to…”),
business correspondence, historical writing, the dramatic beat just
before something happens.

## How to remember

One sentence holding both:

> *Wer die Lage **im Griff** hat, ist nicht **im Begriff**, in Panik
> zu geraten.*
> Whoever has the situation under control is not about to panic.

The logic: **Griff = your hand on the thing, Begriff = your hand on
the deed.** Both are grips; the question is what you have hold of.
*Griff* grabs an object or a situation and **holds** it; *Begriff*
grabs the action itself — you have already **taken it in hand**, so
you are on the point of doing it. The prefix *be-* is what lifts the
grip from the palm to the head: first “encompass, set about” (hence
“about to”), later “comprehend” (hence “concept”). In doubt, ask
yourself: is it in my **hand** (*Griff*), or has my hand already
closed around the **doing** of it (*Begriff*)?
