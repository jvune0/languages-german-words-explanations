# beschließen vs. entscheiden vs. sich entschließen — shades of “decide”

> Three verbs (plus two neighbours, *festlegen* and *bestimmen*) cover what
> English calls “to decide”. What separates them is not the strength of the
> decision but **the picture inside the word**: *beschließen* closes a
> question, *entscheiden* cuts it in two, *sich entschließen* turns a lock
> inside the person. Hence their different subjects: a body, a chooser, and
> someone who has finally made up their mind.

## Grammar

| Verb | Type | Principal parts | Government |
|---|---|---|---|
| *beschließen* | strong, insep. | *beschloss – beschlossen* | *etw.*; *zu* + inf. |
| *entscheiden* | strong, insep. | *entschied – entschieden* | *etw.*; *über etw.* |
| *sich entscheiden* | strong, reflexive | *entschied sich* | *für / gegen etw.* |
| *sich entschließen* | strong, reflexive | *entschloss sich* | *zu etw.* (dat.) |
| *festlegen* | weak, separable | *legte fest – festgelegt* | *etw.*; *sich auf etw.* |
| *bestimmen* | weak, insep. | *bestimmte – bestimmt* | *etw.*; *über etw.* (acc.) |

All take *haben*. The three key nouns divide up just as sharply as the
verbs — and each demands **its own** collocating verb:

| Noun | What it is | Fixed verb |
|---|---|---|
| *der Beschluss* | a resolution passed by a body | *einen Beschluss **fassen*** |
| *die Entscheidung* | a choice, a ruling | *eine Entscheidung **treffen*** |
| *der Entschluss* | inner resolve | *einen Entschluss **fassen*** |

⚠️ *eine Entscheidung treffen* but *einen Beschluss/Entschluss fassen* —
these are not interchangeable.

## The key: a lock versus a knife

Both roots describe a physical act, and that act explains everything else.

- ***beschließen*** ← *schließen* “to close, to lock” (relatives: *das
  Schloss* lock/castle, *der Schlüssel* key, *der Schluss* end and
  conclusion). To decide is to **close the question**. English takes exactly
  the same road: *conclude* ← Latin *claudere* “to close”, and it likewise
  means both “to infer” and “to bring to an end”. That is why *beschließen*
  has both senses too.
- ***entscheiden*** ← *scheiden* “to separate, to part” (relatives: *die
  Scheidung* divorce, *der Scheideweg* crossroads, *unterscheiden* to
  distinguish). To decide is to **cut it in two**, severing one option from
  the other — precisely English *decide* ← Latin *de-caedere* “to cut off”
  (compare *homicide*, *caesura*).
- ***sich entschließen*** — the same lock, turned inward: a person **locks
  themselves into** the decision. Hence *entschlossen* “determined” and
  *die Entschlossenheit* “resolve”.

> **beschließen shuts the door on a question, entscheiden cuts the question
> in half, sich entschließen turns the lock inside a person.**

## 1. *beschließen* — to resolve; to conclude

### 1.1. Taking a decision (usually collective and formal)

> *Der Bundestag hat das Gesetz beschlossen.*
> The Bundestag has passed the law.

> *Der Verein beschloss, die Beiträge zu erhöhen.*
> The association resolved to raise the membership fees.

> *Ich habe beschlossen, mit dem Rauchen aufzuhören.*
> I have decided to give up smoking.

The last example shows that in speech *beschließen* works perfectly well
with a personal “I have decided” — it sounds firm and final, no longer open
to discussion.

### 1.2. To bring to an end (literary)

> *Sie beschloss den Abend mit einem Lied.* — She closed the evening with a
> song.
> *Damit sei die Debatte beschlossen.* — With that, the debate is closed.

📜 This sense is elevated and formal — the same door, only it shuts on an
event rather than a question.

## 2. *entscheiden* / *sich entscheiden* — to settle; to choose

### 2.1. *etw. entscheiden* — to settle a dispute, to determine an outcome

> *Das Gericht muss entscheiden, wer im Recht ist.*
> The court must decide who is in the right.

> *Ein Elfmeter entschied das Spiel.*
> A penalty decided the match.

> *Darüber entscheidet nicht der Chef allein.*
> That is not for the boss alone to decide.

### 2.2. *sich entscheiden für / gegen* — to make a choice

The everyday form: there are options and one must be picked.

> *Ich habe mich für die zweite Wohnung entschieden.*
> I went for the second flat.

> *Sie entschied sich gegen ein Studium im Ausland.*
> She decided against studying abroad.

> *Zwischen den beiden Angeboten kann ich mich nicht entscheiden.*
> I can’t choose between the two offers.

⚠️ This is where learners slip most often: **choosing between options is
always *sich entscheiden***, never *beschließen*.

### 2.3. Derivatives

*entscheidend* — decisive (*der entscheidende Moment*); *es entscheidet
sich* — it will be decided (*Morgen entscheidet sich alles*);
*unentschieden* — a draw.

## 3. *sich entschließen* — to make up one’s mind

An inner decision, often hard-won: there were doubts and the person has
overcome them. The stress is neither on choice nor on procedure but on the
**move from hesitation to action**.

> *Nach langem Zögern entschloss er sich zur Operation.*
> After long hesitation he made up his mind to have the operation.

> *Sie konnte sich nicht dazu entschließen, ihn anzurufen.*
> She could not bring herself to call him.

> *Kurz entschlossen kaufte er das Ticket.*
> Without a second thought he bought the ticket. (set phrase *kurz
> entschlossen*)

## 4. The neighbours: *festlegen* and *bestimmen*

| Verb | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *festlegen* | fixes a date, a rule, a parameter | *den Termin festlegen* |
| *sich festlegen* | commits oneself | *Ich will mich nicht festlegen.* |
| *bestimmen* | determines by authority | *Der Chef bestimmt, wer fährt.* |
| *bestimmen* | determines = governs | *Das Wetter bestimmt unsere Pläne.* |
| *vereinbaren* | two parties agree | *einen Termin vereinbaren* |

📎 *festlegen* in full: the [fest-Verben](fest-Verben.md) card.

## Summary table

| Verb | Subject | What happens | Register |
|---|---|---|---|
| *beschließen* | a body or group; also “I” | the question is closed for good | ⚖️📰💬 |
| *entscheiden* | a judge, an authority, a factor | a dispute is settled | ⚖️📰 |
| *sich entscheiden* | anyone choosing | one option is picked | 💬 everyday |
| *sich entschließen* | a person after hesitating | doubts overcome | 📰 weightier |
| *festlegen* | whoever pins it down | fixed, no longer open | 📰 business |
| *bestimmen* | whoever holds authority | laid down from above | ⚖️ |

## How not to confuse them

> ❌ *Ich habe zwischen zwei Angeboten beschlossen.*
> ✅ *Ich habe mich zwischen zwei Angeboten entschieden.* — options on the
> table → *sich entscheiden*.

> ❌ *Der Vorstand hat sich entschlossen, die Preise zu erhöhen.*
> This makes the board sound like it wrestled with its conscience. For a
> body: ✅ *Der Vorstand hat beschlossen, die Preise zu erhöhen.*

> ❌ *Ich entscheide, morgen zu fahren.* — no options, no authority.
> ✅ *Ich habe beschlossen, morgen zu fahren.* (firmly) or
> ✅ *Ich habe mich entschlossen, morgen zu fahren.* (after thought)

Three questions settle it:

| Question | “Yes” means |
|---|---|
| Is there a body, minutes, formality? | *beschließen* |
| Are there options to choose between? | *(sich) entscheiden* |
| Were there doubts that had to be overcome? | *sich entschließen* |

## Typical contexts

- **Politics and law:** *ein Gesetz beschließen*, *das Gericht entscheidet*,
  *einen Beschluss fassen*, *die Entscheidung anfechten*.
- **Work:** *sich für einen Bewerber entscheiden*, *den Termin festlegen*.
- **Sport:** *das Spiel entscheiden*, *unentschieden*.
- **Personal:** *Ich habe beschlossen …*, *kurz entschlossen*, *ein
  schwerer Entschluss*.
- **Shops and cafés:** *Haben Sie sich schon entschieden?* — “Are you ready
  to order?”, the standard waiter’s question.

## How to remember

Three images, each hidden inside its own word:

- **beschließen** — **a lock on a door** (*das Schloss*): the question is
  shut, the key turned, discussion over. Exactly like English *conclude*
  from *claudere* “to close” — which is also why it can mean “to end”.
- **entscheiden** — **a knife**: two options are cut apart and one is
  discarded. Exactly like English *decide* from *caedere* “to cut”, and like
  *deciding* an *incision*.
- **sich entschließen** — **the same lock, but inside the person**: it
  clicks shut, the wavering stops, and you are *entschlossen*.

One sentence with all three: *Der Vorstand **beschloss** die Prüfung, ich
**entschied** mich für Variante B und **entschloss** mich, sofort zu
beginnen.* — “The board resolved on an audit, I opted for option B, and I
made up my mind to start at once.”
