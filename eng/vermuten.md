# vermuten — to suppose, to suspect, to presume

A weak verb with an inseparable prefix; neutral and very common. It
names a **reasoned guess**: not a shot in the dark, not settled
knowledge, but "by all the signs, this is how it is". Hidden inside is
**der Mut** — not in today's sense of "courage", but in its ancient
one: "**mind, disposition, turn of thought**".

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Type | **weak**; prefix **inseparable**, unstressed |
| Principal parts | *vermutet · vermutete · hat vermutet* |
| Participle II | *vermutet* — **no *ge-*** |
| *zu*-infinitive | *zu vermuten* (not *verzumuten*) |
| Government | *etw.* (acc.) *vermuten* |
| Main pattern | *vermuten, **dass** …* — to suppose that … |
| Location frame | *jdn./etw. irgendwo vermuten* — to think sb. is somewhere |
| Impersonal | *es ist zu vermuten, dass …* — it may be presumed |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |

⚠️ **No reflexive use** and no *sich* construction: *ich vermute mich*
does not exist.

## Main meanings

### 1. To suppose, to presume (with a *dass* clause)

The commonest use. In speech the *dass* is often dropped and main
clause order follows — exactly as in English *I suppose he …*

> *Ich vermute, dass er den Zug verpasst hat.*
> I suppose he missed the train.

> *Wir vermuten, sie kommt gar nicht mehr.*
> We suspect she isn't coming at all now.

> *Es ist zu vermuten, dass die Preise weiter steigen.*
> It may be presumed that prices will keep rising.

### 2. To suppose something to be somewhere

A "where do we look for it" frame: **whom/what + where**.

> *Die Polizei vermutet den Täter im Ausland.*
> The police believe the perpetrator is abroad.

> *Man vermutet dort Öl.*
> Oil is suspected to be there.

> *Ich hätte ihn hier nicht vermutet.*
> I wouldn't have expected to find him here.

### 3. To suspect, to sense (something hidden)

> *Ich habe so etwas schon vermutet.*
> I'd already suspected something of the sort.

> *Dahinter vermute ich Absicht.*
> I suspect intent behind it.

### 4. *vermutlich* — presumably, probably (adverb)

The derived adverb turns up in speech more often than the verb itself.

> *Er kommt vermutlich später.*
> He'll presumably come later.

> *der vermutliche Täter* — the presumed perpetrator

## Word breakdown and etymology

**ver-** + **muten** (← **der Mut**)

- **der Mut** now means "courage", but originally "**mind, temper,
  turn of thought**" (Old High German *muot* "spirit, disposition").
  A whole nest of words preserves that older sense with no bravery in
  sight: *die **Mut**maßung* (conjecture), *die Wehmut* (wistfulness),
  *die Anmut* (grace), *der Unmut* (displeasure), *die Schwermut*
  (melancholy), *zumute sein* (to feel a certain way).
- **muten** is an old verb "to direct one's mind, to desire, to
  demand"; it barely survives alone, but lives on in *zumuten* (to
  expect too much of someone) and *anmuten* (to strike one as).
- **ver-** here is directive-intensive: "to turn over in the mind".

Literally: **"to have a mind that …"**, to set one's thought on a
version of events.

**English parallel.** *Mut* is cognate with English **mood** — and
that is the key to the whole nest: *mood* too once meant "mind,
spirit", not just "state of feeling". Old English *mōd* even meant
"courage", which is exactly how German got from "mind" to "bravery".
So *vermuten* is literally "to be of a mind that…", and *Wehmut* is a
"woeful mood". English *surmise* (Latin "to throw over") and *presume*
("to take beforehand") build the same idea on other images.

## Watch out: vermuten vs. mutmaßen

| Verb | Core idea | Register |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **vermuten** | to infer from indications | neutral ✅ |
| **mutmaßen** | to speculate, to guess about | 📰 bookish ⚠️ |

*Mutmaßen* usually carries a hint of disapproval — guessing without
grounds. Yet ⚖️ **mutmaßlich** is a strict legal term: *der
mutmaßliche Täter* is "the alleged perpetrator", not yet convicted.

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Confidence |
|------|-----------|------------|
| **vermuten** | to suppose from evidence | ~70% |
| **annehmen** | to assume (a working hypothesis) | ~75% |
| **glauben** | to believe, to think (subjective) | ~60% |
| **denken** | to think (💬 the most general) | ~60% |
| **ahnen** | to sense, to have an inkling | ~40% |
| **raten** | to guess at random | ~20% |
| **verdächtigen** | to suspect **a person** (of a crime) | ⚖️ |
| **davon ausgehen** | to proceed on the assumption (💼) | ~85% |
| **wissen** | to know | 100% |

**The key splits:**

- **vermuten vs. ahnen.** *Ahnen* is **intuition without argument**
  (*Ich ahne Schlimmes* — "I sense something bad coming");
  *vermuten* is inference from signs.
- **vermuten vs. annehmen.** *Annehmen* is closer to "let's assume" —
  a working premise you then act on.
- **vermuten vs. verdächtigen.** ⚠️ An important trap: *vermuten*
  targets a **fact** (*Ich vermute Betrug* — I suspect there's fraud),
  *verdächtigen* targets a **person** (*Man verdächtigt ihn des
  Betrugs* — he is suspected of fraud).
- **vermuten vs. raten.** *Raten* is guessing blind, as in a game
  (*Rate mal!* — "Have a guess!"), with no grounds at all.

## Register and style

| Setting | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| 💬 Conversation | ✅ everyday (*Ich vermute mal…*) |
| 📰 Press | ✅ (*Die Ermittler vermuten…*) |
| ⚖️ Legal language | ✅ especially *mutmaßlich*, *Vermutung* |
| 🔬 Science | ✅ (*es ist zu vermuten, dass…*) |
| 💼 Business correspondence | ✅ a softening formula |
| 🎭 Elevated style | ➖ the word is plainly neutral |

💼 Handy as a **polite hedge**: *Ich vermute, da liegt ein
Missverständnis vor* — "I suspect there's a misunderstanding here" —
lands far more softly than a flat assertion.

## Typical collocations

- *ich vermute, dass …* — I suppose that …
- *es ist zu vermuten, dass …* — it may be presumed that …
- *wie zu vermuten war* — as was to be expected
- *jdn./etw. irgendwo vermuten* — to believe sb./sth. to be somewhere
- *Böses / Absicht / Betrug vermuten* — to suspect ill, intent, fraud
- *eine Vermutung äußern / anstellen* — to voice a conjecture
- *die Vermutung liegt nahe, dass …* — it is reasonable to suppose
- *reine Vermutung* — pure speculation
- *der mutmaßliche Täter* — ⚖️ the alleged perpetrator
- *vermutlich / höchstwahrscheinlich* — presumably / most likely

## How to remember

The key is to forget "courage" and remember that **der Mut** started
out as English **mood** — mind, spirit, turn of thought. Then
*vermuten* is literally "**to be of a mind that**".

Test yourself on the nest: *Wehmut* is a "woeful mood", *Anmut* a
"pleasing mood" (grace), *Unmut* an "ill mood", and *mir ist komisch
**zumute*** is "I'm in a strange frame of mind". No bravery anywhere —
only the **mood of the mind**.

The English bridge does the work: *Ich vermute, dass…* ≈ *I'm of a
mind that…* Both languages guess with their *mood*.
