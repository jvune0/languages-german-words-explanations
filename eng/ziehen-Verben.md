# -ziehen (anziehen · beziehen · erziehen · abziehen …) — “to pull, to draw”

> *ziehen* is the verb of pulling: to draw, to extract, to move house. The
> nest is huge, but it comes with a rare gift: its prefixed verbs are
> **exact calques of the Latin *ducere* verbs**. *erziehen* is *e-ducate*,
> *abziehen* is *de-duct*. Know the Latin-English series and you already
> half know the German one.

## Grammar of the base verb

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | **strong**: *ziehen – zog – gezogen* |
| Auxiliary | **haben** with an object, **sein** for movement |
| Government | *etw.* (acc.) *ziehen*; *an etw.* (dat.) *ziehen* — to pull at |
| Participle | *ge-zog-en*; inseparables drop *ge-*: *bezogen*, *erzogen* |

> *Ich **habe** den Wagen gezogen.* — I pulled the cart. (an object)
> *Ich **bin** nach Berlin gezogen.* — I moved to Berlin. (movement)

That is exactly the rule set out in the
[treten-Verben](treten-Verben.md) card: *sein* goes with intransitive verbs
of motion, *haben* with anything that takes an accusative object.

The verb’s own senses:

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to pull | *Zieh fester am Seil!* |
| to draw out, to extract | *einen Zahn ziehen*, *eine Karte ziehen* |
| to move house (sein) | *Sie zieht nach Wien.* |
| to be draughty | ***Es zieht!*** — There’s a draught! |
| to grow, to raise | *Tomaten ziehen*, *ein Kind groß ziehen* |
| to brew, to steep | *Der Tee muss fünf Minuten ziehen.* |

## The key: *ziehen* = Latin *ducere*

German *ziehen* and Latin *ducere* “to lead” go back to the same
Indo-European root \*deuk-. English inherited both the Germanic form
(**tow**, **tug**) and the Latin one (*duke*, *conduct*, *educate*). The
correspondences are startlingly exact:

| German | Latin-English | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *ab-ziehen* | *de-duct* (*de-ducere*) | to deduct |
| *ein-ziehen* | *in-duct* | to bring in, to conscript |
| *er-ziehen* | *e-ducate* (*e-ducere*) | “to lead out” → to bring up |
| *voll-ziehen* | to execute, *con-duct* | to carry out |
| *der Zug* | *duct* | pull, draught, conduit |

> **ziehen = to pull and to lead. The prefix says which way: out, in, up,
> away, towards oneself.**

## The main verbs

### 1. *anziehen* — to put on; to attract

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to put on; *sich anziehen* — to get dressed | *Zieh dir eine Jacke an!* |
| to attract | *Der Magnet zieht das Eisen an.* |
| to tighten (a screw) | *die Schraube anziehen* |
| to rise (of prices) | *Die Preise ziehen an.* |

*anziehend* — attractive; *die Anziehungskraft* — gravitational pull; *der
Anzug* — a **suit** (literally “what one puts on”).

### 2. *ausziehen* — to take off (clothes); to move out

> *Zieh die nassen Schuhe aus.* — Take your wet shoes off.
> *Er ist letzten Monat ausgezogen.* — He moved out last month.
> *ein ausziehbarer Tisch* — an extendable table.

*der Auszug* — an extract, an excerpt; a departure; an exodus.

### 3. *umziehen* — to move house; to change clothes

> *Wir ziehen im Mai um.* — We’re moving in May.
> *Ich muss mich noch umziehen.* — I still have to get changed.

📎 Full card: [umziehen](umziehen.md).

### 4. *einziehen* — to move in; to collect; to conscript

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to move into a flat | *Wann zieht ihr ein?* |
| to collect (taxes, dues) | *Beiträge einziehen* |
| to call up for military service | *Er wurde eingezogen.* |
| to pull in (one’s head, stomach) | *den Kopf einziehen* |

### 5. *beziehen* — to put covers on; to draw (an income); to refer to

> *Ich beziehe das Bett frisch.* — I’m putting fresh sheets on the bed.
> *Sie bezieht eine kleine Rente.* — She draws a small pension.
> *Wir haben die neue Wohnung bezogen.* — We have moved into the new flat.
> *Ich beziehe mich auf Ihr Schreiben.* — I refer to your letter.

*der Bezug* — connection; a cover; receipt; *die Beziehung* — relationship.
📎 On the phrase *in Bezug auf*:
[in Bezug auf vs. gemäß](in_Bezug_vs_gemäß.md).

### 6. *erziehen* — to bring up, to raise

The finest parallel in the family: *er-ziehen* is “to draw out, to lead
forth”, precisely Latin *e-ducere* → English **educate**.

> *Sie hat drei Kinder erzogen.* — She brought up three children.
> *ein gut erzogenes Kind* — a well-brought-up child.

*die Erziehung* — upbringing; *der Erzieher* — an educator. ⚠️ Not to be
confused with *die Ausbildung* (vocational training) or *die Bildung*
(education in the broad sense) — German splits what English calls
“education” three ways.

### 7. *abziehen* — to deduct; to strip; to withdraw

> *Ziehen Sie 10 Prozent ab.* — Deduct 10 per cent.
> *Die Truppen ziehen ab.* — The troops are withdrawing.
> *Das Gewitter ist abgezogen.* — The storm has moved off.

*der Abzug* — a deduction; a withdrawal; an extractor hood; a trigger.

### 8. *vorziehen* — to prefer; to bring forward

> *Ich ziehe Tee dem Kaffee vor.* — I prefer tea to coffee. (+ dat.)
> *Der Termin wurde vorgezogen.* — The appointment was brought forward.

*der Vorzug* — an advantage; *vorzüglich* — excellent.

### 9. *entziehen* — to withdraw, to revoke; to evade

> *Man hat ihm den Führerschein entzogen.* — His licence was revoked.
> *Er entzieht sich jeder Verantwortung.* — He evades all responsibility.
> (*sich* + dat.)

*der Entzug* — withdrawal, revocation; drug withdrawal (*auf Entzug sein*).

### 10. *verziehen* — to twist; to spoil; to disperse

> *Er verzog keine Miene.* — He didn’t bat an eyelid.
> *ein verzogenes Kind* — a spoilt child.
> *Die Wolken haben sich verzogen.* — The clouds have cleared.
> *Verzieh dich!* — 💬 Clear off!

### 11. *überziehen* — a two-way verb

| Form | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *ÜBERziehen* | separable | to pull on over, to slip on |
| *überZIEHEN* | inseparable | to overdraw (an account); to overrun (time) |

> *Zieh dir was über!* — Throw something on!
> *Er hat sein Konto überzogen.* — He has overdrawn his account.
> *Der Redner hat überzogen.* — The speaker ran over time.

### 12. Other useful members

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *aufziehen* | to raise; to wind (a clock); 💬 to tease | *ein Kind aufziehen* |
| *zuziehen* | to draw (curtains); *sich etw. z.* — to catch | *sich eine Grippe z.* |
| *zurückziehen* | to withdraw; *sich z.* — to retire | *einen Antrag zurückziehen* |
| *durchziehen* | 💬 to see it through | *Das ziehen wir durch!* |
| *vollziehen* | to carry out, to execute | *ein Urteil vollziehen* |
| *sich unterziehen* (+ dat.) | to undergo | *sich einer Operation unterziehen* |
| *hinzuziehen* | to call in (an expert) | *einen Experten hinzuziehen* |
| *nachziehen* | to tighten; to follow suit | *die Schraube nachziehen* |

## Phrases with *ziehen*

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Schlüsse / Konsequenzen ziehen* | to draw conclusions / to act on them |
| *Bilanz ziehen* | to take stock |
| *etw. in Betracht ziehen* | to take into consideration |
| *etw. in Erwägung ziehen* | to contemplate, to weigh up |
| *etw. in Zweifel ziehen* | to call into question |
| *jdn. zur Rechenschaft ziehen* | to call someone to account |
| *etw. nach sich ziehen* | to entail, to result in |
| *die Aufmerksamkeit auf sich ziehen* | to draw attention to oneself |
| *den Kürzeren ziehen* | to come off worst, to draw the short straw |
| *jdn. durch den Kakao ziehen* | 💬 to take the mickey out of someone |

Note how many of these match English *draw*: *draw conclusions*, *draw
attention*, *draw the short straw*.

## *der Zug* — one word, many senses

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| train | *Der Zug fährt um acht.* |
| draught, pull | *Mach das Fenster zu, es ist Zug.* |
| trait (of character or face) | *ein sympathischer Zug* |
| move (in a game) | *Du bist am Zug.* — It’s your turn. |
| gulp, drag on a cigarette | *in einem Zug austrinken* — to down in one |
| procession | *der Karnevalszug* |

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *der Anzug* | suit |
| *der Auszug* | extract; departure |
| *der Einzug* | moving in; collection (of dues) |
| *der Abzug* | deduction; withdrawal; trigger |
| *der Bezug* | cover; connection; receipt |
| *der Vorzug* | advantage, preference |
| *der Rückzug* | retreat |
| *der Umzug* | move; procession |
| *der Aufzug* | lift; act of a play; 💬 get-up, outfit |

| *der Vollzug* | execution (*der Strafvollzug*) |

⚠️ **Not related:** *der Betrug* “fraud” comes from *betrügen* “to
deceive”, not from *ziehen*, despite looking like *der Bezug*.

## How not to confuse them

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| *anziehen* / *ausziehen* / *umziehen* | put on / take off / change and move |
| *erziehen* / *ausbilden* / *bilden* | to raise / to train / to educate |
| *beziehen* / *bekommen* | to draw regularly (a salary) / to receive once |
| *abziehen* / *subtrahieren* | to deduct (everyday) / to subtract (maths) |
| *vorziehen* / *bevorzugen* | to prefer (+ dat.) / to favour |
| *ÜBERziehen* / *überZIEHEN* | to slip on / to overdraw |
| *der Bezug* / *der Betrug* | connection, cover / fraud |

## Typical contexts

- **Everyday:** *Zieh dich an!*, *Es zieht!*, *Der Tee zieht noch.*
- **Moving house:** *in eine neue Wohnung einziehen*, *der Umzug*, *der
  Einzugstermin*.
- **Money:** *Steuern einziehen*, *die Kosten abziehen*, *das Konto
  überziehen*.
- **Law:** *den Führerschein entziehen*, *einen Antrag zurückziehen*, *ein
  Urteil vollziehen*.
- **Argument and analysis:** *Schlüsse ziehen*, *Bilanz ziehen*, *in
  Betracht ziehen*.

## How to remember

Keep the Latin pair in mind: **ziehen = ducere**, to pull and to lead. The
prefix then gives the direction of pull:

- **an** towards oneself → **attract**, **put on** (*der Anzug*);
- **aus** out → **take off**, **move out** (*der Auszug*);
- **ein** in → **move in**, **collect**, **conscript**;
- **er** out to the result → **bring up** (= *educate*);
- **ab** away → **deduct** (= *deduct*), **withdraw troops**;
- **be** covering the object → **put covers on**, **draw a salary**,
  **refer to**;
- **ent** away from someone → **revoke**;
- **vor** forward → **prefer**, **bring forward**;
- **über** over → **slip on**; stressed on the stem — **overdraw**.

One sentence with the core of the family: *Sie hat ihre Kinder
**erzogen**, ist nach Berlin **gezogen**, hat dort eine Wohnung
**bezogen** und daraus ihre Schlüsse **gezogen**.* — “She brought up her
children, moved to Berlin, took a flat there, and drew her conclusions from
it all.”
