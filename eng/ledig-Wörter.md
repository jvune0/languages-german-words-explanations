# ledig- (ledig · lediglich · erledigen · entledigen) — “free of”

> These words resemble one another because they all grew out of a single
> adjective: **ledig** “free, unattached, empty”. German then added a suffix
> and a couple of prefixes and got “single”, “merely”, “to deal with” and
> “to rid oneself of”. One idea runs through all of them: **something has
> been released from a tie**.

## How the nest is built

| Word | What it is made of | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *ledig* | the root | free of bonds → **single, unmarried** |
| *lediglich* | *ledig* + the suffix *-lich* | **merely, only** |
| *erledigen* | *er-* + *ledig* + *-igen* | to make free → **to deal with** |
| *sich entledigen* | *ent-* + *ledig* + *-igen* | **to rid oneself** of |
| *unerledigt* | *un-* + participle | **outstanding, undone** |

## The key: freedom from a tie

Middle High German *ledic* meant “free, unbound, empty”. Dutch preserves
exactly that sense: **leeg** “empty” is the same word. Beyond Middle High
German the etymology is disputed — there is no secure earlier source.

The development of the senses, though, is transparent:

> **free of bonds** → unmarried (*ledig*)
> **free of anything else** → nothing but, merely (*lediglich*)
> **made free of a task** → to deal with, to settle (*erledigen*)
> **to free oneself of something** → to get rid of (*sich entledigen*)

English takes the same road in *merely*: it comes from *mere* “pure,
unmixed”, that is “free of admixture” → “only”. And *erledigen* is the
functional twin of *to clear* a task or *to get it off one’s plate*.

## 1. *ledig* — single, unmarried

The main modern sense is marital status, and it is the standard term on
forms:

> *Familienstand: ledig* — Marital status: single.
> *Sie ist noch ledig.* — She is still unmarried.
> *eine ledige Mutter* — 📜 an unmarried mother (dated; today
> *alleinerziehend*).

📜 The older legal sense “free of something” survives in genitive formulas:

> *aller Pflichten ledig sein* — to be free of all obligations.

## 2. *lediglich* — merely, solely, no more than

An adverb of the written register; speech uses *nur* or *bloß*.

> *Es handelt sich lediglich um einen Formfehler.*
> It is merely a formal error.

> *Ich wollte lediglich helfen.* — I only wanted to help.

> *Die Kosten betragen lediglich 20 Euro.* — The cost is a mere 20 euros.

Compared with its neighbours:

| Word | Register | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| *nur* | 💬📰 universal | the neutral “only” |
| *bloß* | 💬 colloquial | “just”, often with feeling |
| *lediglich* | 📰⚖️ written | “no more than”, dry and precise |
| *allein* | 📜 literary | “solely” (*allein der Gedanke*) |
| *einzig* | 📰 | “the only one”, stressing uniqueness |

## 3. *erledigen* — to deal with, to see to, to get done

The most frequent member of the family. Literally “to make (a matter) free”
— to clear it off the agenda.

> *Ich muss noch etwas erledigen.* — I still have something to see to.
> *Die Sache ist erledigt.* — The matter is settled.
> *Können Sie das bis Freitag erledigen?* — Can you get it done by Friday?

💬 Two colloquial senses push the same image to its limit:

> *Ich bin total erledigt.* — I’m completely done in.
> *Der Gegner war schnell erledigt.* — The opponent was quickly finished off.

*die Erledigung* — completion, handling; in the plural, *Erledigungen* means
errands (*Ich habe noch ein paar Erledigungen zu machen*). *unerledigt* —
outstanding, not yet dealt with.

## 4. *sich entledigen* (+ genitive) — to rid oneself of

📜 A bookish, faintly solemn verb with a rare government: the genitive.

> *Er entledigte sich seines Mantels.* — He divested himself of his coat.
> *Sie entledigte sich der lästigen Pflicht.* — She rid herself of the
> tiresome duty.

## Why the shape: the *er-* + adjective model

*erledigen* follows a highly productive German pattern: **er- + adjective
(+ -igen/-en) = “to make it so”**. Spot the pattern and you can decode an
unfamiliar verb on sight:

| Verb | From the adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *erledigen* | *ledig* | to make free → to settle |
| *ermöglichen* | *möglich* | to make possible |
| *erleichtern* | *leicht* | to ease, to lighten |
| *erneuern* | *neu* | to renew |
| *erhöhen* | *hoch* | to raise |
| *ermäßigen* | *mäßig* | to reduce (a price) |
| *ergänzen* | *ganz* | to complete, to supplement |
| *sich erübrigen* | *übrig* | to be superfluous |

📎 On prefixes and suffixes: [Präfixe](Präfixe.md), [Suffixe](Suffixe.md).

## Careful: lookalikes from other roots

Several words in *-leid-* and *-leicht-* look related but come from
different roots entirely. This is the real source of the confusion:

| Word | Its actual root | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *beleidigen* | *das Leid* “sorrow, harm” | to insult |
| *erleiden* | *leiden* “to suffer” | to suffer, to undergo |
| *leider* | *das Leid* | unfortunately |
| *erleichtern* | *leicht* “light” | to ease |
| *leiten* | *leiten* “to lead” | to lead, to manage |
| *leer* | a separate root | empty |

⚠️ The nastiest pair is *erledigen* “to settle” and *beleidigen* “to
insult”: near-twins on the page, with no connection whatsoever.

## Register

| Word | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| *ledig* | 📰⚖️ forms and documents; speech prefers *Single* |
| *lediglich* | 📰⚖️ business correspondence, law, academia |
| *erledigen* | 💬📰 everywhere, from kitchen to ministry |
| *erledigt sein* | 💬 “shattered” — colloquial only |
| *sich entledigen* | 📜 literary, slightly old-fashioned |

## Typical contexts

- **Forms:** *ledig / verheiratet / geschieden / verwitwet*.
- **Office:** *eine Aufgabe erledigen*, *Das erledige ich sofort*,
  *unerledigte Vorgänge*.
- **Legal prose:** *Es handelt sich lediglich um …*, *die Angelegenheit ist
  erledigt*.
- **Everyday:** *Ich muss noch Erledigungen machen* — I have errands to run.
- **Speech:** *Bin völlig erledigt.* — I’m absolutely shattered.

## How to remember

Hold on to one image: **ledig means “untied”**.

- a person not tied by marriage is *ledig*;
- a statement tied to nothing but one point is *lediglich* (“no more
  than”);
- a task you have untied yourself from is *erledigt*;
- a burden you have taken off is *sich entledigt*.

One sentence with the whole family: *Er ist **ledig**, hatte **lediglich**
eine Aufgabe — und die hat er **erledigt**.* — “He is single, he had only
one task — and he has dealt with it.”
