# Partizip II — why *angetreten* and not *angetretet*

> A short reference on how the German past participle is assembled (the
> form used for the perfect and the passive). The ending and the position
> of *ge-* follow from just two questions: **what prefix does the verb
> have** and **is it strong or weak**.

## The formula

> **(prefix) + ge + stem + (-t | -en)**

| Slot | Determined by |
|---|---|
| prefix at the front | whether it is separable or inseparable |
| *ge-* in the middle, or nowhere | inseparable prefix / verb in *-ieren* |
| ending *-t* or *-en* | weak verb or strong verb |

## 1. The ending: *-t* or *-en*

| Class | How to spot it | Participle |
|---|---|---|
| Weak (“regular”) | the stem never changes | *ge-frag-t*, *ge-mach-t* |
| Strong | the stem vowel changes | *ge-tret-en*, *ge-nomm-en* |
| Mixed (about a dozen) | stem changes, but ending is *-t* | *ge-brach-t*, *ge-dach-t* |

*treten* is a strong verb (*treten – trat – getreten*), hence **-en**.
There is nothing to choose here: the ending is locked to the verb class,
and the class is simply given by the three principal parts.

**A useful hint.** If the stem vowel changes in the present tense — *du
tr**i**ttst, du g**i**bst, du f**ä**hrst, du l**äu**fst* — the verb is almost
certainly strong, and its participle will end in *-en*.

**Inserted *-e-*.** If the stem ends in *-t*, *-d*, or *-m/-n* after a
consonant, you get *-et*: *ge-arbeit-et*, *ge-wart-et*, *ge-atm-et*.

## 2. Where *ge-* goes

| Verb type | *ge-* | Example |
|---|---|---|
| No prefix | at the front | *gefragt*, *getreten* |
| Separable prefix (*an-, auf-, ein-, aus-, mit-*) | in the middle | *an-ge-tret-en* |
| Inseparable (*be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-, emp-, miss-*) | none | *betreten* |
| Verbs in *-ieren* | nowhere | *studiert*, *telefoniert* |

The rule in one line: **ge- appears only directly before a stressed
syllable.** In *ÁNtreten* the stress is on the prefix, so *ge-* fits right
after it. In *betréten* the prefix is unstressed, so *ge-* has nowhere to
sit and simply drops out.

## 3. Both axes at once

One row per verb, prefixes across the columns. The point is visible at a
glance: the ending never changes, only *ge-* moves.

| Verb | No prefix | Separable | Inseparable |
|---|---|---|---|
| *fragen* (weak) | *ge-fragt* | *nach-ge-fragt* | *be-fragt* |
| *treten* (strong) | *ge-treten* | *an-ge-treten* | *be-treten* |
| *stehen* (strong) | *ge-standen* | *auf-ge-standen* | *ver-standen* |

Which gives the form in question:

> ***an*** (separable prefix) + ***ge*** (after a stressed prefix) +
> ***tret*** (stem) + ***en*** (strong verb) = *angetreten*.

*angetretet* is impossible, because *-t* is the weak ending and *treten*
is strong.

## 4. Quick algorithm

1. Inseparable prefix, or a verb in *-ieren*? → **no ge-**
2. Separable prefix? → **prefix + ge- + stem**
3. Neither? → **ge-** at the front
4. Weak → **-t** (or **-et**), strong → **-en**

## Where *-en* comes from: look at English

This is an old shared Germanic split, and English keeps the very same one:

| German | English | Class |
|---|---|---|
| *ge-frag-t* | *ask-ed* | weak: *-t* ↔ *-ed* |
| *ge-tret-en* | *trodd-en* | strong |
| *ge-geb-en* | *giv-en* | strong |
| *ge-nomm-en* | *tak-en* | strong |
| *ge-brach-t* | *brough-t* | mixed: vowel change + *-t* |

So the *-en* of *getreten* is literally the *-en* of *given*, *taken*,
*broken*, *trodden*, and the *-t* of *gefragt* is the *-ed* of *asked*.
English lost the *ge-* prefix (its fossils survive in archaic *y-clept*,
*y-clad*) but kept the endings intact.

## Where the form is used

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Perfect | *Er hat die Stelle angetreten.* |
| Pluperfect | *Er hatte die Stelle angetreten.* |
| Passive | *Der Raum wurde betreten.* |
| As an adjective | *die angetretene Mannschaft* |

## How to remember

The participle is a **sandwich**: *ge-* is the filling, and it only fits
where a stressed syllable comes right after it. The tail end is the verb’s
“passport”: weak verbs carry *-t*, like English *-ed*; strong verbs carry
*-en*, like *given* and *trodden*.

*an* + *ge* + *tret* + *en*: the prefix is stressed, so the filling goes
behind it; *treten* is strong, so the tail is *-en*. In one line:
**angetreten** works like *taken*, not like *asked*.
