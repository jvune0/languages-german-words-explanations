# -bringen (verbringen · beibringen · umbringen · aufbringen …) — “to convey”

> *bringen* is the verb of delivery: moving something **to where it is
> needed**. The direction comes from the goal, not from the verb, which is
> why German uses it where English switches between *bring* and *take*. Its
> second and even more productive line is **bringing about a state**:
> *in Ordnung bringen*, *zum Ausdruck bringen*, *zur Welt bringen* — where
> this nest meets [setzen](setzen-Verben.md).

## Grammar of the base verb

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | **mixed**: the stem vowel changes, the ending stays weak |
| Principal parts | *bringen – brachte – gebracht* |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Government | *jdm. etw. bringen* (dat. + acc.); *etw. **zu / in / unter*** + noun |
| Class-mates | *denken – dachte*, *kennen – kannte*, *nennen – nannte* |

📎 On mixed verbs, see the [Partizip II](Partizip_II.md) card.

⚠️ **A false friend for English speakers.** German *bringen* covers both
English *bring* and English *take (somewhere)*:

> *Ich bringe dich zum Bahnhof.* — I’ll take you to the station.
> *Bring bitte den Müll runter.* — Take the rubbish down, please.

## The key: deliver it where it belongs

*bringen* is the direct cognate of English **bring** (PGmc \*bringan). Its
original sense is “to convey, to deliver”, and the family runs along two
lines:

> **1) physical delivery: to bring, take, carry out, accommodate.
> 2) bringing to a state: into order, into a rage, to tears.**

The second line is what makes *bringen* simultaneously a staple of official
prose and of everyday speech.

## The main verbs

### 1. *verbringen* — to spend (time)

> *Wir haben den Sommer in Italien verbracht.* — We spent the summer in
> Italy.
> *Wie verbringst du das Wochenende?* — How are you spending the weekend?

⚠️ Time only. Spending **money** is *Geld ausgeben*, never *verbringen* —
a trap for speakers of English, where one verb does both.

### 2. *beibringen* — to teach; to break news gently

> *Er hat mir das Schachspielen beigebracht.* — He taught me to play chess.
> *Wie bringe ich ihr das nur bei?* — How on earth do I tell her?
> (of bad news — the verb carries the care)

The image is “to bring it close to someone” — knowledge or news is carried
up to a person so that they can take it in.

### 3. *umbringen* — to kill

> *Er hat ihn umgebracht.* — He killed him.
> *Sie hat sich umgebracht.* — She took her own life.
> *Diese Arbeit bringt mich noch um!* — 💬 This job will be the death of me!

Here *um-* means “over, down” (compare *umfallen*, *umwerfen*): to bring
someone down.

### 4. *aufbringen* — to raise, to muster; to enrage

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to raise, to find (funds) | *Ich kann das Geld nicht aufbringen.* |
| to muster (patience, courage) | *Er brachte den Mut auf zu gehen.* |
| to anger, to infuriate | *Diese Bemerkung hat ihn aufgebracht.* |
| to start (a rumour, a fashion) | *ein Gerücht aufbringen* |

*aufgebracht* — incensed, up in arms.

### 5. *unterbringen* — to accommodate, to fit in, to place

> *Können wir die Gäste bei euch unterbringen?* — Can we put the guests up
> with you?
> *Ich bringe den Koffer im Schrank unter.* — I’ll stow the case in the
> wardrobe.
> *Er wurde in einer Bank untergebracht.* — He was found a job at a bank.

*die Unterbringung* — accommodation (of guests, of refugees).

### 6. *einbringen* — to yield; to table; to contribute

> *Die Anlage bringt fünf Prozent ein.* — The investment yields five per
> cent.
> *Die Fraktion brachte einen Antrag ein.* — The parliamentary group tabled
> a motion.
> *Bring dich doch mehr ein!* — Do get more involved, contribute!

### 7. *erbringen* — to produce (a result), to furnish

📰 An official word: results, proof, services.

> *Die Untersuchung erbrachte keine Hinweise.* — The investigation produced
> no leads.
> *den Nachweis / den Beweis erbringen* — to furnish proof.
> *Leistungen erbringen* — to render services, to perform work.

### 8. *vorbringen* and *hervorbringen*

> *Er brachte mehrere Einwände vor.* — He raised several objections.
> *Die Region hat große Dichter hervorgebracht.* — The region has produced
> great poets.
> *Sie brachte kein Wort hervor.* — She couldn’t get a word out.

⚠️ *hervorbringen* means “to bring forth”, not “to highlight”. For that use
*hervorheben*: 📎
[feststellen vs. hervorheben](feststellen_vs_hervorheben.md).

### 9. *abbringen* — to dissuade, to divert

> *Davon lasse ich mich nicht abbringen.* — Nothing will talk me out of it.
> *Er hat mich vom Thema abgebracht.* — He led me off the subject.

### 10. Other useful members

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *mitbringen* | to bring along | *Bring bitte Brot mit.* |
| *anbringen* | to fix, to attach; to slip in a remark | *ein Schild anbringen* |
| *zurückbringen* | to bring back, to return | *das Buch zurückbringen* |
| *durchbringen* | to pull through; to squander | *das Vermögen durchbringen* |
| *fertigbringen* | to manage, to contrive | *Er bringt es fertig zu schweigen.* |
| *zusammenbringen* | to bring together; to raise (a sum) | *500 Euro zusammenbringen* |
| *überbringen* | to deliver (a message) 🎭 | *eine Nachricht überbringen* |
| *entgegenbringen* | to show (trust, respect) | *jdm. Vertrauen entgegenbringen* |
| *ausbringen* | to propose (a toast) | *einen Toast ausbringen* |
| *jdn. um etw. bringen* | to deprive of | 📎 [card](jmdn_um_etw_bringen.md) |

## The great layer: *bringen* = “to bring about a state”

The formula: **jdn./etw. + zu / in / unter / auf + noun + bringen**.

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *etw. in Ordnung bringen* | to put right, to sort out |
| *etw. zum Ausdruck bringen* | to express, to convey |
| *etw. zur Sprache bringen* | to raise a subject |
| *etw. auf den Punkt bringen* | to put it in a nutshell |
| *etw. in Erfahrung bringen* | to find out, to ascertain |
| *etw. unter Kontrolle bringen* | to bring under control |
| *etw. zustande / zuwege bringen* | to accomplish, to pull off |
| *etw. hinter sich bringen* | to get something over with |
| *jdn. zum Lachen / Weinen bringen* | to make someone laugh / cry |
| *jdn. auf eine Idee bringen* | to give someone an idea |
| *jdn. dazu bringen, etw. zu tun* | to get someone to do something |
| *ein Kind zur Welt bringen* | to give birth to a child |
| *jdn. ums Leben bringen* | to take someone’s life |
| *etw. mit sich bringen* | to entail, to bring with it |
| *es weit bringen* | to go far, to do well for oneself |

💬 Colloquial formulas from the same root:

> *Das bringt nichts.* — That’s no use.
> *Was bringt mir das?* — What do I get out of it?
> *Das bringt’s!* — That’s the stuff!

## *bringen* / *holen* / *nehmen* — how to keep them apart

The trio learners trip over constantly:

| Verb | The action | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *bringen* | convey it **to where it’s needed** | *Ich bringe dir das Buch.* |
| *holen* | **go and fetch** it from there | *Ich hole das Buch aus dem Regal.* |
| *nehmen* | pick it up, take it | *Ich nehme das Buch mit.* |

> *Bring mir bitte ein Bier mit.* — Bring me a beer. (you are coming to me)
> *Hol mir bitte ein Bier.* — Go and get me a beer. (fetch and return)
> *Nimm einen Schirm mit.* — Take an umbrella. (you are leaving)

The *mitbringen* / *mitnehmen* pair rests on the same distinction: **you
bring things here, you take things there**.

## Typical contexts

- **Everyday:** *Bring bitte den Müll runter*, *jemanden nach Hause
  bringen*, *etwas in Ordnung bringen*.
- **Learning:** *jemandem etwas beibringen*, *es weit bringen*.
- **Office and law:** *den Nachweis erbringen*, *einen Antrag einbringen*,
  *Einwände vorbringen*.
- **News:** *ums Leben bringen*, *unter Kontrolle bringen*, *zur Sprache
  bringen*.
- **Speech:** *Das bringt nichts*, *Bring mich nicht auf die Palme!* (don’t
  drive me up the wall).

## How to remember

*bringen* is English **bring**, only wider: not “here” but **“to where it
is needed”**. Both lines of the family follow from that:

- **delivery**: *mit-* along → bring with you; *unter-* under a roof →
  accommodate; *zurück-* back → return; *ab-* away → dissuade, divert;
- **bringing to a state**: *ver-* right through (time) → spend; *bei-*
  close up → teach; *auf-* up → muster funds, and flare up; *um-* over →
  kill; *hervor-* forth → bring forth; plus the whole *in Ordnung / zum
  Ausdruck / auf den Punkt bringen* system.

One sentence with the core of the family: *Er hat mir Deutsch
**beigebracht**, den Sommer in Berlin **verbracht** und die Sache endlich
**in Ordnung gebracht**.* — “He taught me German, spent the summer in
Berlin, and finally sorted the matter out.”
