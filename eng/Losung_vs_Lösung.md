# Losung vs. Lösung — “watchword” vs. “solution”

> The short answer to “are they related?” is: **it depends which *Losung***.
> Several distinct words hide under that spelling. The commonest one —
> “password, watchword” — is probably **not** related to *Lösung*; but the
> hunter’s *Losung* and the Austrian one come from exactly the same verb
> *lösen* that gives *die Lösung*. Let us take them in turn.

## The formal difference

| Feature | *die Losung* | *die Lösung* |
|---|---|---|
| Gender, plural | die, *-en* | die, *-en* |
| Pronunciation | [ˈloː-zʊŋ] — long **o** | [ˈløː-zʊŋ] — **ö** |
| Meaning | watchword, motto; ⟨special⟩ takings, droppings | solution |
| Source | several different roots | the verb *lösen* |

⚠️ On the page they differ by two dots; in speech, by the whole vowel. They
are easy to confuse only with the eye.

## 1. *die Lösung* — solution (to a problem, and in a flask)

A noun from the verb *lösen* “to loosen, to release, to dissolve”. Both
senses follow from that:

> *Wir haben endlich eine Lösung gefunden.* — We finally found a solution.
> *Es gibt keine einfache Lösung.* — There is no simple solution.
> *eine wässrige Lösung* — an aqueous solution.
> *die Lösung der Aufgabe* — the solution to the problem.

One idea underlies both: **untying a knot** — whether the knot of a problem
or the bond between particles. English works identically: *solution* and
*to solve* come from Latin *solvere* “to untie”, and English too uses
“solution” for what is in the beaker.

## The *lösen* family

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *lösen* | to loosen; to solve; to dissolve | *ein Rätsel lösen* |
| *sich lösen* | to come away; to resolve itself | *Die Spannung löste sich.* |
| *auflösen* | to dissolve; to disband; to resolve | *eine Firma auflösen* |
| *erlösen* | to redeem, to deliver | *der Erlöser* — the Redeemer |
| *ablösen* | to relieve (on duty); to peel off | *die Wachablösung* |
| *einlösen* | to keep (a promise); to cash in | *ein Versprechen einlösen* |
| *auslösen* | to trigger, to set off | *Panik auslösen*, *der Auslöser* |
| *loswerden* | to get rid of | *Ich werde ihn nicht los.* |
| *loslassen* | to let go | *Lass mich los!* |

Nouns: *die Auflösung* (dissolution, disbandment, the dénouement of a
plot), *die Erlösung* (redemption), *die Ablösung* (relief; repayment),
*das Lösegeld* (ransom), *löslich* (soluble), *unlösbar* (insoluble).

## 2. *die Losung* — several words at once

| Sense | Where it lives | Origin |
|---|---|---|
| password, watchword, slogan | 📰 politics, the army | *losen* “to listen” |
| the daily Bible verse | the Moravian Church | *das Los* “lot, draw” |
| the day’s takings | 🇦🇹 Austrian/Bavarian trade | *lösen* “to realise money” |
| animal droppings | 🏹 hunters’ jargon | *sich lösen* “to come away” |

> *Die Losung lautet: Freiheit und Gleichheit.* — The watchword is freedom
> and equality.
> *Der Offizier gab die Losung aus.* — The officer issued the password.
> *die Tageslosung* — 🇦🇹 the day’s takings (Germany says *die
> Tageseinnahmen*).

The standard, pan-German sense is **password or motto**, a synonym of *die
Parole*. The other three are specialist and never turn up in ordinary
speech.

## So are they related?

| Which *Losung* | Related to *Lösung*? |
|---|---|
| password, watchword | ❌ probably not: from *losen* “to listen” (or from *Los*) |
| the Moravian *Losungen* | ❌ from *das Los* “lot” |
| takings (Austrian) | ✅ yes: *Geld lösen* “to realise money” |
| droppings (hunting) | ✅ yes: *sich lösen* “to come away” |

In other words: **in its commonest sense, no** — the resemblance is
misleading. German simply has three different roots that fell together in
this shape.

## Three lookalike roots

| Root | German words | English kin |
|---|---|---|
| *laus-* “loose” | *los*, *lösen*, *die Lösung*, *-los* | *loose*, *lose*, *-less* |
| *hlaut-* “lot” | *das Los*, *die Lotterie*, *auslosen* | *lot*, *lottery* |
| *hleus-* “hear” | *losen* (dial.), *laut* | *listen*, *loud* |

A useful anchor: the German suffix ***-los*** is exactly English
***-less***: *hoffnungslos* — *hopeless*, *arbeitslos* — *jobless*,
*sinnlos* — *senseless*. But *das Los* “lot, fate” is English *lot* (*a lot
of*, *lottery*) — a different root altogether.

> *Er hat ein schweres Los gezogen.* — He drew a hard lot in life.
> *Er ist seine Sorgen los.* — He is rid of his worries.

## How not to confuse them

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *die Lösung* | solution (to a problem; in chemistry) |
| *die Losung* | watchword, password, motto |
| *die Parole* | slogan, watchword (usually political) |
| *die Auflösung* | dissolution; disbandment; dénouement |
| *die Erlösung* | redemption, deliverance |
| *die Ablösung* | changing of the guard; repayment |
| *das Los* | lot; lottery ticket; one’s fate |

⚠️ In political contexts “slogan” is usually *die Parole* or *der Slogan*;
*die Losung* in that sense reads as slightly archaic and solemn 🎭, though
it is firmly at home in military usage as “password”.

## Typical contexts

- **Problems:** *eine Lösung finden*, *die Lösung des Konflikts*,
  *lösungsorientiert* (solution-oriented — a job-ad favourite).
- **Chemistry:** *eine Lösung herstellen*, *das Lösungsmittel* (solvent).
- **Army and politics:** *die Losung ausgeben*, *unter der Losung “…”*.
- **Austria:** *die Tageslosung* in a shop till.

## How to remember

Hold on to the **umlaut**: the two dots came from the verb *lösen* “to
untie”, which has an *ö*.

- **Lösung** is what **unties**: the knot of a problem or the bond between
  molecules. Compare *solve / solution* from Latin *solvere* “to untie”.
- **Losung** is what is **called out**: a password you have to hear (the
  “listen” root) or a verse drawn by lot.

The test sentence: *Die **Lösung** löst das Problem, die **Losung** ruft man
aus.* — “The solution dissolves the problem; the watchword is called out.”
