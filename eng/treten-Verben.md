# -treten (treten · antreten · eintreten · vertreten …) — “to tread, to step”

> One strong verb — *treten* “to put your foot down” — and a dozen or so
> prefixed descendants. The prefix tells you **where the foot goes**: in,
> out, up, back, across, instead of. That single image yields “join a
> party”, “appear on stage”, “resign”, “transgress a law”. The range runs
> from everyday (*Tritt mir nicht auf den Fuß!*) to pure officialese
> (*in Kraft treten* — to come into force).

## Grammar of the base verb

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | strong, ablaut class V, with *e → i* in the present |
| Principal parts | *treten — trat — getreten* |
| Present | *ich trete, du **tritt**st, er **tritt**, wir treten* |
| Imperative | *Tritt!* / *Tretet!* (no *-e*, and with *i*) |
| Subjunctive II | *träte* (bookish: *Er träte gern zurück.*) |
| Auxiliary | **sein** for movement, **haben** for “to kick” |
| Government (kick) | *jdn./etw.* (acc.) *treten* |
| Government (step) | *in / auf / an / vor* + **acc.** (direction) |

**The double *t*.** *du trittst, er tritt, tritt!* really do have two *t*’s
— a fossil of the old intensive stem (*tredan* → *trettan*).

**sein or haben?** The rule is visual:

> *Er **ist** ins Zimmer getreten.* — He stepped into the room. → motion
> *Er **hat** den Ball getreten.* — He kicked the ball. → transitive kick
> *Du **bist** mir auf den Fuß getreten!* — You stepped on my foot!

### Why *ist getreten* but *hat betreten*?

The choice is not driven by meaning but by **transitivity**. German uses
*sein* in the perfect only for verbs that (a) have **no accusative object**
and (b) express a **change of place or state**. The moment a verb acquires
a direct object, the auxiliary switches to *haben* — even if the event
described is the very same movement:

> *Er **ist** in den Raum getreten.* — the goal sits inside a prepositional
> phrase (*in* + acc.); the verb itself has no object → intransitive →
> *sein*.
> *Er **hat** den Raum betreten.* — *den Raum* is a direct object →
> transitive → *haben*.

This is exactly what the prefix *be-* is for: it pulls the goal out of the
prepositional phrase and promotes it to accusative object (*in den Raum
treten* → *den Raum betreten*). The verb turns transitive, and with
transitivity come *haben* and a passive: *Der Raum darf nicht betreten
werden* — whereas “step into the room” has no passive at all. The same
promotion happens all over the language:

| No object → *sein* | Object → *haben* |
|---|---|
| *in den Saal treten* → *ist getreten* | *den Saal betreten* → *hat betreten* |
| *auf den Berg steigen* → *ist gestiegen* | *den Berg besteigen* → *hat bestiegen* |
| *durch Italien reisen* → *ist gereist* | *Italien bereisen* → *hat bereist* |
| *über die Brücke gehen* → *ist gegangen* | *die Brücke überschreiten* → *hat …* |

Two traps that follow from the same rule:

> *Du **bist** mir auf den Fuß getreten.* — *den Fuß* looks like an object,
> but it belongs to the preposition *auf*: the verb has none → *sein*.
> *Er **hat** gegen die Tür getreten.* — no object either, yet no change of
> location: kicking is an activity, not locomotion → *haben*.

Hence, across the family: the transitive members always take *haben* (*hat
betreten, hat vertreten, hat zertreten, hat die Stelle angetreten*), while
the intransitive members of motion and event take *sein* (*ist eingetreten,
ist ausgetreten, ist zurückgetreten, ist aufgetreten*).

## Separable vs inseparable

Separable prefixes are stressed, inseparable ones unstressed.

| Type | Verbs | Past participle |
|---|---|---|
| Separable | *AN-, AUF-, EIN-, AUS-, BEI-, AB-, ZURÜCK-, HERVOR-* | *an-ge-treten* |
| Inseparable | *betreten, vertreten, zertreten* | *betreten* — no *ge-* |
| Both (see below) | *ÜBERtreten* / *überTRETEN* | *übergetreten* / *übertreten* |

> *Ich trete morgen die neue Stelle an.* — I start the new job tomorrow.
> *Ich habe den Raum betreten.* — I entered the room. (no *ge-*)

## The master key: a foot on the ground

Proto-Germanic \*tredan- “to tread, trample” gives English **tread**
(*trod, trodden*) — the direct cognate of *treten*. The same root, seen
from several angles:

| Word | Connection |
|---|---|
| *tread*, *treadmill*, *treadle* | step; the wheel you tread; the pedal |
| *trade* | OE/MLG “a track, a trodden path” → “a course, a business” |
| *trot*, *trottoir* | borrowed into Romance from Germanic “to tramp” |
| German *die Treppe* | “the thing you tread on” — a staircase |
| German *der Trott*, *trotten* | a rut, a plod — the same worn path |

The formula for the whole nest:

> **prefix + treten = “put your foot exactly where the prefix points”**

- *ein-* in → enter, join, (of an event) set in;
- *aus-* out → leave an organisation, stamp out a fire;
- *auf-* up, onto → step onto the stage, appear, occur;
- *an-* up to → take up (a post), fall in, face an opponent;
- *bei-* alongside → accede, join;
- *zurück-* back → step back, resign;
- *über-* across the line → cross over; **trans-gress**;
- *ver-* instead of / awry → represent someone; twist your ankle;
- *zer-* to bits → trample;
- *ab-* off, away → cede, exit the stage.

Every abstract sense is the foot metaphor: to “break” a law is literally
to **step over** it — exactly what Latin *trans-gredi* (“step across”)
says, and what English *overstep the mark* still says out loud.

## The main verbs

### 1. *treten* — to step; to kick; to press

The bare verb lives on in three main roles.

> *Bitte treten Sie näher!* — Please step closer!
> *Er trat gegen die Tür.* — He kicked the door.
> *Tritt auf die Bremse!* — Step on the brake!
> *Tränen traten ihr in die Augen.* — Tears came to her eyes.

That last one is a productive pattern: *treten* for things that appear of
their own accord — *Schweiß trat ihm auf die Stirn* (sweat broke out on
his forehead).

### 2. *antreten* — to take up; to line up; to face an opponent

Government: *etw.* (acc.) *antreten* — *die Stelle, die Reise, das Erbe,
den Dienst, den Beweis antreten*; *gegen jdn. antreten* — to compete.
Core image: walk right up to a thing and set your foot on it.

> *Sie tritt im Mai ihre neue Stelle an.* — She starts her new job in May.
> *Die Mannschaft tritt gegen Spanien an.* — The team is playing Spain.
> *Alles antreten!* — Fall in! (army command)

📎 There is a separate full card: [antreten](antreten.md).

### 3. *auftreten* — to appear, perform; to occur; to behave

Three high-frequency senses; auxiliary *sein* throughout.

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to appear, perform on stage | *als Sänger auftreten* |
| to occur, arise (a phenomenon) | *Nebenwirkungen treten selten auf* |
| to carry oneself, come across | *selbstbewusst auftreten* |

> *Er ist gestern im Fernsehen aufgetreten.* — He was on TV yesterday.
> *Das Problem tritt nur bei Kälte auf.* — The problem only occurs in the
> cold.
> *Sie trat sehr höflich, aber bestimmt auf.* — She came across as very
> polite but firm.

The noun *der Auftritt* covers all three: a performance, a scene in a
play, and colloquially a scene someone *makes*.

### 4. *eintreten* — to enter; to join; to set in; to stand up for

The most polysemous member. Three constructions, three senses:

| Construction | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *in etw.* (acc.) *eintreten* | to walk in | *ins Zimmer eintreten* |
| *in etw.* (acc.) *eintreten* | to join a body | *in die Partei eintreten* |
| impersonal, of events | to set in, occur | *Der Tod trat sofort ein* |
| *für jdn./etw. eintreten* | to stand up for | *für Menschenrechte eintreten* |

> *Treten Sie bitte ein!* — Do come in!
> *Eine Besserung ist nicht eingetreten.* — No improvement has set in.
> *Er tritt entschieden für den Klimaschutz ein.* — He is a firm advocate
> of climate protection.

⚠️ *eintreten* “walk in” is fairly formal/elevated — everyday German uses
*hereinkommen* (*Komm rein!*). But *in einen Verein eintreten* is the only
way to say “join a club”.

### 5. *austreten* — to leave (a body); to stamp out; to wear out; to be excused

The mirror image of *eintreten*, plus some homely extras.

> *Er ist aus der Kirche ausgetreten.* — He formally left the church.
> *Tritt die Zigarette aus!* — Stamp out that cigarette!
> *Die Schuhe sind völlig ausgetreten.* — The shoes are worn right down.
> *Darf ich mal austreten?* — May I be excused? (school/army euphemism
> for the loo)

Also “to leak, escape” of substances: *Gas ist ausgetreten*. Noun: *der
Austritt* — withdrawal; *der EU-Austritt* — Brexit.

### 6. *beitreten* — to join, accede (+ **dative**!)

A bare dative with no preposition — rare enough to be a real trap.

> *Sie ist der Gewerkschaft beigetreten.* — She joined the union.
> *Kroatien trat der EU 2013 bei.* — Croatia acceded to the EU in 2013.

*beitreten* (dat.) vs *eintreten in* (acc.): both “join”, but *beitreten*
is the formal, treaty-and-state word (English *accede*), while *eintreten
in* suits clubs, parties and firms. Noun: *der Beitritt* — accession.

### 7. *betreten* — to enter, set foot on (inseparable, transitive)

A direct object with no preposition — that is the whole difference from
*eintreten*.

> *Betreten verboten!* — No entry / Keep off! (sign)
> *Er betrat den Saal, ohne zu grüßen.* — He entered the hall without a
> greeting.
> *Wir betreten hier juristisches Neuland.* — We are breaking new legal
> ground here.

The identical-looking adjective *betreten* means “abashed, sheepish”:
*betretenes Schweigen* — an embarrassed silence. Same image: someone who
has stepped where they shouldn’t and frozen.

### 8. *vertreten* — to represent, stand in for, hold (a view)

> *Der Anwalt vertritt seinen Mandanten vor Gericht.* — The lawyer
> represents his client in court.
> *Ich vertrete morgen meine Kollegin.* — I’m covering for my colleague.
> *Diese Meinung vertrete ich nicht.* — That is not a view I hold.
> *Ich muss mir die Beine vertreten.* — I need to stretch my legs.
> *Sie hat sich den Fuß vertreten.* — She twisted her ankle.

Here *ver-* does both of its jobs at once: “in place of” (step in someone’s
stead → represent) and “awry” (step wrong → sprain). 📎 Full card:
[vertreten](vertreten.md).

### 9. *zurücktreten* — to step back; to resign; to withdraw from a contract

> *Bitte von der Bahnsteigkante zurücktreten!* — Please stand back from
> the platform edge!
> *Der Minister ist zurückgetreten.* — The minister has resigned.
> *Vom Vertrag zurücktreten* — to withdraw from the contract.

*der Rücktritt* is both “resignation” and the **back-pedal brake** on a
bicycle (*die Rücktrittbremse*).

### 10. *übertreten* — one spelling, two verbs

The classic stress pair; learn it as a pair.

| Form | Stress | Type | Sense |
|---|---|---|---|
| *ÜBERtreten* | on the prefix | separable, *sein* | convert, defect; burst its banks |
| *überTRETEN* | on the stem | inseparable, *haben* | to transgress a law |

> *Er ist zum Islam übergetreten.* — He converted to Islam.
> *Der Fluss ist über die Ufer getreten.* — The river burst its banks.
> *Wer die Vorschriften übertritt, wird bestraft.* — Anyone who breaks the
> rules will be punished.

One logic, two applications: carry the foot **across** a line. Physical
line → motion (*sein*, separable). Legal line → a transitive offence
(*haben*, inseparable) — precisely English *transgress* / *overstep*.

### 11. *abtreten* — to cede; to exit the stage; to wipe (shoes)

> *Er trat seine Rechte an den Bruder ab.* — He ceded his rights to his
> brother.
> *Der Schauspieler tritt ab.* — Exit the actor. (stage direction)
> *Bitte die Schuhe abtreten!* — Please wipe your feet! (*der Abtreter*
> is the doormat)

Figuratively “to bow out, to die”: *von der politischen Bühne abtreten*.

### 12. Other useful family members

| Verb | Government | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| *zertreten* | acc., insep. | to trample, crush | *eine Blume zertreten* |
| *festtreten* | acc. | to tamp down; (fig.) deadlock | *die Erde festtreten* |
| *entgegentreten* | **dat.** | to confront, counter | *Gerüchten entgegentreten* |
| *hervortreten* | *sein* | to emerge, stand out | *Adern treten hervor* |
| *durchtreten* | acc. | to press right down | *die Kupplung durchtreten* |
| *nachtreten* | — | to kick a man when he’s down | *nach dem Rücktritt nachtreten* |
| *kürzertreten* | — | to cut back, ease off | *beruflich kürzertreten* |
| *niedertreten* | acc. | to trample down | *das Getreide niedertreten* |
| *hinzutreten* | *sein*, formal | to be added | *Es tritt hinzu, dass …* |

## How not to confuse them

### “Enter / join”

| Verb | What follows | Flavour |
|---|---|---|
| *betreten* + acc. | *den Raum betreten* | physically set foot on; neutral-formal |
| *eintreten in* + acc. | *ins Zimmer / in die Partei* | walk in; join a body |
| *beitreten* + dat. | *dem Verein beitreten* | accede — legal/diplomatic |
| *hereinkommen* | *Komm rein!* | everyday “come in” 💬 |
| *einsteigen in* + acc. | *ins Auto einsteigen* | get into a vehicle |

### “Leave / quit”

| Verb | Use |
|---|---|
| *austreten aus* + dat. | formally leave a body (church, party, union) |
| *aussteigen aus* + dat. | get out of a vehicle; colloq. “get out of” a deal |
| *zurücktreten* | resign an office |
| *kündigen* | quit a job / terminate a contract (not membership) |
| *verlassen* + acc. | to leave (a room, a country, a person) |

### “Break, violate”

| Option | Register |
|---|---|
| *gegen etw. verstoßen* | the neutral default ⚖️ |
| *etw. übertreten* | bookish-legal: laws and commandments 📜 |
| *etw. brechen* | *das Gesetz / sein Wort brechen* — strong, vivid |
| *etw. verletzen* | violate a right, a border, a confidence (formal) |

## Set phrases

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *in Kraft treten* | to come into force (of a law) |
| *außer Kraft treten* | to cease to be in force |
| *in Erscheinung treten* | to make an appearance, become visible (formal) |
| *in den Vordergrund treten* | to come to the fore |
| *auf der Stelle treten* | to mark time, get nowhere |
| *jdm. zu nahe treten* | to offend someone |
| *jdm. auf die Füße treten* | to tread on someone’s toes |
| *ins Fettnäpfchen treten* | to put your foot in it |
| *den Beweis antreten* | to furnish proof |
| *die Nachfolge antreten* | to succeed someone |
| *kürzertreten* | to ease off, scale back |
| *jdn. mit Füßen treten* | to trample on someone, treat with contempt |

Note how many of these have exact English foot-idioms: *tread on someone’s
toes*, *put your foot in it*, *mark time*, *trample on*. The metaphor is
shared Germanic property.

## Register

| Item | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| *treten* (kick, press) | 💬 everyday, any context |
| *eintreten* “walk in” | 🎭 elevated; everyday German says *reinkommen* |
| *betreten* | 📰 neutral-official: signs and reportage |
| *beitreten* | ⚖️ legal and diplomatic register |
| *in Kraft treten* | ⚖️📰 laws, treaties, news — the only phrasing used |
| *austreten* “be excused” | 💬 school and barracks, mildly comic |
| *zurücktreten* | 📰 daily political news vocabulary |
| *jdm. zu nahe treten* | 🎩 polite hedge: *ohne Ihnen zu nahe treten zu wollen* |
| *ins Fettnäpfchen treten* | 💬 friendly colloquial idiom |

## Typical contexts

- **Politics and news:** *Der Minister trat zurück*; *Das Gesetz tritt am
  1. Januar in Kraft*; *Großbritannien ist aus der EU ausgetreten*.
- **Stage and media:** *Sie tritt heute Abend auf*; *ihr erster Auftritt*.
- **Medicine and engineering:** *Nebenwirkungen können auftreten*; *Es ist
  Öl ausgetreten*.
- **Work:** *eine Stelle antreten*, *einen Kollegen vertreten*, *beruflich
  kürzertreten*.
- **Signs and announcements:** *Betreten der Baustelle verboten*; *Bitte
  zurücktreten!* (on the U-Bahn).
- **Sport:** *gegen den Titelverteidiger antreten*; *den Ball treten*.
- **Home:** *Tritt mir nicht auf den Fuß!*; *Tritt die Kippe aus!*

## How to remember

Picture **a foot over a threshold**. The root *treten* is English **tread**
— to set the sole down. The prefix merely points at where:

- **ein**tritt — foot **in** → entered, joined, an event set in;
- **aus**tritt — foot **out** → left the party, stamped out the stub;
- **auf**tritt — foot **onto the stage** → performed, appeared, occurred;
- **bei**tritt — foot **alongside** → acceded;
- **zurück**tritt — foot **back** → resigned;
- **über**tritt — foot **across the line** → over-stepped = transgressed;
- **ver**tritt — foot **in another’s place** → I represent you (and if the
  foot lands crooked, *sich den Fuß vertreten*, you sprain it).

One sentence holding the whole family: *Er **trat** in die Partei **ein**,
**trat** öffentlich **auf**, **übertrat** ein Gesetz und **trat**
schließlich **zurück**.* — “He joined the party, appeared in public,
broke a law and finally resigned.” A whole career in four steps of one
foot.
