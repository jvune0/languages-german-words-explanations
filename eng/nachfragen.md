# nachfragen — to ask again, to check, to enquire

> Neutral to colloquial: to ask a **follow-up** question — because
> you didn't catch it, didn't understand it, or want to know how
> something turned out. Everything hangs on *nach-*: the question
> comes **after**, in pursuit of something already said.

## Grammar

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | weak verb, **separable** prefix *nach-* |
| Principal parts | *fragt nach — fragte nach — hat nachgefragt* |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Government | **bei + dat.** (the person asked) |
| | **wegen + gen./dat.** (the matter enquired about) |
| | **nach + dat.** (what you enquire after) |
| | a clause with *ob* or a question word |
| Imperative | *frag nach!* / *fragen Sie bitte nach* |
| *zu*-infinitive | *nachzufragen* |
| Noun | *die Nachfrage* — 1) enquiry; 2) **demand** (economics) |

> *Ich habe bei der Bank nachgefragt, ob die Überweisung
> angekommen ist.*
> I checked with the bank whether the transfer had arrived.

⚠️ *nachfragen* is **not** transitive: never *ich frage ihn nach*.
The person is introduced by **bei**: *ich frage bei ihm nach*.

## Main meanings

### 1. To ask again, to double-check (you didn't catch it)

> *Wenn du etwas nicht verstehst, frag einfach nach.*
> If you don't understand something, just ask.

> *Sie nickte, ohne nachzufragen.*
> She nodded without asking any further.

> *Darf ich noch einmal nachfragen: Sie meinen den Montag?*
> May I just check again: you mean Monday?

### 2. To enquire, to check with an office or a person

A question chasing something already set in motion — an
application filed, a matter left open.

> *Ich frage morgen im Sekretariat nach.*
> I'll check with the office tomorrow.

> *Er fragte wegen der Bewerbung nach und bekam eine Absage.*
> He enquired about his application and got a rejection.

> *Fragen Sie bitte bei der Hausverwaltung nach den Kosten nach.*
> Please enquire about the costs with the property management.

### 3. To press, to follow up insistently

The overtone: your counterpart dodged, and you come back at them.

> *Der Journalist fragte hartnäckig nach.*
> The journalist kept pressing the point.

> *Als niemand antwortete, fragte sie noch einmal nach.*
> When nobody answered, she asked again.

### 4. *die Nachfrage* — demand (economic term) 📰

The verbal noun migrated into economics: demand is simply how
often people ask after a good.

> *Angebot und Nachfrage bestimmen den Preis.*
> Supply and demand determine the price.

> *Die Nachfrage nach Wohnraum ist stark gestiegen.*
> Demand for housing has risen sharply.

## Word breakdown and etymology

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *nach-* | "after, in pursuit of" — the question comes **after** |
| *fragen* | to ask |

**The prefix *nach-*** contributes three shades to separable verbs,
and all three are audible in *nachfragen*:

| Shade | Examples | In *nachfragen* |
|---|---|---|
| "after, in pursuit" | *nachlaufen*, *nachrufen* | asking **after** a first try |
| "additionally, again" | *nachzahlen*, *nachbestellen* | asking **again** |
| "into it, checking" | *nachschlagen*, *nachprüfen* | **looking into** a matter |

**The root *fragen*** goes back to OHG *frāgēn*, from PIE *\*prek̑-*
"to ask, entreat" — the same root as Latin *precari* "to pray"
(hence English *pray*, *precarious*) and *procus* "suitor". English
kept a direct cognate in the archaic *to frain* "to ask"; modern
*ask* is unrelated. The prefix, though, maps perfectly: *nachfragen*
is literally **to ask after** something.

## Related and cognate words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *die Nachfrage* | 1) enquiry, follow-up question; 2) **demand** |
| *nachhaken* (coll.) | lit. "to hook after" — to press even harder |
| *anfragen* | to submit a formal enquiry (*die Anfrage*) |
| *befragen* | to question, to survey (takes the accusative) |
| *erfragen* | to find out by asking, to elicit |
| *hinterfragen* | to question critically, to probe assumptions |
| *abfragen* | to quiz through a list; to query data (IT) |
| *fragwürdig* | dubious (literally "worthy of a question") |

## Synonyms and how they differ

| Word | Government | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| *nachfragen* | *bei jdm.* | 💬 follow-up: ask again / check up |
| *fragen* | *jdn.* (acc.!) | plain "to ask", no "after" element |
| *nachhaken* | *bei jdm.* | 💬 to keep at it, refuse to drop the point |
| *anfragen* | *bei jdm.* | 📰 a formal, usually written enquiry |
| *sich erkundigen* | *nach + dat.* | 📰 politely formal: to make enquiries |
| *befragen* | *jdn. zu etw.* | 📰 to interview, to poll |
| *hinterfragen* | *etw.* (acc.) | 📰 to challenge a premise, not learn a fact |
| *nachforschen* | *nach + dat.* | 📰 to investigate, to dig yourself |

**Pitfalls:**

- *fragen* takes an **accusative** person (*ich frage ihn*);
  *nachfragen* takes **bei** (*ich frage bei ihm nach*).
- *hinterfragen* ≠ *nachfragen*: the first questions the premise,
  the second asks for a missing detail.
- *die Nachfrage* in an economics text means **demand**, not
  "question"; a business "enquiry" is normally *die Anfrage*.
- English *to ask after someone* means to ask how they are —
  German *nach jemandem fragen*, not *nachfragen*.

## Register and style

| Setting | Fit |
|---|---|
| 💬 Everyday speech | ✅ its home ground |
| 🏢 Spoken business German | ✅ *ich frage mal nach* |
| ✉️ Business email | ✅ *ich möchte höflich nachfragen* |
| 📰 Press, interviews | ✅ *der Reporter fragte nach* |
| 🏛 Legal prose | ⚠️ prefer *anfragen*, *erfragen* |
| 📈 Economics | ✅ but as the noun *die Nachfrage* |

## Typical contexts and collocations

- *(noch einmal) nachfragen* — to ask (once) again;
- *höflich / vorsichtig / hartnäckig nachfragen* — to enquire
  politely / cautiously / insistently;
- *bei der Firma / im Büro / beim Amt nachfragen* — to check with
  the company / the office / the authority;
- *wegen des Termins nachfragen* — to check about the appointment;
- *nachfragen, ob …* — to ask whether …;
- *Wenn Sie Fragen haben, fragen Sie gern nach.* — a standard
  closing courtesy in emails;
- *rege / große Nachfrage* — brisk / high demand.

## How to remember

*nach* is English **after**. *nachfragen* is literally **to ask
after**: the question sent in pursuit, once the first one went
unheard or unanswered. And that is also where **demand** comes from
— *die Nachfrage* is the goods people **keep asking after**.

> One line to keep: *nachfragen* = chase the conversation with one
> more question; ask often enough and you have created **demand**.
