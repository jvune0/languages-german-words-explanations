# aufwenden — to expend, to devote (money, time, effort)

A separable-prefix verb of formal register: everyday German reaches
for *ausgeben* or *brauchen*, while *aufwenden* lives in reports,
contracts and commentary. The sense is not merely "to spend" but
**to commit a resource to a purpose**. The root *wenden* "to turn"
says it: you **turn** your means in the required direction.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Prefix type | **separable** (*trennbar*), stressed: *AUFwenden* |
| Principal parts | *wendet auf · wandte/wendete auf · aufgewandt/-wendet* |
| Participle II | *aufgewandt* (commoner) / *aufgewendet* — both correct |
| Preterite | *wandte auf* (commoner) / *wendete auf* — both correct |
| *zu*-infinitive | *aufzuwenden* |
| Government | *etw.* (acc.) *für etw.* (acc.) *aufwenden* |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Separation | *Er wendet viel Zeit dafür auf.* |
| Passive | very frequent: *es wurden Mittel aufgewandt* |

**On the double forms.** This is a "mixed" verb: strong *wandte auf*
and weak *wendete auf* are both standard, with *aufgewandt* slightly
ahead in usage. Simple *wenden* splits its forms by meaning — see
below.

## Main meanings

### 1. To expend money, funds

> *Die Stadt wandte drei Millionen Euro für die Sanierung auf.*
> The city spent three million euros on the refurbishment.

> *Es müssen erhebliche Mittel aufgewendet werden.*
> Considerable funds will have to be expended.

> *Dafür wurde kein Cent aufgewandt.*
> Not a cent was spent on it.

### 2. To devote time, effort, energy

> *Sie hat viel Zeit und Mühe dafür aufgewandt.*
> She devoted a great deal of time and effort to it.

> *Wie viel Energie wendest du täglich dafür auf?*
> How much energy do you expend on it daily?

> *Er wandte seine ganze Kraft auf, um zu überzeugen.*
> He applied all his strength to convincing them.

### 3. To apply, to exert (in set phrases)

> *alle Kräfte / großen Fleiß aufwenden* — to exert every effort,
> great diligence

> *Es wurde große Sorgfalt aufgewendet.*
> Great care was taken.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**auf-** + **wenden**

- **wenden** — "to turn"; the English cognate is **to wend**, still
  alive in *to wend one's way*. More strikingly, English **went** is
  the old past tense of *wend*, later borrowed by *go*. So German
  *wandte* and English *went* are **literally the same word**.
- **auf-** here means neither "up" nor "open" but **"use up,
  expend"** — the same force as in *aufbrauchen* (to use up),
  *aufessen* (to eat up), *aufzehren* (to consume).

Literally: **"to turn (a resource) onto something, and so use it up"**.

English parallels line up neatly: *to devote* is Latin "to vow away",
*to expend* is "to weigh out", and *to spend* is that same *expendere*
worn down. All of them frame the resource as something moved outward
toward a purpose, exactly as *aufwenden* does.

⚠️ **A trap inside German.** The adjective **aufwendig** (also spelt
*aufwändig*) does **not** mean "wasteful" but "costly, elaborate,
labour-intensive": *ein aufwendiges Verfahren* is a laborious
procedure. The tone is neutral or admiring, never disapproving —
closer to English *elaborate* than to *extravagant*.

## Watch out: the forms of *wenden*

Simple *wenden* splits its forms **by meaning**, while the prefixed
verbs (*aufwenden*, *verwenden*, *anwenden*) allow both freely:

| Meaning | Forms | Example |
|---------|-------|---------|
| To turn physically | *wendete · gewendet* | *Er wendete den Wagen* |
| To turn to someone | *wandte · gewandt* | *Sie wandte sich an mich* |

> *Bitte wenden!* (*b.w.*) — "Please turn over" (P.T.O.), the standard
> note at the foot of a page.

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Register |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **aufwenden** | to commit a resource to a purpose | 💼 formal |
| **ausgeben** | to spend money (neutral) | 💬 everyday |
| **verwenden** | to use, to employ (for sth.) | neutral |
| **investieren** | to invest (expecting a return) | 💼 |
| **verbrauchen** | to consume (fuel, electricity) | neutral |
| **aufbringen** | to raise, to muster (a sum, courage) | 💼 |
| **opfern** | to sacrifice (with loss) | 🎭 |
| **verschwenden** | to squander, to waste | ⚠️ negative |

**The key splits:**

- **aufwenden vs. ausgeben.** *Ausgeben* takes money only, in everyday
  speech (*Ich habe 50 Euro ausgegeben*). *Aufwenden* covers money,
  time and effort alike, and sounds like a report.
- **aufwenden vs. aufbringen.** *Aufbringen* is to **muster** the
  resource (*die Summe aufbringen* — to raise the sum); *aufwenden* is
  to **expend** it. First you *aufbringen*, then you *aufwenden*.
- **aufwenden vs. verschwenden.** One prefix flips the verdict:
  *verschwenden* is to spend **in vain**. *Aufgewandte Zeit* was
  well used; *verschwendete Zeit* is time wasted.

## Register and style

| Setting | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| 💼 Reports, accounting | ✅ its home ground (*Aufwendungen*) |
| ⚖️ Legal texts | ✅ (*erforderliche Mittel aufwenden*) |
| 📰 Press, analysis | ✅ (*Milliarden wurden aufgewandt*) |
| 🔬 Science, engineering | ✅ (*Energieaufwand*) |
| 📚 Written standard | ✅ neutral-formal |
| 💬 Conversation | ➖ speakers use *ausgeben*, *brauchen*, *stecken in* |

The colloquial substitute: *Da habe ich viel Zeit **reingesteckt**.* —
"I put a lot of time into that."

## Typical collocations

- *Zeit / Mühe / Kraft / Energie aufwenden*
- *Geld / Mittel / Millionen für etw. aufwenden*
- *große Sorgfalt / viel Fleiß aufwenden*
- *alle Kräfte aufwenden, um … zu …*
- *der finanzielle / zeitliche / personelle Aufwand*
- *mit großem Aufwand betreiben* — to run at great expense
- *ohne großen Aufwand* — without much trouble
- *Aufwand und Ertrag* — cost and benefit
- *sich den Aufwand sparen* — to save oneself the trouble
- *ein aufwendiges Verfahren / eine aufwendige Produktion*

## How to remember

Look at the root: **wenden** = English **went**. You **turn** your time
and money toward the goal — and away they go. The prefix *auf-* works
as in *aufessen*: right to the last drop.

Second image: **der Aufwand** is "what has been turned onto the job".
From it come *aufwendig* "elaborate" and the accountant's
*Aufwendungen* "expenses".

And English *devote* holds the same shape from the Latin side: things
vowed away to a purpose. German turns them, Latin vows them — one
gesture, two languages.
