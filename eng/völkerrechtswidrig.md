# völkerrechtswidrig — contrary to international law

A long German compound (a classic *Bandwurmwort*, “tapeworm word”)
that comes apart into three tidy pieces. Firmly legal/press register.

## Breakdown

> **Völker** + **Recht** + **s** + **widrig**

| Part | Meaning |
|-------|----------|
| **das Volk** (pl. *Völker*) | people, nation (peoples) |
| **das Recht** | law, right |
| **-s-** | the linking *Fugen-s* |
| **widrig** | contrary to, adverse |

- **Völkerrecht** = international law (literally “the law of
  peoples”, a calque of Latin *ius gentium* — English once had the
  same calque, “law of nations”)
- **-widrig** as a suffix = “contrary to, in violation of”; as a free
  adjective it takes the dative: *etwas ist dem Gesetz widrig*

Result: *völkerrechtswidrig* = “contrary to international law”, “in
breach of international law”.

**widrig** is the same root as **wider** “against” (and *wieder*
“again” — same origin, split in spelling). English relatives: **with-**
in *withstand* and *withhold*, which preserves the old sense
“against”, not “together with”. So *-widrig* ≈ English *anti-* /
*-breaching*.

## The inflected form *völkerrechtswidrigen*

The ending **-en** marks weak or mixed adjective declension — most
often accusative masculine, or any case in the plural after an
article.

> *die Verurteilung des völkerrechtswidrigen Angriffs*
> the condemnation of the attack, which breached international law
>
> *gegen den völkerrechtswidrigen Krieg*
> against the war waged in breach of international law

English has no single adjective here, so translations usually turn
the word into a relative clause or a prepositional phrase — “in
violation of international law”, “unlawful under international law”.

## Examples in context

- *ein völkerrechtswidriger Angriffskrieg*
  a war of aggression in violation of international law
- *die völkerrechtswidrige Annexion*
  the annexation, unlawful under international law
- *Diese Handlung ist eindeutig völkerrechtswidrig.*
  This act is unambiguously contrary to international law.

## The **-widrig** pattern (worth knowing)

The suffix **-widrig** is highly productive in legal and official
German:

| Word | Meaning |
|-------|----------|
| *gesetzwidrig* | unlawful, illegal |
| *verfassungswidrig* | unconstitutional |
| *vertragswidrig* | in breach of contract |
| *rechtswidrig* | unlawful, wrongful |
| *sittenwidrig* | contrary to public policy / good morals (legal) |
| *artgerecht ↔ artwidrig* | suited to ↔ against a species’ nature |
| *zweckwidrig* | contrary to the intended purpose |

The opposite is built with **-gemäß** or **-konform**:
*völkerrechtskonform* — in conformity with international law,
*gesetzeskonform* — law-compliant.

## How to remember

Read **widrig** as English **“-breaching”** or **“anti-”**, and hear
*wider* “against” inside it. Then any *X-widrig* unpacks
mechanically:

- *gesetzwidrig* → against the law → unlawful
- *verfassungswidrig* → against the constitution → unconstitutional
- *völkerrechtswidrig* → against the law of nations → contrary to
  international law

Same trick for the opposite: *-konform* = “in line with”. German
builds in one word what English needs a whole clause for — which is
exactly why the word looks frightening and behaves so predictably.
