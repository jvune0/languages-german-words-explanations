# erheblich — considerable, substantial, significant

A written, businesslike adjective (and adverb). Not an emotional
"huge", but a sober statement that **the amount is one you have to
reckon with**. At home in the press, in administration and in court;
in speech Germans reach for *ziemlich*, *viel* or *deutlich* instead.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Part of speech | adjective; also an **intensifying adverb** |
| Comparison | *erheblich · erheblicher · am erheblichsten* |
| In practice | the superlative is barely ever used |
| Attributive | *ein erheblicher Schaden* — considerable damage |
| Predicative | *Der Schaden ist erheblich.* |
| As adverb | only with **comparatives**, verbs and participles |
| Negated form | ***unerheblich*** — negligible; ⚖️ immaterial |
| Litotes | *nicht unerheblich* — "far from small" (very common) |
| Noun | *die Erheblichkeit* — ⚖️ materiality, relevance |

⚠️ **Adverb trap:** *erheblich* does not modify a plain positive
adjective. ✗ *erheblich groß* — ✅ *erheblich größer* (considerably
bigger), ✅ *erheblich beschädigt* (badly damaged).

## Main meanings

### 1. Considerable, substantial (of size, damage, cost)

> *Der Sturm hat erheblichen Schaden angerichtet.*
> The storm caused considerable damage.

> *Das verursacht erhebliche Kosten.*
> That entails substantial costs.

> *Die Sanierung erfordert einen erheblichen Aufwand.*
> The refurbishment requires a considerable outlay of effort.

> *Es bestehen erhebliche Zweifel an seiner Darstellung.*
> There are serious doubts about his account.

### 2. Adverb: considerably, significantly, a great deal

Only with comparatives, verbs and participles.

> *Die Preise sind erheblich gestiegen.*
> Prices have risen significantly.

> *Er fährt erheblich schneller als erlaubt.*
> He drives considerably faster than permitted.

> *Das Fahrzeug wurde erheblich beschädigt.*
> The vehicle was badly damaged.

> *Die Frist wurde erheblich überschritten.*
> The deadline was overrun by a wide margin.

### 3. ⚖️ Legal: material, relevant to the case

Here *erheblich* is not about size but about **relevance** — what
carries weight in the decision. English says **material**.

> *Dieser Umstand ist für die Entscheidung nicht erheblich.*
> This circumstance is not material to the decision.

> *Der Vortrag ist unerheblich und wird zurückgewiesen.*
> The submission is immaterial and is rejected.

Hence **entscheidungserheblich** — "material to the outcome" — and
**die Erheblichkeit**, the materiality of a submission.

### 4. The litotes *nicht unerheblich*

A double negative used to say "quite substantial" without sounding
like boasting. A favourite of reports and journalism.

> *Er war nicht unerheblich am Erfolg beteiligt.*
> He played a far from negligible part in the success.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**er-** + **heb-** + **-lich**

- **heben** — "to lift, raise"; Gmc *\*hafjaną*. The direct English
  cognate is **to heave** — and, from the same root, **heavy**:
  OE *hefig* meant literally "needing to be heaved", i.e. weighty.
- **er-** — the prefix of upward motion and result: *erheben*
  (raise, elevate), *erwachen* (awaken), *erstehen* (arise).
- **-lich** — the suffix of capacity or quality (cf. English *-ly*,
  *-able*): "such that it can be lifted / that rises up".

Literally: **"liftable, able to be put on the scales"** — something
with weight, something that stands out from the background. From old
chancery usage — an argument worth *raising* in court — comes the
legal sense "relevant to the matter at hand".

🔑 **The English key:** *erheblich* is built exactly like the English
pair *heave* → *heavy*. What matters is what weighs; German just
kept the verb visible inside the adjective. Compare English
**weighty** and **momentous** (Latin *momentum* ← *movēre*): every
language measures significance in mass.

⚠️ **Do not confuse it with *erhaben***, the old past participle of
the same *erheben*. That one means "lofty, sublime" (*erhabene
Stille* — sublime silence), or "raised, embossed" in technical use.
One root, two storeys: *erheblich* is about quantity, *erhaben*
about grandeur.

## Distinctions from close words

| Word | Sense | Flavour |
|------|-------|---------|
| **erheblich** | considerable, weighty | 💼 formal, neutral |
| **beträchtlich** | sizeable, of quantity | 💼 more countable |
| **bedeutend** | significant in importance | 📰 also of people |
| **wesentlich** | essential, substantial | ✅ also intensifier |
| **deutlich** | marked, clearly measurable | ✅ plain |
| **spürbar / merklich** | noticeable, perceptible | 💬 softer |
| **gravierend** | grave, serious | ⚠️ bad things only |
| **massiv** | massive, heavy-handed | 📰 emphatic |
| **enorm / gewaltig** | enormous | 💬 emotional |

**Key pitfalls:**

- ⚠️ *erheblich* is neutral in polarity: you get *erheblicher
  Schaden* and *erhebliche Verbesserung* alike. *Gravierend* only
  attaches to bad news.
- ⚠️ *wesentlich* also intensifies (*wesentlich besser*), but it
  additionally means "essential, in essence": *im Wesentlichen* —
  "basically". *Erheblich* has no such sense.
- ⚠️ In conversation *erheblich* sounds like a form being filled in.
  Say *viel besser*, *deutlich mehr*, *echt teuer* instead.
- ⚠️ English *considerable* and German *erheblich* match well, but
  the German word leans further towards damage, defects and costs —
  it is a risk-assessor's word.

| Sphere | Usage |
|--------|-------|
| ⚖️ Law, courts | ✅ a term of art (*entscheidungserheblich*) |
| 🏛️ Administration, reports | ✅ its native habitat |
| 💼 Business, economics | ✅ (*erhebliche Einbußen*) |
| 📰 Press | ✅ very frequent |
| 🩺 Medicine, engineering | ✅ (*erheblich eingeschränkt*) |

| 🎭 Elevated style | ➖ a sober word, not a grand one |

## Typical collocations

- *erheblicher Schaden / Mangel / Aufwand / Nachteil*
- *erhebliche Kosten / Zweifel / Bedenken / Einbußen*
- *in erheblichem Maße / Umfang* — to a considerable extent
- *erheblich beeinträchtigen / einschränken* — to impair severely
- *erheblich steigen / sinken / abweichen* — to rise, fall or
  deviate significantly
- *erheblich mehr / weniger / besser / teurer*
- *unter erheblichem Alkoholeinfluss* — 🚔 heavily under the
  influence (police-report boilerplate)
- *nicht unerheblich* — far from negligible (litotes)
- *für die Entscheidung (un)erheblich* — ⚖️ (im)material

## How to remember

Split it: **er-heb-lich** = "**liftable**". The stem *heben* is
English **heave**, and from that same root English got **heavy** —
the heavy thing is the thing you must heave. So *erheblich* is what
**has weight**: it tips the scales, and therefore it goes into the
record.

Picture a clerk laying a fact on a balance. The needle moves —
*erheblich*, write it down. It doesn't — *unerheblich*, strike it
out. That is also how the courtroom sense works: "material" simply
means "heavy enough to count".

And remember the grammatical catch: as an adverb *erheblich* only
boosts a **comparative** — *erheblich besser*, "considerably
better". You can only lift what has already begun to move.
