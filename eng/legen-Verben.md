# -legen (überlegen · verlegen · anlegen · belegen …) — “to lay”

> The twin nest to [stellen-Verben](stellen-Verben.md): there things are
> stood upright, here they are laid flat. And the same second logic applies:
> nearly every figurative sense is “**to put something down so that it stays
> put**” — lay a document under a claim (*belegen* = to substantiate), lay
> it out before a listener (*darlegen* = to set out), turn it over in your
> head (*überlegen* = to consider).

## Grammar of the base verb

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Type | **weak**: *legen – legte – gelegt* |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Government | *etw.* (acc.) + where to: *auf / in / unter / an* + **acc.** |
| Reflexive | *sich legen* — to lie down; to subside |
| Participle | *ge-leg-t*; inseparables drop *ge-*: *verlegt*, *belegt* |

*legen* is the **causative** of *liegen*: “to make something lie”. English
has exactly the same pair — **lay** beside **lie** — and confuses them just
as thoroughly. The full positional quartet is in the
[stellen-Verben](stellen-Verben.md) card.

> *Ich lege das Buch **auf den** Tisch.* — I lay the book on the table.
> (acc.)
> *Das Buch liegt **auf dem** Tisch.* — The book lies on the table. (dat.)

The base verb’s own senses:

> *Die Henne legt Eier.* — The hen lays eggs.
> *Der Sturm hat sich gelegt.* — The storm has died down. (*sich legen*)
> *Sie legt großen Wert auf Pünktlichkeit.* — She sets great store by
> punctuality. (*Wert legen auf* + acc.)

## The key: lay it down and leave it there

The root \*legh- gave *liegen*, *legen*, *die Lage* (position), *das Lager*
(store, camp, bearing) and English **lie / lay / law**. The formula:

> **prefix + legen = “lay it where the prefix points — and leave it there”.**

The abstract senses grow straight out of that, and English takes the very
same road:

| German | English | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *auslegen* | *to lay out* | to spread out → to interpret |
| *festlegen* | *to lay down* | to fix (a rule) |
| *darlegen* | *to set out* | to expound |
| *ablegen* | *to lay aside* | to take off, to put away |
| *überlegen* | *to turn over* (in one’s mind) | to consider |

## The main verbs

### 1. *überlegen* — to consider (and “superior”)

Government: *sich* (**dat.**) *etw. überlegen*. The reflexive pronoun goes
in the dative — the same trap as *sich vorstellen*.

> *Ich muss es mir noch überlegen.* — I still have to think it over.
> *Überleg dir das gut!* — Think it over carefully!
> *Ohne zu überlegen, sagte er ja.* — Without thinking, he said yes.

⚠️ The identical-looking **adjective** *überlegen* means “superior”:

> *Er ist mir körperlich überlegen.* — He is physically stronger than me.
> *ein überlegener Sieg* — a commanding victory.
> Its antonym is *unterlegen* (inferior): *Wir waren klar unterlegen.*

One image underlies both: what lies **on top** — in the mind (you turn the
matter over) and in strength (you came out above).

### 2. *verlegen* — four verbs and one adjective

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to mislay | *Ich habe meine Brille verlegt.* |
| to postpone, to move (a date or venue) | *Das Treffen wurde verlegt.* |
| to lay (cable, tiles) | *Wir lassen Fliesen verlegen.* |
| to publish (books) | *Der Roman wird in München verlegt.* |

Hence *der Verlag* — a publishing house, and *der Verleger* — a publisher.

⚠️ The adjective *verlegen* means “embarrassed, at a loss”:

> *Er lächelte verlegen.* — He smiled sheepishly.
> *Ich bin nie um eine Antwort verlegen.* — I am never at a loss for an
> answer.
> *die Verlegenheit* — embarrassment; an awkward spot.

The logic matches English *mislaid*: someone who has put themselves down in
the wrong place and cannot find what they need.

### 3. *anlegen* — to apply; to invest; to lay out; to dock

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to apply, to put on | *einen Verband anlegen* |
| to invest money | *Geld in Aktien anlegen* |
| to lay out (a garden, a database) | *einen Garten anlegen* |
| to dock, to berth | *Das Schiff legt in Kiel an.* |
| *es auf etw. anlegen* | *Er legt es auf Streit an.* — He’s asking for a fight. |
| *sich mit jdm. anlegen* | *Leg dich nicht mit ihm an!* — Don’t take him on! |

*die Anlage* — a plant or facility; an investment; an enclosure to a letter;
and natural aptitude (*musikalische Anlagen*).

### 4. *belegen* — to occupy; to substantiate; to enrol

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to occupy (a seat) | *Der Platz ist belegt.* |
| to back up with evidence | *Die These ist gut belegt.* |
| to enrol in a course | *Ich belege einen Spanischkurs.* |
| to take a place in a ranking | *Sie belegte den zweiten Platz.* |

*der Beleg* is both a receipt and a **piece of documentary evidence**;
*belegtes Brot* is an open sandwich (literally “covered bread”), and *der
Belag* is a covering, a coating, or a filling.

### 5. *ablegen* — to take off; to sit (an exam); to give up; to cast off

> *Legen Sie bitte ab!* — Do take your coat off!
> *Er hat die Prüfung abgelegt.* — He sat the exam.
> *Sie legte ein Geständnis ab.* — She made a confession.
> *eine schlechte Gewohnheit ablegen* — to break a bad habit.
> *einen Eid ablegen* — to swear an oath.

### 6. *einlegen* — to insert; to pickle; to lodge (an appeal)

> *eingelegte Gurken* — pickled gherkins.
> *Wir legen eine Pause ein.* — Let’s take a break.
> *Gegen das Urteil wurde Berufung eingelegt.* — An appeal was lodged
> against the verdict.
> *Kannst du ein gutes Wort für mich einlegen?* — Could you put in a good
> word for me?

### 7. *auslegen* — to display; to interpret; to pay on someone’s behalf

> *Die Ware liegt im Schaufenster aus.* — The goods are on display.
> *Wie ist dieser Paragraph auszulegen?* — How is this section to be
> interpreted?
> *Kannst du mir zehn Euro auslegen?* — Could you front me ten euros?

*die Auslegung* — interpretation (legal, religious); *die Auslage* — a shop
window display.

### 8. *darlegen* and *offenlegen* — to expound and to disclose

> *Er legte seine Gründe ausführlich dar.* — He set out his reasons in
> detail.
> *Die Firma muss ihre Finanzen offenlegen.* — The company must disclose
> its finances.

### 9. *vorlegen* — to present, to produce

> *Bitte legen Sie Ihren Ausweis vor.* — Please produce your ID.
> *Die Kommission legte einen Bericht vor.* — The commission presented a
> report.

*die Vorlage* — a template, a bill before parliament; in football, an assist.

### 10. *nahelegen* and *widerlegen*

> *Ich lege Ihnen dringend nahe, einen Anwalt zu nehmen.*
> I strongly urge you to get a lawyer. (a gentle-sounding but forceful
> formula)
> *Die Studie widerlegt diese Annahme.* — The study refutes this assumption.
> (*widerlegen* is inseparable: *widerlegt*)

### 11. Other useful members

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *zulegen* | to put on (weight, pace) | *Er hat 5 Kilo zugelegt.* |
| *sich etw. zulegen* | to get oneself something | *Ich habe mir ein Auto zugelegt.* |
| *beilegen* | to enclose; to settle (a dispute) | *einen Streit beilegen* |
| *umlegen* | to throw on; to flip; to apportion | *den Hebel umlegen* |
| *nachlegen* | to add more (wood, effort) | *Holz nachlegen* |
| *zerlegen* | to take apart, to dismantle | *den Motor zerlegen* |
| *hinterlegen* | to deposit, to lodge (a bond) | *eine Kaution hinterlegen* |
| *auferlegen* | to impose (a duty) | *jdm. eine Strafe auferlegen* |
| *stilllegen* | to shut down (a plant) | *ein Werk stilllegen* |
| *lahmlegen* | to paralyse | *Der Streik legte die Stadt lahm.* |
| *trockenlegen* | to drain; to change a nappy | *ein Moor trockenlegen* |
| *festlegen* | to fix, to determine | 📎 [fest-Verben](fest-Verben.md) |

## How not to confuse them

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| *legen* / *liegen* | to lay (acc.) / to lie (dat.) |
| *überlegen* (verb) / *überlegen* (adj.) | to consider / superior |
| *überlegen* / *unterlegen* (adj.) | superior / inferior |
| *verlegen* (verb) / *verlegen* (adj.) | to mislay / embarrassed |
| *belegen* / *beweisen* | to document / to prove by argument |
| *darlegen* / *erklären* | to set out (structured) / to explain |
| *ablegen* / *ausziehen* | to take off (a coat, formal) / to undress |
| *anlegen* / *investieren* | to invest (native) / to invest (loanword) |

## Typical contexts

- **Office and bureaucracy:** *Unterlagen vorlegen*, *Belege einreichen*,
  *Widerspruch einlegen*, *einen Termin verlegen*.
- **Finance:** *Geld anlegen*, *die Geldanlage*, *Kaution hinterlegen*.
- **Research and law:** *eine These belegen*, *ein Gesetz auslegen*, *eine
  Behauptung widerlegen*.
- **Everyday:** *Holz nachlegen*, *sich hinlegen*, *eingelegte Gurken*,
  *Fliesen verlegen*.
- **Speech:** *Überleg’s dir!*, *Leg dich nicht mit ihm an!*, *Der Sturm hat
  sich gelegt.*

## How to remember

*legen* means “make it lie” — the causative of *liegen*, exactly as English
*lay* stands to *lie*. The whole nest is about **where you lay it and why it
stays there**:

- **über** on top → **turn it over in your mind** = consider; and whatever
  lies on top is **superior**;
- **ver** astray / for good → **mislay**, **postpone**, **lay cable**,
  **publish**;
- **an** apply → **put on, invest, lay out, dock**;
- **be** cover the object → **occupy a seat**, **back up with evidence**;
- **ab** off → **take off**, **sit an exam**, **cast off**;
- **ein** in → **insert**, **pickle**, **lodge an appeal**;
- **aus** spread out → **display** and **interpret**;
- **dar** before the listener → **expound**;
- **nahe** close to → **urge strongly**;
- **wider** against → **refute**.

One sentence with the core of the family: *Ich habe **es mir überlegt**, den
Termin **verlegt**, die Unterlagen **vorgelegt** und alles mit Zahlen
**belegt**.* — “I thought it over, moved the appointment, submitted the
paperwork and backed it all up with figures.”
