# -machen (anmachen · ausmachen · zumachen · aufmachen …) — “make/do” with prefixes

> *machen* is the emptiest verb in German: on its own it means almost
> nothing, and the prefix does all the work. Two consequences follow. First,
> **the prefix is the meaning** — exactly as in English *turn on / shut up /
> make out*. Second, nearly every one of these verbs has a **neutral twin**
> from the written register (*zumachen* → *schließen*), which is why the
> whole family belongs to spoken German 💬.

## Grammar shared by the family

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| The base verb | **weak**: *machen – machte – gemacht* |
| Participle | always in *-t*: *auf-ge-macht* 📎 [Partizip II](Partizip_II.md) |
| Auxiliary | always *haben* |
| Prefixes | nearly all **separable** and stressed: *ÁUFmachen* |
| The exception | *vermachen* (to bequeath) — inseparable, no *ge-* |
| Spelling | *kaputtmachen*, *saubermachen* are written as one word |

> *Mach bitte das Fenster **auf**.* — Please open the window.
> *Er hat das Licht **an**gemacht.* — He turned the light on.

## The key: kneading clay

*machen* goes back to Proto-Germanic \*makōn “to knead, to shape” —
originally of the clay you build a wall or a pot from. Hence its direct
kinship with English **make**, and with Greek *massein* “to knead” (→
*Masse*). The sense “give shape to anything at all” wore down over the
centuries into a bare “do” — leaving a universal blank that needs a prefix
in order to mean something.

⚠️ A false connection: ***die Macht*** “power” is **not** from *machen* but
from *mögen* “to be able” (like English *may – might*). The resemblance is
accidental.

> **machen is the clay. The prefix is the shape you press into it.**

## The main verbs

### 1. *aufmachen* — to open

> *Mach die Tür auf!* — Open the door!
> *Sie hat ein kleines Café aufgemacht.* — She has opened a little café.
> *Wir machten uns früh auf.* — We set off early. (*sich aufmachen*)

📰 *der Aufmacher* — the lead story, the front-page splash.

### 2. *zumachen* — to close, to shut

> *Mach bitte die Augen zu.* — Please close your eyes.
> *Der Laden macht um acht zu.* — The shop closes at eight.
> *Die Firma hat dichtgemacht.* — The firm has shut up shop. (*dichtmachen*)

### 3. *anmachen* — to switch on; to dress (salad); to light; to hit on

Five live senses:

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| switch on (light, device) | *Mach das Radio an!* |
| light (a fire) | *Feuer anmachen* |
| dress (a salad) | *den Salat mit Öl anmachen* |
| colloq. to chat up, to hit on | *Er hat sie in der Bar angemacht.* |
| colloq. to turn someone on | *Diese Musik macht mich an.* |

⚠️ Because of those last two, *anmachen* applied to a person is almost
always suggestive — for “to speak to someone” use *ansprechen*.

### 4. *ausmachen* — the most polysemous of them all

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| to switch off, to put out | *Mach das Licht aus!* |
| to agree, to arrange | *Wir haben einen Termin ausgemacht.* |
| to amount to, to constitute | *Das macht 20 Euro aus.* |
| to matter, to be a bother | *Macht es Ihnen etwas aus, wenn ich rauche?* |
| to make out, to spot | *In der Ferne war ein Schiff auszumachen.* |
| to be the essence of | *Das macht seinen Charme aus.* |

Two formulas are worth learning whole:

> *Macht es dir etwas aus, wenn …?* — Do you mind if …?
> *Das macht nichts aus.* / *Das macht nichts.* — It doesn’t matter.

Note that English *make out* covers three of these senses too — “spot”,
“manage”, “work out” — which is a useful hook, not a coincidence.

### 5. *abmachen* — to take off; to agree

> *Mach das Preisschild ab.* — Take the price tag off.
> *Wir haben abgemacht, uns um sechs zu treffen.* — We agreed to meet at six.
> ***Abgemacht!*** — Agreed! It’s a deal!

*die Abmachung* — an arrangement (less formal than *die Vereinbarung*).

### 6. *mitmachen* — to join in; to live through

> *Machst du mit?* — Are you joining in?
> *Sie hat im Krieg viel mitgemacht.* — She went through a lot in the war.

### 7. *nachmachen* — to copy, to imitate, to counterfeit

> *Der Kleine macht alles nach.* — The little one copies everything.
> *nachgemachte Markenware* — counterfeit branded goods.

### 8. *vormachen* — to demonstrate; to fool

> *Machen Sie es mir bitte einmal vor.* — Please show me how it’s done.
> *Mir kannst du nichts vormachen!* — You can’t fool me!
> *Er macht sich selbst etwas vor.* — He is kidding himself.

### 9. *durchmachen* — to go through; to stay up all night

> *Sie hat eine schwere Zeit durchgemacht.* — She went through a hard time.
> *Wir haben die Nacht durchgemacht.* — We made a night of it.

### 10. *weitermachen* — to carry on

> *Mach weiter so!* — Keep it up!
> *Nach der Pause machen wir weiter.* — We’ll carry on after the break.

### 11. Compounds with an adjective

| Verb | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *kaputtmachen* | to break, to ruin | *Du machst alles kaputt!* |
| *saubermachen* | to clean | *die Wohnung saubermachen* |
| *klarmachen* | to make clear, to get across | *Mach ihm das klar!* |
| *fertigmachen* | to get ready; 💬 to crush | *Der Chef hat ihn fertiggemacht.* |
| *wiedergutmachen* | to make amends for | *einen Fehler wiedergutmachen* |
| *wettmachen* | to make up for, to offset | *den Rückstand wettmachen* |
| *einmachen* | to preserve, to bottle | *Marmelade einmachen* |

### 12. *vermachen* — the one inseparable member

> *Er hat sein Haus dem Neffen vermacht.* — He left his house to his nephew.

Participle without *ge-*: *vermacht*. 📎 Why: [Präfixe](Präfixe.md).

## Spoken versus written

The family’s main practical value: every member has a neutral or bookish
twin. Formal writing takes the right-hand column.

| 💬 Colloquial | 📰 Neutral / written |
|---|---|
| *aufmachen* | *öffnen* |
| *zumachen* | *schließen* |
| *anmachen* | *einschalten*, *anzünden* |
| *ausmachen* (a device) | *ausschalten*, *löschen* |
| *ausmachen* (a meeting) | *vereinbaren* |
| *abmachen* (to detach) | *entfernen*, *abnehmen* |
| *kaputtmachen* | *zerstören*, *beschädigen* |
| *saubermachen* | *reinigen* |
| *fertigmachen* | *vorbereiten*, *fertigstellen* |
| *weitermachen* | *fortsetzen*, *fortfahren* |
| *nachmachen* | *imitieren*, *fälschen* |

> *Bitte schließen Sie die Tür.* (a notice) vs *Mach die Tür zu!* (at home)

## Set phrases with *machen*

| Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Mach’s gut!* | Take care! So long! |
| *Mach schon!* | Come on! Get a move on! |
| *Das macht nichts.* | It doesn’t matter. |
| *Das macht 20 Euro.* | That’ll be 20 euros. |
| *Was macht die Arbeit?* | How’s work going? |
| *sich Sorgen / Gedanken machen* | to worry / to ponder |
| *Mach dir nichts draus!* | Don’t let it get to you! |
| *Schluss machen* | to finish; ⚠️ to break up with someone |
| *Feierabend machen* | to knock off for the day |
| *blaumachen* | 💬 to skive off work or school |
| *sich auf den Weg machen* | to set off |
| *Das macht sich gut.* | That looks good, that works well. |

## How to remember

*machen* is **clay**: a shapeless mass in itself (the root meant “to
knead”), given form by the prefix. So learn the prefix, not the verb — the
prefix is where the meaning lives:

- **auf** up, open → **to open**;
- **zu** shut, to → **to close**;
- **an** on, attached → **to switch on, light, dress a salad**;
- **aus** out, to the end → **to switch off; to arrange; to amount to**;
- **ab** off → **to take off; to shake on a deal**;
- **mit** along → **to join in**;
- **nach** after → **to copy someone**;
- **vor** before → **to demonstrate; to pull the wool over eyes**;
- **durch** through → **to go through, to endure**.

One sentence with most of the family: *Mach das Licht **an**, das Fenster
**zu**, den Fernseher **aus** — und dann **machen** wir einen Termin
**aus**.* — “Turn the light on, shut the window, turn the TV off — and then
let’s fix a date.”
