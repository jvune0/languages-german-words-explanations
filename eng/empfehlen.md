# empfehlen — to recommend, to advise

A strong verb with an inseparable prefix; neutral and very common,
covering everything from a casual "you should try it" to a formal
"it is advisable". Hidden inside is an ancient root meaning
"**to entrust**": recommending is not just advising — it is
**handing something over into another person's trust**.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Type | strong; prefix **inseparable**, unstressed |
| Principal parts | *empfiehlt · empfahl · hat empfohlen* |
| Participle II | *empfohlen* — **no *ge-*** |
| Subjunctive II | *empföhle* (📚) / *empfähle* (rarer) |
| *zu*-infinitive | *zu empfehlen* (never *empzufehlen*) |
| Stem change *e→ie* | *du empfiehlst*, *er empfiehlt* |
| Imperative | *Empfiehl!* (with *ie*, no *-e*) |
| Government | *jdm.* (dat.) *etw.* (acc.) *empfehlen* |
| With infinitive | *jdm. empfehlen, etw. zu tun* |
| Reflexive | *sich empfehlen* — to be advisable; to take one's leave |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |

**Conjugation trap:** the *e → ie* shift happens only in the 2nd and
3rd person singular: *ich empfehle, du empfiehlst, er empfiehlt*, but
*wir empfehlen*. Exactly like *befehlen*, *stehlen*, *sehen*.

## Main meanings

### 1. To recommend something to someone

The basic frame: **to whom** (dative) + **what** (accusative) — the
same double object English uses in *recommend me a book*.

> *Ich kann Ihnen dieses Restaurant wärmstens empfehlen.*
> I can warmly recommend this restaurant to you.

> *Was empfiehlst du mir?*
> What do you recommend?

> *Der Arzt hat mir mehr Bewegung empfohlen.*
> The doctor recommended more exercise.

### 2. To advise doing something (+ infinitive clause)

> *Ich empfehle Ihnen, früh zu buchen.*
> I advise you to book early.

> *Es wird empfohlen, den Impfschutz aufzufrischen.*
> It is recommended to renew your vaccination. (📰 impersonal)

> *Man empfahl uns, den Zug zu nehmen.*
> We were advised to take the train.

### 3. To recommend a person (for a post, to someone)

Here the original sense — "to entrust someone to someone" — is
literal.

> *Er hat mich für die Stelle empfohlen.*
> He recommended me for the position.

> *Können Sie mir einen guten Anwalt empfehlen?*
> Can you recommend me a good lawyer?

> *Sie wurde mir von einer Kollegin empfohlen.*
> She was recommended to me by a colleague.

### 4. *es empfiehlt sich* — it is advisable

An impersonal formula of formal register, equivalent to *es ist
ratsam*. The subject is *es*, never a person.

> *Es empfiehlt sich, die Unterlagen vorab zu prüfen.*
> It is advisable to check the documents beforehand.

> *In diesem Fall empfiehlt sich Vorsicht.*
> In this case caution is advisable.

### 5. *sich empfehlen* — to take one's leave (📜 dated)

An old-fashioned parting formula: "I commend myself to your favour" —
and English *to commend oneself* did exactly the same job.

> *Ich empfehle mich!*
> I shall take my leave!

> *Er empfahl sich und verließ den Saal.*
> He took his leave and left the hall.

In 19th-century letters: *Ich empfehle mich Ihnen bestens* — "I remain
yours most respectfully". Today it survives mainly as a joke.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**emp-** (= *ent-*) + ***felhan*** "to entrust, conceal"

- **emp-** is the phonetic variant of the prefix **ent-**, from
  Germanic ***and-*** "against, towards" — the same element as in
  **Ant**wort (= English **answer**). Not "un-, de-" here, but the
  archaic "towards, in the other's direction". For the sound change
  *ent-f- → emp-f-*, see the card on [emp-Verben](emp-Verben.md).
- The root is **not** modern *fehlen* "to be missing"! That one came
  from Old French *faillir* (English *fail*) and is unrelated. This is
  ancient Germanic ***felhan*** "to entrust, commit, hide", Old High
  German *bifelhan*.

Literally: **"to entrust (someone/something) towards another"**.

The same root survives in **be**fehlen "to command" and in archaic
*anbefehlen* "to commit to someone's care". Its oldest sense, "to
hide, to put away in the ground", also yielded "to bury" — visible in
Old English *befeolan* "to commit, consign".

**English parallel.** *Recommend* is built symmetrically, only from
Latin: *re-* (intensive) + *commendare* "to entrust" (← *manus*
"hand") = "to hand over into someone's care". So *commend* and
*empfehlen* encode the identical gesture from different bricks —
and English *commend* still carries both halves of the old meaning,
"to praise" and "to entrust": *I commend my soul to God*.

## Watch out: empfehlen vs. befehlen

One root ***felhan*** "to entrust", two prefixes — and utterly
different force. The forms differ by literally **one letter in the
prefix**, a classic listening trap:

| Verb | Forms | Meaning | Force |
|------|-------|---------|-------|
| **empfehlen** | *empfiehlt · empfahl · empfohlen* | to recommend | advice |
| **befehlen** | *befiehlt · befahl · befohlen* | to command | order |

The prefix logic: **be-** makes the act binding and lands it on its
object (an order falls on the person who must obey); **emp-** keeps it
a gesture held out towards you — take the advice or leave it.

> *Der Arzt empfahl Ruhe.* — The doctor recommended rest.
> *Der Offizier befahl den Rückzug.* — The officer ordered the retreat.

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Register |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **empfehlen** | to recommend (with a personal "this is good") | neutral |
| **raten** (*jdm. zu etw.*) | to advise (in a given situation) | neutral |
| **vorschlagen** | to suggest, to propose (an option) | neutral |
| **nahelegen** | to urge, to strongly suggest | 💼 |
| **anraten** | to advise (📜 formal, bookish) | 📰 |
| **befürworten** | to endorse, to speak in favour of | 💼 |
| **loben** | to praise (without advising anyone to take it) | neutral |

**The *empfehlen* / *raten* split:**

- *raten* normally takes a preposition or a clause: *Ich rate dir
  **zu** diesem Kurs*, *Ich rate dir, das nicht zu tun.* It is about
  a **course of action**, and it often warns off (*abraten*).
- *empfehlen* is about an **object** you judge positively: a book,
  a doctor, a restaurant, a person.

⚠️ Only *raten* has a built-in negative twin: **abraten** "to advise
against" (*Ich rate dir davon ab*). *Empfehlen* has none — German
says *Ich kann es nicht empfehlen*.

| Setting | Usage |
|---------|-------|

| 🍽️ Service, dining | ✅ a fixture (*Ich empfehle den Fisch*) |

## Typical collocations

- *jdm. etw. wärmstens / dringend / uneingeschränkt empfehlen*
- *Ich kann es nur empfehlen!* — I can only recommend it!
- *Das Buch sei dir empfohlen.* (📚 bookish, with subjunctive)
- *es empfiehlt sich, … zu tun* — it is advisable to …
- *auf Empfehlung von jdm.* — on someone's recommendation
- *die empfohlene Tagesdosis* — the recommended daily dose
- *der empfohlene Verkaufspreis* (UVP) — the recommended retail price
- *ein Empfehlungsschreiben ausstellen* — to issue a reference letter
- *sehr empfehlenswert* — highly recommended

## How to remember

Look inside: **emp- ("towards") + felhan ("entrust")** = **"entrust
towards"**. When you recommend something you are literally **handing
it over** — the very same gesture English packed into *commend* /
*recommend*, from Latin *manus*, "hand".

The twin to keep straight: **emp**fehlen — I **hold out** advice;
**be**fehlen — I **lay down** an order. One prefix is soft, the other
hard; and *empfahl* ≠ *befahl*, whatever your ear says.

One more pair: *empfing* (he caught, ← *fangen*) vs. *empfahl* (he
advised). Both start in *emp-*, but they catch different things — see
[empfangen](empfangen.md).
