# antreten — to take up, set out, line up (a many-sided verb)

One of those German verbs whose meaning depends almost entirely on
its object and context. The core idea is **“step up to something”** —
but English needs a different verb almost every time.

## Main meanings

### 1. To take up / set out on (a job, an office, a journey)

> **etwas antreten** — to formally begin something significant

- *eine neue Stelle antreten* — to take up a new job
- *eine Reise antreten* — to set out on a journey
- *den Dienst antreten* — to report for duty, start a shift
- *ein Erbe antreten* — to come into an inheritance
- *eine Strafe antreten* — to begin serving a sentence

> *Er tritt seine neue Stelle am 1. März an.*
> He takes up his new job on 1 March.

Note the register: English “take up a post” and “set out on a
journey” are exactly as slightly formal as the German.

### 2. To compete, to run (in a contest, an election)

> **gegen jemanden antreten** — to go up against someone

- *gegen den Titelverteidiger antreten* — to face the defending champion
- *bei der Wahl antreten* — to stand (BrE) / run (AmE) for election
- *für Deutschland antreten* — to compete for Germany

> *Sie tritt morgen im Finale an.*
> She competes in the final tomorrow.

### 3. To line up, fall in (military / sports)

- *Die Soldaten traten an.* — The soldiers fell in.
- *Antreten!* — Fall in! (command)

### 4. To kick-start, to tamp down (physical, less common)

- *ein Motorrad antreten* — to kick-start a motorbike
- *die Erde antreten* — to tamp the soil down with your foot

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|----------|----------|
| Type | strong, separable |
| Forms | *tritt an — trat an — ist/hat angetreten* |
| Auxiliary | **sein** (movement, beginning) or **haben** (tamping) |

Present tense: *ich trete an, du trittst an, er tritt an*.

## How to remember

The root is **treten** “to tread, to step” — the same word as English
**tread** — and the prefix **an-** means “towards, up to”. So the verb
is literally “**to step up to something**”, and context decides what:
up to a new post, up to an opponent, up into formation, or down onto
the earth with your boot.

Handy English mirror: “to step up” covers most of it — you step up to
a job, step up to a rival, step into line.

## Derived noun

**der Antritt** — the taking up, the start, the entry

- *Dienstantritt* — start of duty, first day on the job
- *Amtsantritt* — taking office (politics)
- *Reiseantritt* — departure, start of a journey
- *Antrittsrede* — inaugural address
