# “im Begriff sein” — to be about to (do something)

A set phrase meaning “**to be on the point of** doing something”,
“**to be just about to**”, “**to be on the verge of**”.

## Construction

> **im Begriff sein, etwas zu tun**

or with **gerade** for emphasis:

> **gerade im Begriff sein, etwas zu tun** — to be just about to

The comma before *zu* is optional in modern usage when the infinitive
clause is short: *Ich war im Begriff zu gehen.*

## Examples

- *Ich war gerade im Begriff zu gehen, als das Telefon klingelte.*
  I was just about to leave when the phone rang.
- *Sie ist im Begriff, eine wichtige Entscheidung zu treffen.*
  She is on the verge of making an important decision.
- *Das Unternehmen ist im Begriff, neue Märkte zu erschließen.*
  The company is about to open up new markets.

## Where the meaning comes from

**der Begriff** normally means “concept, notion”, but it comes from
the verb **begreifen** — “to grasp”, both literally and mentally
(compare English *to grasp an idea*). The older sense of *Begriff*
was “the act of grasping / getting hold of something, the setting
about it”.

So *im Begriff sein* is literally ≈ “**to be in the act of taking
hold**” → to be on the threshold of doing something. English keeps
the same metaphor in *to be in the grip of* and *to get a grip on*,
though it never developed the “about to” sense.

## Register

It sounds **slightly formal / bookish**. In everyday speech Germans
reach for something lighter:

| Register | Version |
|---------|---------|
| formal | *Ich bin im Begriff zu gehen.* |
| neutral | *Ich will gerade gehen.* |
| colloquial | *Ich wollte gerade gehen.* / *Ich bin grad am Gehen.* |

English has the same ladder: *on the point of departing* (formal) →
*about to leave* (neutral) → *just heading out* (colloquial). Match
the rung, not just the meaning.

## Similar expressions

- **dabei sein, etwas zu tun** — to be in the middle of doing
  something (more colloquial; note it leans to “in the act” rather
  than “about to”)
- **drauf und dran sein, etwas zu tun** — to be on the verge of doing
  something (colloquial, with a hint of impulse or resolve)
- **kurz davor sein, etwas zu tun** — to be close to doing something
- **im Begriff stehen** — a stiffer, older variant of the same phrase
