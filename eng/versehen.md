# versehen — to provide with; to slip up

Why does one verb mean both “**to supply**” and “**to make a
mistake**”? Both senses go back to **sehen** “to see”, but the prefix
**ver-** pulls in two different directions — a very typical story for
German verbs with this prefix.

## 1. *aus Versehen* — by mistake, inadvertently

Here **ver-** means “wrongly, off course” — as in:

- *sich verlaufen* — to lose one’s way
- *sich versprechen* — to misspeak, make a slip of the tongue
- *sich verschreiben* — to make a slip of the pen

So *sich versehen* is literally “to see wrong, to misperceive”.

Hence the noun **das Versehen** = an oversight, a slip. And *aus
Versehen* = “out of oversight”, i.e. accidentally, inadvertently.

> *Ich habe aus Versehen die falsche Datei gelöscht.*
> I deleted the wrong file by mistake.

English has exactly the same word-formation logic: *mis-* is our
*ver-*. Compare *misspeak*, *mistake*, and above all **oversight** —
which, like *Versehen*, is “failing to see” something.

## 2. *jemanden mit etwas versehen* — to provide, furnish, equip

Here **ver-** works differently: “to see something through, to attend
to it fully”. Compare English **to see to something** (“I’ll see to
it” = I’ll take care of it) — the very same metaphor with the very
same verb of seeing.

Examples:

- *jemanden mit Geld versehen* — to furnish someone with money
- *ein Amt versehen* — to hold / discharge an office (i.e. “see to
  it”)
- *ein Dokument mit einer Unterschrift versehen* — to append a
  signature to a document
- *versehen mit* (past participle) — “fitted with, bearing”:
  *ein mit Warnhinweisen versehenes Gerät* — a device bearing
  warning labels

## The thread that ties them together

The root is one and the same — *sehen*, “to see”. In one case it is
“**to overlook**” (→ a mistake), in the other “**to look after, to
see to**” (→ to provide).

The German prefix **ver-** is famous for exactly this double life:

| Sense | Example |
|----------|--------|
| “wrongly” | *verlernen* — to unlearn, forget how to |
| “completely, all the way” | *verbrauchen* — to use up |

English shows the same split with *over-*: *oversee* (supervise) vs
*overlook* (fail to notice).

## Mnemonic

Picture an eye. *sehen* is the eye; **ver-** either **turns it away**
(→ oversight, mistake) or **fixes it on something** (→ seeing to it,
providing for it).

One sentence to hold both: *Aus Versehen hat er das Formular mit der
falschen Unterschrift versehen.* — “By mistake he provided the form
with the wrong signature.”
