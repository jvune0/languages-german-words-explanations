# verlangen — to demand, to require; to long for

> Neutral to bookish, and a strong word: not "to ask" but **to
> demand as one's due**. In an elevated register it flips to
> "to yearn, to long for" (*es verlangt mich nach …*) — and that
> is no accident: it is the same root as English *long for*.

## Grammar

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | weak verb, **inseparable** prefix *ver-* |
| Principal parts | *verlangt — verlangte — hat verlangt* |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Government 1 | **etw. (acc.) von jdm.** — to demand sth. of sb. |
| Government 2 | **nach + dat.** — to long for, to call for |
| Government 3 | impersonal: *es verlangt mich nach + dat.* (📜) |
| With a clause | *verlangen, dass …* / *zu* + infinitive |
| Passive | *es wird verlangt, dass …* — "it is required that …" |
| Noun | *das Verlangen* — 1) demand; 2) craving, desire |
| Participial adj. | *verlangt* — required, requested |

⚠️ *ver-* is **inseparable** and takes **no *ge-*** in the past
participle: *hat verlangt*, never *ver**ge**langt*.

> *Er verlangte von mir eine schriftliche Erklärung.*
> He demanded a written explanation from me.

## Main meanings

### 1. To demand (as a right or a condition)

The core sense. The speaker feels **entitled**: by law, contract,
office, or plain justice.

> *Der Kunde verlangte sein Geld zurück.*
> The customer demanded his money back.

> *Man kann nicht von jedem verlangen, dass er alles versteht.*
> You can't expect everyone to understand everything.

> *Die Polizei verlangte seinen Ausweis.*
> The police demanded to see his ID.

> *Das verlangt viel Geduld.*
> That takes a great deal of patience.

### 2. To ask (a price), to charge 💶

> *Wie viel verlangen Sie für das Auto?*
> How much are you asking for the car?

> *Für diese Wohnung verlangt er 900 Euro Miete.*
> He's asking 900 euros' rent for this flat.

⚠️ English uses the soft verb *ask* here, but German cannot:
*bitten* is impossible — only *verlangen* (or *haben wollen*).

### 3. To ask for someone, to summon ☎️

> *Am Telefon wird Herr Meier verlangt.*
> Mr Meier is wanted on the phone.

> *Der Chef verlangt Sie sofort in sein Büro.*
> The boss wants you in his office immediately.

### 4. *nach etw. verlangen* — to long for, to crave 📜

With *nach* the verb changes both case and register: no longer a
demand made outward but a pull felt inward.

> *Sie verlangte nach Ruhe.*
> She longed for peace and quiet.

> *Das Kind verlangte nach seiner Mutter.*
> The child cried out for its mother.

> *Es verlangt mich nach der Heimat.* (📜)
> I am filled with longing for home.

### 5. *das Verlangen* — craving, desire

> *Sie spürte ein starkes Verlangen nach Freiheit.*
> She felt a strong craving for freedom.

> *auf Verlangen des Gerichts* (🏛)
> at the court's demand / on request of the court

## Word breakdown and etymology

| Part | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *ver-* | intensifying here: pushes the action to its limit |
| *langen* | "to reach, to reach for" (from *lang* "long") |

**Everything turns on *lang* "long".** The simple verb *langen*
means to reach out and get something: *Kannst du mir das Salz
langen?* — "Can you reach me the salt?" Put *ver-* in front and the
outstretched arm becomes an outstretched **soul**: to strain toward
what isn't there — hence both to yearn for it and to demand it.

**The English parallel is exact.** *To long for* comes from the very
same adjective *long*, and Middle English *me longeth* "I yearn" is
built precisely like the German impersonal *es verlangt mich*. Both
languages arrived at one metaphor: **desire is elongation, a
stretching of the self toward its object.**

**The semantic arc of the word:**

> reach (*langen*) → strain toward, yearn (*verlangen nach*)
> → press for → **demand** (*verlangen* + acc.)

## Synonyms and how they differ

| Word | Register | How it differs |
|---|---|---|
| *verlangen* | neutral/📰 | to demand as one's due; direct and strong |
| *fordern* | 📰🏛 | to demand **publicly**, to put forward a demand |
| *anfordern* | 🏛 | to requisition, request officially (files, backup) |
| *erfordern* | 📰 | "to require", with a **thing** as subject |
| *bitten (um)* | neutral | to **ask** politely, claiming no right |
| *beanspruchen* | 🏛 | to lay claim to something |
| *einfordern* | 🏛 | to call in what was already promised |
| *sich sehnen (nach)* | 📜🎭 | to yearn — feeling only, no demand |
| *begehren* | 📜🎭 | to covet, to desire (elevated, often sensual) |

**Pitfalls:**

- *verlangen* ≠ *bitten*. *Ich bitte Sie um Hilfe* is a polite
  request; *Ich verlange Ihre Hilfe* is close to an ultimatum.
- *erfordern* takes a **thing** as subject: *Die Aufgabe erfordert
  Geduld*, but *Er verlangt Geduld von uns*.
- Don't map *verlangen* onto English *require* in the soft,
  administrative sense — German *verlangen* keeps a sharp edge.
- *das Verlangen* can read as sexual craving; in business prose
  prefer *die Forderung*.

## Register and style

| Setting | Fit |
|---|---|
| 💬 Everyday speech | ✅ but it lands hard: "I demand" |
| 🏢 Business correspondence | ✅ *wir verlangen eine Erklärung* |
| 🏛 Legal prose | ✅ *auf Verlangen*, *das Gesetz verlangt* |
| 📰 Press | ✅ very frequent: *Opposition verlangt Rücktritt* |
| 🎭 Literature, lyric | ✅ in the *verlangen nach* sense |
| 📜 Archaic-elevated | ✅ *es verlangt mich nach …* |
| 🙏 Polite request | ❌ that is *bitten* territory |

## Typical contexts and collocations

- *Rechenschaft / eine Erklärung verlangen* — to demand an account
  / an explanation;
- *zu viel von jdm. verlangen* — to ask too much of someone;
- *einen hohen Preis verlangen* — to charge a steep price;
- *auf Verlangen* (🏛) — on demand, upon request;
- *das verlangt Mut / Zeit / Erfahrung* — that takes courage /
  time / experience;
- *nach Aufmerksamkeit verlangen* — to crave attention;
- *Was verlangst du eigentlich von mir?* — "What exactly do you
  want from me?"

## How to remember

**It all hangs on *lang* = *long*.** *langen* is to reach out an arm;
*ver-langen* is to reach so hard and so long that the reaching turns
into a claim — first "I ache for it", then "hand it over".

> The English cognate gives you the whole card in three words:
> **ver·LANG·en ↔ to LONG for.** Longing stretches you toward the
> thing; convince yourself it is yours by right, and that longing
> hardens into a **demand**.
