# bedauerlich — "regrettable, unfortunate"

> The adjective to the verb *bedauern* "to regret". It does not mean
> "sad" but **"to be regretted"**: it judges facts, incidents and
> mistakes, not moods. Register runs from ordinary written German to
> full diplomatic damage control — *ein bedauerlicher Zwischenfall*
> is the press office's favourite phrase.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| Part of speech | adjective (attributive and predicative) |
| Formation | *bedauern* + suffix **-lich** |
| Comparison | *bedauerlicher*, *am bedauerlichsten* (rare) |
| Declension | regular: *ein bedauerlicher Fehler* |
| Adverb | ***bedauerlicherweise*** "regrettably, unfortunately" |
| Typical frame | *Es ist bedauerlich, **dass** …* |
| Intensifiers | *höchst*, *zutiefst*, *überaus*, *umso* |

⚠️ **The suffix *-lich* is passive here**: *bedauerlich* = "worthy of
regret", not "feeling regret" — exactly like English *regrettable* vs
*regretful*. Compare *erfreulich* "gratifying", *erstaunlich*
"astonishing", *ärgerlich* "annoying".

## Main meanings

### 1. Regrettable, to be deplored (of a fact or event)

The core use: a verdict on a **situation**.

> *Es ist sehr **bedauerlich**, dass niemand reagiert hat.*
> It is most **regrettable** that nobody responded.

> *Ein **bedauerlicher** Irrtum, den wir sofort korrigiert haben.*
> An **unfortunate** error, which we corrected at once.

> *Der Vorfall ist **zutiefst bedauerlich**.*
> The incident is **deeply regrettable**.

### 2. A pity, a shame (of a small setback)

In speech Germans reach for *schade*; in writing *bedauerlich* holds
its ground.

> *Eine **bedauerliche** Verzettelung von Kräften.*
> An **unfortunate** dissipation of effort.

> *Umso **bedauerlicher** ist es, dass er nicht kommen konnte.*
> It is all the more **of a shame** that he could not come.

### 3. The diplomatic shield 📰

The word that concedes the mishap without conceding the fault — the
German twin of "mistakes were made".

> *Ein **bedauerlicher** Einzelfall — kein Systemversagen.*
> A **regrettable** one-off — not a systemic failure.

> *Wir bedauern den Vorfall zutiefst.*
> We deeply regret the incident.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**be- + dauer + -lich** — and everything turns on that root.

German has **two unrelated verbs *dauern*** that fell together in
sound:

| Verb | Origin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *dauern*¹ | Latin ***durare*** "to last" | to last, to take time |
| *dauern*² | Germanic root of ***teuer*** | "to be a source of regret" |

*bedauern* and *bedauerlich* belong to the **second**. OHG/MHG *tûren*
meant "to be dear, precious"; *es dauert mich* was originally "it
costs me dear" → "I am sorry about it". The same root gives modern
***teuer*** "expensive, dear".

> **bedauerlich = "something that cost us dear" — hence the regret**

The old construction is still alive, if literary:

> *Du **dauerst** mich.* — I feel sorry for you.
> *Es **dauert** mich, dass …* — I am sorry that …

**The English key.** *Dear* works the same way: a price word that
doubles as sympathy — *Oh dear!*, *dear me!* Cost slides into
compassion in both languages. Meanwhile *dauern*¹ is plainly visible
in *durable*, *duration*, *during* — and has nothing to do with
regret.

## Related and cognate words

| Word | English |
|---|---|
| *bedauern* (+ acc.) | to regret sth.; to feel sorry for sb. |
| *das Bedauern* | regret (*mit Bedauern*, *zu meinem Bedauern*) |
| *bedauerlicherweise* | regrettably (formal) |
| *bedauernswert*, *bedauernswürdig* | pitiable — of **people** |
| *bedauernd* | regretful (*ein bedauernder Blick*) |
| *teuer* | dear, expensive — same root |
| *dauern*, *die Dauer*, *dauerhaft* | to last, duration — **other** root |

## How not to confuse them ⚠️

### *bedauerlich* ≠ *bedauernswert*

The family's main trap — and English has the very same pair:

| Word | Applies to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ***bedauerlich*** | **facts**: regrettable | *ein bedauerlicher Fehler* |
| ***bedauernswert*** | **people**: pitiable | *ein bedauernswerter Mann* |

❌ *ein bedauerlicher Mann* makes the man sound like an unfortunate
incident. Mirror it on *regrettable* vs *pitiable* and you will never
slip.

### Neighbours in meaning

| Word | Flavour |
|---|---|
| ***schade*** 💬 | "a pity" — spoken, light, no officialese |
| ***betrüblich*** | saddening, distressing |
| ***beklagenswert*** 🎭 | lamentable, deplorable; stronger |
| ***ärgerlich*** | annoying — irritation rather than regret |
| ***unglücklich*** | unhappy, ill-chosen (*eine unglückliche Formulierung*) |
| ***fatal*** | fateful, with grave consequences |
| ***leider*** | the adverb "unfortunately" — a caveat, not a verdict |

Fine point: *Es ist bedauerlich, dass…* passes judgement from
outside; *Leider…* merely reports bad news.

## Register and style

| Context | Fit |
|---|---|
| 📰 press, press releases | ✔✔ its natural habitat |
| 🏛 official statements, diplomacy | ✔✔ |
| 📜 business correspondence | ✔✔ *Wir finden es bedauerlich, dass…* |
| 🎓 academic prose | ✔ |
| 💬 everyday conversation | ✔ but stiff — say *schade* |
| 🎭 elevated style | ✔ (stronger: *beklagenswert*) |

## Typical contexts and collocations

- **Incidents:** *ein bedauerlicher Zwischenfall / Vorfall / Unfall*,
  *ein bedauerlicher Einzelfall*.
- **Mistakes:** *ein bedauerliches Missverständnis*, *ein
  bedauerlicher Irrtum*, *ein bedauerlicher Fehler*.
- **Intensified:** *höchst / zutiefst / überaus bedauerlich*,
  *umso bedauerlicher*.
- **Frames:** *Es ist bedauerlich, dass…*, *Ich finde es bedauerlich,
  wenn…*, *Bedauerlicherweise müssen wir Ihnen mitteilen, dass…* —
  the standard opening of a rejection letter.

## How to remember

Split it as **be-DAUER-lich** and remember that *Dauer* here is not
"duration" but the old sense "dear, costly" — the root of **teuer**.

> **Bedauerlich is what came at a price:**
> that is why it is to be regretted.

The English prop: *dear* is both a price tag and a sigh — *"Oh
dear!"*. Cost and sympathy live in one root in both languages.

And keep the pair in mind: a **fact** is *bedauerlich*, a **person**
is *bedauernswert*.
