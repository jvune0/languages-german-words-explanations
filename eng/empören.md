# (sich) empören — to outrage, to be indignant; to rise up

A weak verb with an inseparable prefix. The register is bookish and
journalistic: this is **moral, public outrage**, not everyday
annoyance. Inside sits the adverb *empor* "upwards" — to be outraged
is literally **to rise up against**, first with weapons, later with
words alone.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Type | **weak** (the only weak verb in the *emp-* family) |
| Principal parts | *empört · empörte · hat empört* |
| Participle II | *empört* — **no *ge-*** |
| *zu*-infinitive | *zu empören* (never *empzuören*) |
| Reflexive (main use) | *sich **über** etw.* (acc.) *empören* |
| Transitive | *etw. empört jdn.* — sth. outrages sb. |
| Archaic sense | *sich **gegen** jdn. empören* — to rise against |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Commoner than the verb | the participle *empört*, adj. *empörend* |

**Why weak?** The other three *emp-* verbs are strong because they sit
on strong roots (*fangen*, *finden*, *felhan*). Here the base is not a
verb at all but the adverb *empor* "upwards", and such denominal
formations always join the weak class.

## Main meanings

### 1. *sich über etw. empören* — to be outraged at something

The main modern pattern. The preposition is always *über* + accusative.

> *Die Öffentlichkeit empörte sich über das Urteil.*
> The public was outraged at the verdict.

> *Er empörte sich lautstark über die Preise.*
> He loudly protested about the prices.

> *Worüber empörst du dich eigentlich?*
> What exactly are you so outraged about?

### 2. *etw. empört jdn.* — something outrages someone

The transitive frame: the cause is the subject, the person the object.

> *Diese Ungerechtigkeit empört mich zutiefst.*
> This injustice outrages me deeply.

> *Sein Ton hat alle empört.*
> His tone outraged everyone.

### 3. *empört* — outraged, indignant (participle as adjective)

In practice this shows up more often than the verb itself.

> *Er reagierte empört auf den Vorwurf.*
> He reacted indignantly to the accusation.

> *"Das ist doch unmöglich!", rief sie empört.*
> "That's simply impossible!" she cried indignantly.

> *ein empörter Leserbrief* — an indignant letter to the editor

### 4. *empörend* — outrageous

> *Das ist ja empörend!*
> That's outrageous!

> *Die Zustände im Heim sind empörend.*
> Conditions in the home are outrageous.

### 5. To revolt, to rebel (📜 archaic, historical)

The original sense was armed insurrection; it survives in historical
prose and set phrases.

> *Die Bauern empörten sich gegen ihre Herren.*
> The peasants rose up against their lords.

> *Sich gegen Gott zu empören ist Hybris.*
> To rebel against God is hubris. (📚)

## Word breakdown and etymology

**emp-** (= *ent-*) + **por** (← *bor* "up")

- The base is the adverb **empor** "upwards, aloft": *empor**steigen***
  "to ascend", *empor**heben*** "to raise", *empor**kommen*** "to rise
  in the world".
- **empor** is itself a compound: *ent-* + Old High German *bor*
  "height, top". The same root gives *der Bord* "edge, board" and
  English **board**.
- **emp-** is a variant of the prefix **ent-** (Germanic ***and-***
  "towards, against"). Here it stands before **b**, not *f*, but the
  mechanism is identical: *n* assimilates to the labial (*n → m*) and
  a *p* is inserted between *m* and *b*: *ent-bor → em-bor → em-por*.
  See [emp-Verben](emp-Verben.md).

Literally: **"to rise up (against)"** → to revolt → to be indignant.

**English parallel.** Anger goes upwards in English too: *uprising*,
*to be up in arms*, *to rise up against*, *my hackles rise*. Even
*revolt* and *rebel* come from Latin motion verbs (*re-volvere* "to
roll back", *re-bellare* "to make war again"). German simply chose the
vertical axis where Latin chose the reverse.

⚠️ Don't connect the root to *die Pore* "pore" or *pur* "pure" — those
are accidental look-alikes.

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Register |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **sich empören** | to be outraged, morally and publicly | 📰 bookish |
| **sich aufregen** | to get worked up, agitated | 💬 everyday |
| **sich ärgern** (*über*) | to be annoyed (personally) | 💬 neutral |
| **sich entrüsten** | to be indignant | 📜 literary |
| **sich echauffieren** | to get hot under the collar (ironic) | 🎭 |
| **protestieren** | to protest (by acting) | neutral |
| **sich auflehnen** (*gegen*) | to rebel, to resist | 📚 |
| **rebellieren / revoltieren** | to rebel (political) | 📰 |

**The key splits:**

- **empören vs. ärgern.** *Ärgern* is "this annoys me" (a personal
  irritation); *empören* is "this is unacceptable" (a **moral**
  verdict). A late bus *ärgert* you; a court verdict *empört* you.
- **empören vs. aufregen.** *Sich aufregen* is about **nerves**
  (*Reg dich nicht auf!* — "Don't get worked up!"); *sich empören* is
  about **principles**. You say the first to a friend, the second in
  a newspaper.
- **empören vs. entrüsten.** Near-synonyms, but *entrüsten* is
  distinctly more literary and often carries a whiff of performed
  indignation. Charmingly, it literally means "to take off one's
  armour" (*die Rüstung*).

## Register and style

| Setting | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| 📰 Press, politics | ✅ its home ground (*Empörung*) |
| 🌐 Social media, debate | ✅ (*Empörungswelle*, *Shitstorm*) |
| 📚 Literature, essays | ✅ neutral-literary |
| ⚖️ Opinion writing | ✅ (*empörende Zustände*) |
| 💬 Conversation | ➖ speakers say *sich aufregen*, *sich ärgern* |
| 📜 Historical texts | ✅ in the sense "to revolt" |

📰 A current media coinage: *die Empörungskultur* — the German
counterpart of English *outrage culture*.

## Typical collocations

- *sich über etw. empören* — to be outraged at sth.
- *etw. empört mich zutiefst* — sth. outrages me deeply
- *empört reagieren / aufschreien / protestieren*
- *helle / große / allgemeine Empörung auslösen* — to provoke sheer
  outrage
- *für Empörung sorgen* — to cause outrage
- *eine Welle der Empörung* — a wave of outrage
- *empörende Zustände / Vorwürfe* — outrageous conditions / charges
- *voller Empörung* — full of indignation
- *sich gegen jdn. empören* (📜) — to rise up against sb.

## How to remember

The key is the adverb **empor** "upwards", sitting in plain sight
inside *emp-ör-en*. Outrage **lifts** a person — out of the chair
first, onto the barricades next. English keeps the same picture in
**uprising** and *up in arms*.

A church-shaped hook: **die Empore** is the upper gallery. Whoever
*empört sich* is figuratively climbing up there to shout down at the
congregation.

And the family's four preterites together: *empfing* — he **caught**;
*empfahl* — he **advised**; *empfand* — he **felt**; *empörte sich* —
he **rose up**. One prefix meaning "towards", four different gestures.
