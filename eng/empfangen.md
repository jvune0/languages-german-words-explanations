# empfangen — to receive, to welcome; to pick up (a signal); to conceive

A strong verb with an inseparable prefix. Neutral-to-elevated: where
plain "get" is *bekommen*, **empfangen** sounds more ceremonious and
always implies a **movement towards** — someone receiving, and someone
or something coming to meet them.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Type | strong; prefix **inseparable**, unstressed |
| Principal parts | *empfängt · empfing · hat empfangen* |
| Participle II | *empfangen* — **no *ge-*** |
| *zu*-infinitive | *zu empfangen* (never *empzufangen*) |
| Present umlaut | *du empfängst*, *er empfängt* (like *fangen*) |
| Government | *jdn./etw.* (acc.) *empfangen* |
| Common pattern | *jdn. mit etw.* (dat.) *empfangen* — to greet with |
| Auxiliary | *haben* |
| Passive | very frequent: *wurde herzlich empfangen* |

The conjugation simply mirrors *fangen*: *fing – gefangen* →
*empfing – empfangen*. The only difference is the missing *ge-*.

## Main meanings

### 1. To receive, to welcome (guests, visitors)

The core sense. It presupposes a **host on their own ground**: an
office, a house, a palace, a consulting room. Usually paired with a
manner adverbial — *herzlich*, *kühl*, *mit Applaus*.

> *Der Bundespräsident empfing die Delegation im Schloss Bellevue.*
> The Federal President received the delegation at Bellevue Palace.

> *Der Arzt empfängt heute keine Patienten.*
> The doctor is seeing no patients today.

> *Sie wurde mit Applaus empfangen.*
> She was greeted with applause.

> *Man empfing uns kühl.*
> We were given a cool reception.

### 2. To receive a signal, to get reception (technology)

Here the root *fangen* "to catch" is almost literal — as in English
*to catch a station*.

> *Hier empfange ich kein Signal.*
> I get no signal here.

> *Der Sender wird in ganz Europa empfangen.*
> The station is received all over Europe.

> *Haben Sie meine E-Mail empfangen?*
> Did you receive my email? (more formal than *bekommen*)

### 3. To receive ceremonially (a prize, a blessing, a sacrament)

Not "to get a parcel", but **to take what is formally handed over**.

> *Er empfing den Preis aus den Händen der Ministerin.*
> He received the prize from the minister's hands.

> *die Sakramente empfangen* — to receive the sacraments
> *den Segen empfangen* — to receive the blessing

> *Sie empfing die Nachricht mit Fassung.*
> She received the news with composure. (literary)

### 4. To conceive (a child) — biblical and medical

> *Sie empfing ein Kind.*
> She conceived a child. (📜 elevated)

> *die Unbefleckte Empfängnis* — the Immaculate Conception
> *das Empfängnisdatum* — the date of conception (med./legal)

English *conceive* works identically: Latin *con-* + *capere* "to
take" — again a taking-in. Hence modern *die Empfängnisverhütung*,
literally "conception prevention" = contraception.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**emp-** (= *ent-*) + **fangen**

- **fangen** — "to catch"; the same root as English **fang**, which
  once meant "to seize, capture" (still visible in the noun *fang*,
  the tooth that seizes, and in dialectal *to fang*).
- **emp-** is the phonetic variant of the prefix **ent-**, from
  Germanic ***and-*** "against, towards" — the element in
  **Ant**wort (= English **answer**, "a word given back") and in
  Greek *anti-*. Here it is **not** "un-, de-" but the old "towards".

Literally: **"to catch what comes towards you"**.

The shift *ent-f-* → *emp-f-*: dental *n* before labial *f*
assimilates to *m*, and an epenthetic *p* slips in between — the same
insertion English shows in *empty* (Old English *ǣmettig*) and
*Thompson*. See the card on [emp-Verben](emp-Verben.md).

**English parallel.** *Receive* is built on exactly the same plan,
only from Latin: *re-* "back, towards" + *capere* "to take" = "to take
towards oneself". German assembled the identical idea from native
bricks. So *empfangen* : *fangen* :: *receive* : *capture* — and the
loftier feel of *receive* over *get* matches *empfangen* over
*bekommen* almost exactly.

## Watch out: *der Empfang* means three things

| Sense | Example |
|-------|---------|
| 🥂 A reception (event) | *ein Empfang im Rathaus* — a civic reception |
| 🏨 Front desk, lobby | *Fragen Sie am Empfang.* — Ask at reception |
| 📶 Signal reception | *Ich habe keinen Empfang.* — I have no signal |

Handy pair: *Ich habe kein Netz* and *Ich habe keinen Empfang* both
mean "I've got no signal"; the second is slightly more formal.

## How not to confuse it

| Verb | Core idea | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **empfangen** | to receive formally / to pick up a signal | *Gäste empfangen* |
| **bekommen** | to get (neutral, everyday) | *ein Paket bekommen* |
| **erhalten** | to receive (formal, written) | *eine Absage erhalten* |
| **kriegen** | to get (💬 colloquial) | *Ärger kriegen* |
| **annehmen** | to accept, agree to take | *ein Geschenk annehmen* |
| **abholen** | to meet = to pick up in person | *jdn. abholen* |
| **begrüßen** | to greet (in words) | *die Gäste begrüßen* |
| **aufnehmen** | to take in, admit | *Flüchtlinge aufnehmen* |

**Traps:**

- ⚠️ *Ich habe ein Paket empfangen* sounds like officialese; everyday
  German wants *bekommen*.
- ⚠️ *empfangen* is about **ceremony or technology**; *abholen* is
  about **physically collecting**. You *holst* a friend *ab* at the
  airport, while an official *empfängt* an ambassador at the ministry.
- ⚠️ Don't mix it with **empfehlen** "to recommend": the shapes rhyme,
  but *empfing* ≠ *empfahl*.

## Register and style

| Setting | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| 📰 Press, diplomacy | ✅ the standard verb (*empfing zum Gespräch*) |
| 🏨 Hospitality | ✅ (*am Empfang*, *Empfangsdame*) |
| 📡 Technology, telecoms | ✅ terminological (*Signale empfangen*) |
| ⛪ Church | ✅ (*die Sakramente empfangen*) |
| 💼 Business correspondence | ✅ (*Empfangsbestätigung*) |
| 💬 Conversation | ➖ everyday speech prefers *bekommen*, *kriegen* |
| 📜 Elevated style | ✅ *sie empfing ein Kind* |

## Typical collocations

- *Gäste / Besucher / eine Delegation empfangen*
- *jdn. herzlich / kühl / mit offenen Armen empfangen*
- *jdn. mit Applaus / mit Buhrufen empfangen*
- *Sprechstunde: der Arzt empfängt von 9 bis 12 Uhr*
- *ein Signal / einen Sender / Daten empfangen*
- *keinen Empfang haben* — to be out of range
- *am Empfang fragen* — to ask at the front desk
- *einen Preis / den Segen / die Sakramente empfangen*
- *jdn. zu einem Gespräch empfangen* — to receive sb. for talks

## How to remember

Split it: **emp- (towards) + fangen (catch)** = **"catch what is
coming towards you"**. Everything follows: the host catches the guest
at the door, the aerial catches the signal out of the air, the laureate
catches the prize from the minister's hand.

The English bridge does the work for you: *empfangen* ↔ **receive** =
*re-* "towards" + *capere* "take". Native *fang* still lurks in the
German root, so *empfangen* is quite literally *to fang towards* — and
*fangen* : *empfangen* stands to *catch* : *receive*.

An ear-test pair: *empfing* — he **caught** (fangen); *empfahl* — he
**advised** (kin of *befehlen*). One letter, another story.
