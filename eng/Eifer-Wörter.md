# Eifer- (Eifer · eifrig · eifern · nacheifern · Eifersucht) — "burning zeal"

> One root, one family — and a startling spread of meaning: from
> praiseworthy **zeal** (*Eifer*, *eifrig*) through **competition**
> (*wetteifern*, *nacheifern*) all the way to corrosive **jealousy**
> (*Eifersucht*). It all hangs on the original sense of the root:
> "sharp, bitter, burning". Register runs from everyday (*eifrig*) to
> pulpit-formal (*eifern gegen etwas*).

## Grammar: the family at a glance

| Word | Part of speech | Key forms | Government |
|---|---|---|---|
| *der Eifer* | noun, m. | *-s*, **no plural** | *mit / voller Eifer* |
| *eifrig* | adj./adv. | *eifriger, am eifrigsten* | *bei* + D, *in* + D |
| *eifern* | weak verb | *eiferte, hat geeifert* | *nach* + D / *gegen* + A |
| *nacheifern* | separable | *eiferte nach, nachgeeifert* | **+ dative** |
| *wetteifern* | inseparable | *wetteiferte, gewetteifert* | *mit* + D, *um* + A |
| *die Eifersucht* | noun, f. | **no plural** | *auf* + **acc.** |
| *eifersüchtig* | adjective | *-er, am eifersüchtigsten* | *auf* + **acc.** |
| *der Eiferer* | noun, m. | *-s, -*; f. *die Eiferin* | *gegen* + A |

Three grammatical traps in this family:

1. **No linking *-e-*** in the adjective: it is *eifrig*, never
   ❌ *eiferig*. The fleeting *-e-* of the stem drops out, exactly as
   in *sicher → sichrer*, *teuer → teurer*.
2. **Prefix behaviour splits.** *nacheifern* is separable (*ich eifere
   ihm **nach***, participle *nach**ge**eifert*), while *wetteifern*
   is not — it was built on the noun *der Wetteifer*, not on a verb:
   *ich wetteifere*, participle *gewetteifert* (❌ *wettgeeifert*).
3. **Jealousy always takes *auf* + accusative**: *eifersüchtig **auf
   ihn***, *die Eifersucht **auf den Bruder*** — direction of feeling,
   not location.

## The key to the whole family: "sharp, bitter, burning"

*Der Eifer* is a fairly late noun (16th century), and it grows out of
an **adjective**: OHG *eibar / eivar* "bitter, sharp, acrid". The
adjective itself has died out, but its taste survives in the noun.
*Eifer* is not calm diligence — it is **heat**, an inner burning that
drives a person forward.

> **root = "it burns" → heat of the soul → zeal → jealousy**

The word owes its career to **Luther**, who used *Eifer* and *eifern*
to render Latin *zelus* / *zelare* in his Bible ("the zeal of the
Lord", "the zeal of thine house hath eaten me up"). That is why
*eifern gegen etwas* still sounds like a preacher thundering from the
pulpit.

**The English parallel is the perfect teaching aid.** Greek *zēlos*
gave English **two** words: *zeal* and *jealousy* — the very same
split. English spread it over two look-alike descendants; German
packed it into one family:

| English | German | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *zeal*, *zealous* | *Eifer*, *eifrig* | burning eagerness ➕ |
| *zealot* | *Eiferer* | fanatic ➖ |
| *jealousy*, *jealous* | *Eifersucht*, *eifersüchtig* | fear of a rival ➖ |
| *emulate* | *nacheifern* | measure yourself against a model |

So *Eifersucht* is, quite literally, German's home-grown *jealousy*:
same idea, built from native parts instead of borrowed ones.

### *-sucht* is not "search" — it is "sickness"

The second half of *Eifer·sucht* has **nothing** to do with *suchen*
"to seek". *Die Sucht* comes from OHG *suht* "illness", from the same
root as *siech* "ailing", *die Seuche* "epidemic" — and English
**sick**. Hence:

> **Eifersucht = "the sickness of zeal"; heat that has turned
> feverish.**

The same pattern runs through a whole set of German nouns:

| Word | Literally | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *die Gelbsucht* | "yellow-sickness" | jaundice |
| *die Schwindsucht* | "wasting-sickness" | consumption, TB |
| *die Tobsucht* | "raving-sickness" | frenzy, mania |
| *die Sehnsucht* | "longing-sickness" | yearning |
| *die Eifersucht* | "zeal-sickness" | jealousy |

Modern *Sucht* "addiction" (*Spielsucht*, *Magersucht*) continues that
same "pathological" sense; the popular link to *suchen* is folk
etymology, however neatly it fits today's usage.

## The members, one by one

### 1. *der Eifer* — zeal, ardour, eagerness

The inner heat you bring to a task. Neutral-to-bookish; nearly always
in the phrases *mit Eifer*, *voller Eifer*, *im Eifer*.

> *Sie machte sich **mit großem Eifer** an die Arbeit.*
> She set about the work **with great zeal**.

> *Sein **Eifer** ließ nach, als die ersten Schwierigkeiten kamen.*
> His **eagerness** waned once the first difficulties appeared.

> ***Im Eifer des Gefechts** habe ich ganz vergessen, dich anzurufen.*
> **In the heat of the moment** I completely forgot to call you.

> ***Blinder Eifer** schadet nur.* (proverb)
> **Blind zeal** does nothing but harm. ≈ "The road to hell is paved
> with good intentions."

### 2. *eifrig* — eager, keen, zealous

The most frequent and most neutral member; works as adjective and as
adverb. The flavour is "with visible enthusiasm", sometimes with a
smile at the excess.

> *Er nickte **eifrig** und schrieb alles mit.*
> He nodded **eagerly** and took down every word.

> *Sie ist eine **eifrige** Leserin von Krimis.*
> She is an **avid** reader of crime novels.

> *Die Kinder waren **eifrig bei** der Sache.*
> The children were **keenly** into it.

Intensified forms: *übereifrig* "overzealous" (disapproving),
*feuereifrig* "burning with eagerness", *diensteifrig* "officiously
keen" — nearly always ironic, the *jobsworth* register.

### 3. *eifern* — 1) to strive after, 2) to rail against

The bare verb barely exists without a preposition, and it sounds
**elevated**, literary or biblical:

- ***nach etwas (D) eifern*** — to strive passionately after;
- ***gegen jemanden/etwas (A) eifern*** — to rail, inveigh, thunder.

> *Er **eiferte nach** Ruhm und Anerkennung.*
> He **strove after** fame and recognition.

> *Der Prediger **eiferte gegen** die Sitten der Zeit.*
> The preacher **railed against** the morals of the age.

### 4. *nacheifern* (+ dative) — to emulate, follow an example

To strive to match a model. Always **dative** — you run *after* the
person.

> *Sie **eifert** ihrer älteren Schwester **nach**.*
> She **models herself on** her elder sister.

> *Einem solchen Vorbild kann man nur **nacheifern**.*
> Such a model can only be **emulated**.

⚠️ Not *nachahmen* (+ acc.) "to imitate, mimic": *nacheifern* is about
matching someone's achievement, *nachahmen* about copying their
outward manner.

### 5. *wetteifern* — to vie, compete

*Die Wette* "bet" + *eifern*: zeal turned into a race. The overtone is
sporting and rather noble — competing, not scheming.

> *Zwei Firmen **wetteiferten um** den Auftrag.*
> Two companies **vied for** the contract.

> *Sie **wetteiferten miteinander**, wer schneller fertig wird.*
> They **competed with each other** over who would finish first.

### 6. *die Eifersucht* / *eifersüchtig* — jealousy / jealous

Fear of losing what is yours — love, attention, standing — to a rival.
Always a **triangle**: me, my person, the third party.

> *Seine **Eifersucht** war krankhaft.*
> His **jealousy** was pathological.

> *Sie ist **eifersüchtig auf** seine neue Kollegin.*
> She is **jealous of** his new colleague.

> *Der Ältere war **eifersüchtig auf** das Baby.*
> The older child was **jealous of** the baby. (not only romantic!)

> *Er hat sie **aus Eifersucht** getötet.* (press style)
> He killed her **out of jealousy**.

### 7. *der Eiferer*, *der Übereifer* — zealot, overzealousness

> *Ein religiöser **Eiferer** störte die Veranstaltung.*
> A religious **zealot** disrupted the event.

> *Sein **Übereifer** hat mehr kaputtgemacht als geholfen.*
> His **overzealousness** broke more than it fixed.

## How not to confuse them

### Jealousy vs envy

| Word | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *die **Eifersucht*** | "someone may **take mine**" | jealousy of a rival |
| *der **Neid*** | "they have it, I **don't**" | envy of a nicer car |
| *die **Missgunst*** | "I want them **not** to have it" | begrudging spite |
| *die **Scheelsucht*** 📜 | bookish synonym of *Neid* | "looking askance" |

German keeps the two apart more strictly than colloquial English does
— *jealous of your success* would be *neidisch*, not *eifersüchtig*.

### The eager neighbours

| Word | Flavour |
|---|---|
| ***eifrig*** | eager, fired up; may tip into too much |
| ***fleißig*** | industrious, hard-working — regularity, not heat |
| ***emsig*** | busy as a bee; small, bustling activity |
| ***strebsam*** | ambitious, career-driven, upwardly striving |
| ***beflissen*** 🎭 | assiduous, eager to please — usually ironic |
| ***engagiert*** 📰 | committed, involved; modern and neutral |

### Dangerous look-alikes ⚠️

| Looks like | But is | Difference |
|---|---|---|
| *eifrig* | ***eilig*** | "hurried, urgent" — unrelated root |
| *der Eifer* | ***der Eiter*** | **pus**! *eitrig* "purulent" — one letter |
| *eifern* | ***geifern*** | "to slaver, spew bile"; *Geifer* = drool |
| *Eifersucht* | ***Sehnsucht*** | yearning, not jealousy |

| *der Eifer* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ (less) |

| *nacheifern* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | rare |
| *wetteifern* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ❌ |
| *Eifersucht* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔✔ | ✔✔ |
| *eifersüchtig* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ✔✔ |
| *der Eiferer* | ✔ | ✔ | ✔ | ❌ |

In everyday conversation you will meet *eifrig* and *eifersüchtig*;
*eifern* and *wetteifern* belong to newspapers, lecterns and sermons —
much like English *zeal* against *keen*.

## Typical contexts and collocations

- **Work and school:** *mit Eifer bei der Sache sein*, *voller Eifer*,
  *ein eifriger Schüler*, *der Eifer erlahmt / lässt nach*.
- **Set phrases:** *im Eifer des Gefechts* "in the heat of the
  moment", *blinder Eifer schadet nur* (proverb), *jemanden in Eifer
  bringen* "to get someone worked up", *in Eifer geraten*.
- **Relationships:** *krankhafte / blinde / rasende Eifersucht*,
  *von Eifersucht zerfressen* "eaten up with jealousy",
  *aus Eifersucht handeln*, *jemanden eifersüchtig machen*.
- **Press:** *Tat aus Eifersucht*, *Eifersuchtsdrama*,
  *religiöse Eiferer*, *übereifrige Beamte*.
- **Bible and rhetoric:** *gegen die Missstände eifern*,
  *nach Gerechtigkeit eifern*, *ein Eiferer für den Glauben*.

## How to remember

Hold on to one picture — **a fire under a cauldron**:

> ***Eifer*** is heat. Work **eifrig** and your cauldron boils; when
> you **einem Vorbild nacheifern** or **mit anderen wetteifern**, you
> aim the heat at a goal; when you **gegen** something **eiferst**,
> you scorch other people; and when the heat is aimed at a **person**
> you are afraid of losing, the cauldron boils over into
> **Eifer·sucht** — "zeal-sickness", jealousy.

Two props to hang it on:

- **The English one:** *zeal* and *jealousy* are the same Greek word
  *zēlos*. German did exactly the same thing with its own material:
  *Eifer* → *Eifersucht*. Remember *Eiferer* = *zealot* and the whole
  family lines up.
- **The spelling one:** ***Eifer* burns, *Eiter* festers** — one
  letter apart, and your ardour turns into an abscess.
