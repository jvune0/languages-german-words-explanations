# umziehen — to move house; to change clothes (and, stressed differently, to encircle)

One spelling, **two different verbs**. Separable *úmziehen* (stress
on the prefix) is the everyday word for *moving house* and *getting
changed*. Inseparable *umzíehen* (stress on the stem) is a literary
word meaning *to ring, encircle, cloud over*. Only the stress — and
the position of *ge-* in the participle — tells them apart.

## Grammar

| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Stem | *ziehen* — strong: *zieht · zog · gezogen* |
| **1. úmziehen** | prefix **separable**, stressed |
| Principal parts | *zieht um · zog um · **ist** umgezogen* |
| Auxiliary | *sein* — it is a change of place |
| **1b. sich úmziehen** | *hat sich umgezogen* — to get changed |
| Auxiliary | *haben* (reflexives always take *haben*) |
| Government | *nach* + Dat (town), *in* + Acc (a flat) |
| Also | *zu jdm.* — to move in with somebody |
| Infinitive with *zu* | *um**zu**ziehen* (*zu* goes inside) |
| **2. umzíehen** | prefix **inseparable**, unstressed |
| Principal parts | *umzieht · umzog · hat **umzogen*** (no *ge-*) |
| Government | *etw.* (Acc) *mit etw.* (Dat) *umziehen* |
| Reflexive | *der Himmel umzieht sich* — the sky clouds over |

⚠️ **The participle gives it away:**
*umgezogen* = moved / changed clothes; *umzogen* = ringed, encircled.

## Main meanings

### 1. To move house (also offices, servers)

The core meaning. Always with *sein* — a change of location.

> *Wir ziehen nächsten Monat nach Hamburg um.*
> We're moving to Hamburg next month.

> *Sie ist in eine größere Wohnung umgezogen.*
> She has moved into a bigger flat.

> *Meine Schwester zieht zu ihrem Freund um.*
> My sister is moving in with her boyfriend.

> *Die Firma zieht ins neue Bürogebäude um.*
> The company is moving into the new office building.

> *Der Server ist auf eine andere Domain umgezogen.*
> The server has moved to a different domain. (💻 quite normal)

⚠️ It is the **person** who moves, never the furniture: there is no
transitive *ich ziehe meine Möbel um*. Things get *transportiert*.

### 2. *sich umziehen* — to get changed

Literally "to pull something different around oneself". Reflexive,
with *haben*.

> *Ich muss mich noch schnell umziehen.*
> I still need to get changed quickly.

> *Zieh dich um, wir gehen ins Theater!*
> Get changed, we're going to the theatre!

> *Nach dem Sport haben wir uns umgezogen.*
> After sport we got changed.

Transitive, when you change someone else:

> *Sie zieht das Kind um.*
> She's changing the child('s clothes).

### 3. *umzíehen* (inseparable) — to ring, encircle, cloud over

📜 Literary; met mostly in description and in participles.

> *Dunkle Wolken umzogen den Gipfel.*
> Dark clouds ringed the summit.

> *Der Himmel hat sich mit Wolken umzogen.*
> The sky had clouded over.

> *Ein von Mauern umzogener Garten.*
> A garden ringed by walls.

> *Ihre Augen waren dunkel umzogen.*
> Her eyes had dark rings around them.

## Word breakdown and etymology

**um-** + **ziehen**

- **ziehen** — "to pull, draw, drag"; Gmc *\*teuhaną*, cognate with
  Latin *dūcere* "to lead" (hence *duke*, *conduct*, *produce*).
  The direct English cognate is **to tow** (OE *tēon* "to pull"),
  and **tug** belongs to the same family.
- **um-** — "around" → and from that, two paths:
  - **separable** and stressed = "differently, into another
    position": *umsteigen* (change trains), *umbauen* (rebuild),
    *umbenennen* (rename), *umziehen* (move house, change clothes);
  - **inseparable** and unstressed = literally "around":
    *umarmen* (embrace), *umgeben* (surround), *umzingeln*
    (encircle), *umzíehen* (ring, encircle).

**The English angle.** *Ziehen* ↔ *tow*: both mean "draw along
behind you". A German moving house **tows** their belongings to a new
address (*ein Umzug*), and when changing clothes **pulls** a
different shirt around themselves. English splits the job between
two unrelated verbs, *move* and *change*; German does both with one.

Note also the *ziehen* ↔ *dūcere* pairing at work in **erziehen**
"to bring up, educate" — literally "to draw out", exactly like Latin
*ēdūcere* behind English *educate*. Same metaphor, built twice.

⚠️ **der Umzug** is a trap: it means both **a move (relocation)**
and **a procession, parade** — *Karnevalsumzug*, *Faschingsumzug*,
*Fackelumzug* (torchlight procession). Same logic either way: a line
of people trailing through town.

## Housing verbs vs. clothing verbs

**Moving:**

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|------|-------|---------|
| **umziehen** | to move (general) | *nach Köln umziehen* |
| **einziehen** | to move in | *Wir ziehen am 1. ein.* |
| **ausziehen** | to move out | *Er zieht aus.* |
| **beziehen** | to take up, occupy (trans.) | *die Wohnung beziehen* |
| **umsiedeln** | to resettle (groups) | ⚖️ official |
| **übersiedeln** | to relocate (🇦🇹 and formal) | 📰 |
| **verlegen** | to relocate (an institution) | *den Sitz verlegen* |

**Clothes:**

| Verb | Sense |
|------|-------|
| **sich umziehen** | to get changed (swap one outfit for another) |
| **sich anziehen** | to get dressed |
| **sich ausziehen** | to get undressed |
| **sich umkleiden** | 📜 to change (formal; cf. *die Umkleide*) |

**Key pitfalls:**

- ⚠️ *ausziehen* means both "move out" and "take off (clothes)".
  *Er zieht aus* — he's moving out; *Er zieht sich aus* — he's
  undressing. The **sich** makes the difference.
- ⚠️ "To move house" is intransitive in German. English *"we moved
  the office"* becomes *das Büro ist umgezogen* or *wir haben den
  Sitz verlegt* — never *wir haben das Büro umgezogen*.
- ⚠️ English *move* covers far more ground: *bewegen* (physically
  move), *verschieben* (shift, postpone), *rühren* (move
  emotionally). *Umziehen* only ever means changing address.
- ⚠️ Compare *umfahren*, where the same stress split flips the sense
  entirely: *úmfahren* = run over, *umfáhren* = drive around.
  *Umziehen* has no such reversal, but the mechanism is identical.

## Typical collocations

- *nach Berlin / in eine neue Wohnung / ins Ausland umziehen*
- *zu den Eltern / zu jdm. umziehen* — move in with someone
- *beruflich umziehen* — move for work
- *den Umzug planen / organisieren* — plan the move
- *beim Umzug helfen* — help with the move
- *sich schnell / fürs Abendessen umziehen* — get changed
- *der Himmel zieht sich zu* (also *umzieht sich*) — it clouds over
- *ein von Bäumen umzogener Platz* — a square ringed by trees

## How to remember

**um** = "around / re-", **ziehen** = English **tow**. So *umziehen*
is a **re-tow**: you tow yourself and your boxes to a new address,
or tow a different shirt around your shoulders. To German ears both
are the same gesture — pull, and you're somewhere new.

Stress decides everything: **Ú**mziehen, you pull *yourself* (house,
clothes); umz**í**ehen, you pull a line *around* something (clouds
around a peak). The participle says it out loud: *umgezogen* — I
moved; *umzogen* — it is ringed.

And keep *der Umzug* in mind: first it is the straggling line of
friends carrying boxes down the street, then the same straggling line
in carnival costume. One word, two processions.
