# -forderung (Forderung · Anforderung · Herausforderung …) — the demand family

> One nest of words, but **two different verbs** at its root: *fordern* “to
> demand” and *fördern* “to promote, to further”. Only the umlaut tells them
> apart, and both descend from old *vorder* “forward” — the same root as
> English *further*. Hence the logic of the whole family: **fordern = call
> something forward (demand), fördern = move something forward (support)**.

## Grammar shared by the family

| Feature | Value |
|---|---|
| All the nouns | **die**, plural *-en* (suffix *-ung*) 📎 [Suffixe](Suffixe.md) |
| Both verbs | weak: *forderte – gefordert*, *förderte – gefördert* |
| Stress | always on the root: *FORderung*, *AUFforderung* |
| Prefixes | *an-, auf-, heraus-, über-, unter-, ein-, zurück-, nach-, er-, be-* |
| Pronunciation | *fordern* [ˈfɔrdɐn] vs *fördern* [ˈfœrdɐn] — one vowel apart |

⚠️ The family’s central trap: *die Forderung* (a demand) and *die Förderung*
(support, funding) differ by **two dots** and mean nearly opposite things.

## The master key: *vorder* “forward”

- ***fordern*** ← OHG *fordarōn* “to summon forward, to call for” — from
  *fordar* “front, further”. To demand is to **call a thing forward** so
  that it is produced.
- ***fördern*** ← OHG *furdiren* “to advance, to move on”, the same root
  with an umlaut. English preserves it exactly: **to further** (*to further
  a cause*). *Fördern* is *to further*, word for word.
- German relatives: *vorder-* (front), *fort* (away, onward), *der
  Vordergrund*, *fortsetzen*. English: *forth*, *forward*, *further*.

> **Both halves of the family are about forward motion. The difference is
> what moves: *fordern* brings the demand forward, *fördern* brings the
> person or the project forward.**

## Branch 1: *fordern* — to demand

### 1. *die Forderung* — a demand; a financial claim

Government: *eine Forderung **an** jdn.* (acc.) *stellen*; *die Forderung
**nach** etw.* (dat.). Somebody always demands it of somebody — this is a
pointed, often adversarial word.

> *Die Gewerkschaft stellt hohe Forderungen an die Arbeitgeber.*
> The union is making heavy demands on the employers.

> *Die Forderung nach mehr Transparenz wird lauter.*
> The call for more transparency is growing louder.

> *Das Unternehmen hat offene Forderungen von 2 Millionen Euro.*
> The company has outstanding receivables of two million euros.

💼 The legal-financial sense is fully established and very frequent:
*eine Forderung* is a monetary claim, a debt owed to a creditor.

### 2. *die Anforderung* — a requirement, a standard

Government: *Anforderungen **an** etw./jdn.* (acc.) *stellen*. Unlike
*Forderung*, no one is doing the demanding: these are **impersonal
requirements** placed on a job, a material, a candidate.

> *Der Job stellt hohe Anforderungen an die Konzentration.*
> The job places high demands on one’s concentration.

> *Das Material erfüllt alle technischen Anforderungen.*
> The material meets all technical requirements.

> *Bitte beachten Sie das Anforderungsprofil der Stelle.*
> Please note the requirements profile for the position.

A second sense is **a request for something to be sent**: *die Anforderung
von Unterlagen*, *auf Anforderung* (on request).

### 3. *die Aufforderung* — a call to act, a summons

Government: *die Aufforderung **zu** etw.* (dat.), ***an** jdn.* (acc.).
Not a claim but a **prompt**: do it, come, pay.

> *Er kam der Aufforderung des Gerichts nicht nach.*
> He did not comply with the court’s summons.

> *Eine Aufforderung zur Zahlung ist noch keine Mahnung.*
> A request for payment is not yet a formal reminder.

> *Auf Aufforderung des Polizisten hielt sie an.*
> She stopped at the policeman’s instruction.

📚 Grammatical term: *der **Aufforderungssatz*** — an imperative sentence
(*Komm her!*). A useful anchor: *Aufforderung* is the imperative in noun
form.

### 4. *die Herausforderung* — a challenge

Literally “calling someone out”: *jdn. heraus-fordern* meant summoning a man
out of his house — **to a duel**. The duelling origin explains the modern
sense exactly, and English *challenge* has travelled the same road.

> *Der neue Job ist eine große Herausforderung für sie.*
> The new job is a big challenge for her.

> *Er forderte den Champion zum Kampf heraus.*
> He challenged the champion to a fight.

> *Wir sehen darin eher eine Herausforderung als ein Problem.*
> We see it as a challenge rather than a problem.

💬 In corporate German *Herausforderung* has become the polite euphemism for
*Problem* — precisely as *challenge* has in English.

### 5. *die Überforderung* / *die Unterforderung* — over- and under-demand

A very German pair, and one English has no single word for.

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *die Überforderung* | demands beyond one’s capacity | *Das war eine Überforderung.* |
| *sich überfordert fühlen* | to feel out of one’s depth | *Ich bin überfordert.* |
| *die Unterforderung* | abilities left unused | *Unterforderung macht krank.* |
| *unterfordert sein* | to be bored by easy work | *Er ist im Job unterfordert.* |

> *Kinder leiden sowohl unter Überforderung als auch unter Unterforderung.*
> Children suffer both from being pushed too hard and from being pushed too
> little.

### 6. *erfordern* and *erforderlich* — to require (said of a task)

Here it is not a person demanding but **the situation itself**. The
adjective *erforderlich* (“necessary, required”) is a workhorse of official
German.

> *Diese Aufgabe erfordert viel Geduld.* — This task requires much patience.
> *Eine Anmeldung ist nicht erforderlich.* — No registration is required.
> *das Erfordernis* — a requirement, a necessary condition (⚖️ officialese).

### 7. Other members of this branch

| Word | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *einfordern* | to insist on what is due | *Rechte einfordern* |
| *zurückfordern* | to demand back | *das Geld zurückfordern* |
| *die Nachforderung* | additional claim, back charge | *Nachforderung des Amts* |
| *abfordern* | to exact from someone | *jdm. alles abfordern* |
| *gefordert sein* | to be up against it | *Jetzt sind wir alle gefordert.* |

## Branch 2: *fördern* — to further

### 8. *die Förderung* — support, funding; extraction

Government: *die Förderung **von** etw.* (dat.). Two large senses:

> *Der Staat unterstützt die Förderung erneuerbarer Energien.*
> The state supports the promotion of renewable energy.

> *Sie erhält eine Förderung für ihr Forschungsprojekt.*
> She has been awarded funding for her research project.

> *Die Förderung von Erdöl ist zurückgegangen.*
> Oil extraction has declined.

The second sense is “bringing up and out” — literally furthering something
up out of a mine. Hence *das Förderband* (conveyor belt) and *etw. zutage
fördern* (to bring to light, to unearth).

### 9. *die Beförderung* — promotion; transport

Both senses are “moving forward”, one literal and one in a career.

> *Er hat seine Beförderung zum Abteilungsleiter gefeiert.*
> He celebrated his promotion to head of department.

> *Die Beförderung von Gefahrgut ist streng geregelt.*
> The transport of hazardous goods is strictly regulated.

## How not to confuse them

### The *Forderung / Anforderung / Aufforderung* triangle

| Word | In one line | Who it is aimed at |
|---|---|---|
| *Forderung* | “I demand this of you” | a specific person or party |
| *Anforderung* | “this is what it takes” | a job, a task, a material |
| *Aufforderung* | “do it now” | whoever is told to act |

> *Die Forderung der Streikenden* — the strikers’ demand (a conflict).
> *Die Anforderungen der Stelle* — the requirements of the post (a standard).
> *Die Aufforderung zum Verlassen des Gebäudes* — the call to leave the
> building.

### *fordern* / *fördern* / *erfordern*

| Verb | Who or what is the subject | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *fordern* | a person demanding of another | *Sie fordert eine Erklärung.* |
| *erfordern* | the task or the circumstances | *Das erfordert Mut.* |
| *fördern* | whoever helps something along | *Er fördert junge Talente.* |
| *verlangen* | to demand (broader, plainer) | *Er verlangt eine Antwort.* |

📎 Neighbouring card: [verlangen](verlangen.md).

## Typical contexts

- **Unions and politics:** *Forderungen stellen*, *auf seinen Forderungen
  beharren*.
- **Job ads:** *Anforderungsprofil*, *hohe Anforderungen an Teamfähigkeit*.
- **Legal letters:** *Aufforderung zur Zahlung*, *letzte Aufforderung*.
- **Business-speak:** *eine spannende Herausforderung* — in recruitment
  copy, almost invariably.
- **Psychology and schooling:** *Überforderung im Alltag*, *Unterforderung
  begabter Kinder*.
- **Grants and industry:** *EU-Förderung*, *Förderung von Rohstoffen*.

## How to remember

German has a ready-made slogan holding both branches together — the motto of
its welfare reform: **“Fördern und Fordern”**, “support and demand”. The
state helps you forward (*fördern*) and requires effort of you (*fordern*).

The anchor:

- **fordern** — “call it forward”: I demand that you **produce** what is
  owed. Hence *Forderung* (a demand and a debt), *Anforderung* (a standard),
  *Aufforderung* (an order to step up), *Herausforderung* (a calling-out,
  originally to a duel).
- **fördern** — “move it forward”, exactly English **to further**: I help
  you, the project, or the oil to **come up**. Hence *Förderung* (support
  and extraction) and *Beförderung* (promotion and transport).

One sentence with the whole nest: *Der Chef **fordert** viel, **fördert**
aber auch — die **Herausforderung** ist groß, die **Überforderung** nah, die
**Beförderung** sicher.* — “The boss demands a lot but backs you too: the
challenge is big, burnout is close, promotion is certain.”
