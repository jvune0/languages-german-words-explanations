# erfordern vs. anfordern — “to require” vs. “to request”

> Both belong to the *fordern* nest 📎
> [-forderung](Forderung-Wörter.md), but they must not be mixed: with
> *erfordern* the subject is a **task or situation** and there is no
> addressee (“this requires time”); with *anfordern* the subject is a
> **person or authority** and an addressee is essential (“request the file
> from the archive”). The prefixes make the difference: *er-* supplies a
> result, *an-* supplies an addressee.

## Grammar

| Feature | *erfordern* | *anfordern* |
|---|---|---|
| Prefix | *er-*, **inseparable** | *an-*, **separable**, stressed |
| Past participle | *erfordert* (no *ge-*) | *an-ge-fordert* |
| Subject | a task, a situation, a case | a person, a firm, an authority |
| Object | abstract: time, effort, qualities | concrete: documents, parts |
| Addressee | ❌ impossible | ✅ *bei jdm. / von jdm.* |
| What happens | a **need** arises | something must be **sent over** |
| Register | 📰 neutral written | 📰⚖️ business, logistics |

> *Das erfordert viel Geduld.* — That requires a great deal of patience.
> *Wir haben die Unterlagen bei der Zentrale angefordert.* — We requested
> the documents from head office.

## The key: *er-* is result, *an-* is addressee

The shared root *fordern* means “to call forward, to demand production of”.
The prefixes then diverge:

- ***er-*** carries an action through to its result (*erreichen*,
  *erarbeiten*, *ermöglichen*). *Er-fordern* = “to call forth as a
  necessary condition”, that is **to make necessary**. English pair: *to
  require*, *to call for*.
- ***an-*** marks turning to someone: *anrufen*, *ansprechen*,
  *anschreiben*, *anfragen*, *anmahnen*. *An-fordern* = “to turn to someone
  with a demand that they send it”. English pair: *to request*, *to
  requisition*.

> **erfordern describes a property of the matter. anfordern describes an
> act by a person. That is why the first can have no addressee and the
> second no abstract object.**

## 1. *erfordern* — to require, to call for

The subject is never a person: a task, a job, a situation, a law.

> *Diese Arbeit erfordert höchste Sorgfalt.*
> This work demands the utmost care.

> *Die Lage erfordert schnelles Handeln.*
> The situation calls for swift action.

> *Der Umbau erfordert mehrere Monate.*
> The conversion will take several months.

⚠️ ❌ *Ich erfordere eine Erklärung.* is impossible: *erfordern* takes no
personal subject. Say *Ich fordere / verlange eine Erklärung*.

**Derivatives.** The adjective *erforderlich* “necessary, required” is far
more frequent than the verb itself:

> *Eine Anmeldung ist nicht erforderlich.* — No registration is required.
> *die erforderlichen Unterlagen* — the required documents.
> *das Erfordernis* — ⚖️ a requirement, a necessary condition.

## 2. *anfordern* — to request, to requisition

The subject is a person or organisation; the object is something that must
be **sent or supplied**.

> *Bitte fordern Sie die Akte beim Archiv an.*
> Please request the file from the archive.

> *Die Feuerwehr forderte Verstärkung an.*
> The fire brigade called for reinforcements.

> *Das Formular kann online angefordert werden.*
> The form can be requested online.

The passive is very common here: *Die Ersatzteile wurden bereits
angefordert.*

**Derivative.** *die Anforderung* works in two senses:

| Sense | Example |
|---|---|
| a request, a requisition | *auf Anforderung* — on request |
| a requirement, a standard | *hohe Anforderungen an das Material* |

The second sense barely refers back to the verb any more — it is a word in
its own right, at home in job ads and technical standards.

## How not to confuse them

| If you mean | Use |
|---|---|
| “this takes time, courage, precision” | *erfordern* |
| “please send me this” | *anfordern* |

> ❌ *Die Aufgabe fordert viel Zeit an.*
> ✅ *Die Aufgabe **erfordert** viel Zeit.* — The task requires a lot of
> time.

> ❌ *Ich erfordere die Unterlagen von der Bank.*
> ✅ *Ich **fordere** die Unterlagen bei der Bank **an**.* — I am requesting
> the documents from the bank.

One question settles it: **can you name who is being asked?** If yes,
*anfordern*; if the demand has no addressee and comes from the matter
itself, *erfordern*.

## Neighbours

### On the “the matter requires it” side

| Verb | Nuance |
|---|---|
| *erfordern* | the matter makes it necessary 📰 |
| *verlangen* | broader: a person or a matter may demand |
| *voraussetzen* | to presuppose as a precondition |
| *benötigen* | to need (📰 more formal than *brauchen*) |
| *brauchen* | 💬 to need, everyday |
| *notwendig machen* | to make necessary (descriptive) |

### On the “I am requesting” side

| Verb | What is actually done |
|---|---|
| *anfordern* | demand that something be sent (a file, a part, backup) |
| *anfragen* | ask whether something is possible (an enquiry) |
| *beantragen* | file a formal application (leave, asylum) |
| *bestellen* | order goods or a service |
| *abrufen* | call off something already reserved |
| *einfordern* | insist on what is due by right |

> *Ich frage an, ob das möglich ist.* — I am enquiring whether that is
> possible.
> *Ich fordere die Unterlagen an.* — I am requesting the documents.
> *Ich beantrage Urlaub.* — I am applying for leave.

## Register of *erforderlich* and its neighbours

| Word | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| *nötig* | 💬 everyday “needed” |
| *notwendig* | 📰 neutral “necessary” |
| *erforderlich* | 📰⚖️ forms, instructions, law |
| *unerlässlich* | 📜 “indispensable”, a bookish intensifier |
| *vonnöten* | 📜 dated and solemn |

## Typical contexts

- **Instructions and forms:** *Eine Unterschrift ist erforderlich*, *die
  erforderlichen Angaben*.
- **Analysis:** *Das erfordert eine gründliche Prüfung.*
- **Administration and logistics:** *Unterlagen anfordern*, *auf
  Anforderung*, *Ersatzteile anfordern*.
- **Emergency services:** *Verstärkung anfordern*, *einen Hubschrauber
  anfordern*.
- **Job ads:** *hohe Anforderungen an Teamfähigkeit* (here the noun means a
  standard, not a request).

## How to remember

Two short images:

- ***er**fordern* — the prefix of **result**: the task itself has called
  forth your time and effort. Nobody has to be asked; the necessity simply
  arises. English *require*: *the job requires patience*.
- ***an**fordern* — the prefix of **addressing someone** (as in *anrufen*,
  *ansprechen*): you turn to a specific party and demand delivery. English
  *request*: *to request the file from the archive*.

One sentence with both: *Der Fall **erfordert** Sorgfalt — deshalb habe ich
alle Unterlagen beim Amt **angefordert**.* — “The case requires care, which
is why I requested all the documents from the authority.”
